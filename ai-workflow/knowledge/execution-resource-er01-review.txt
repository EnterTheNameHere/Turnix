1. Admission revocation must not revoke cleanup authority
--------------------------------------------------------
The proposed live-owner/generation checks apply to new admission/use. Exact issued
claims/tickets must remain able to finish/report/dispose their own outstanding work
after participant/reservation/domain admission closes. Otherwise owner close would
make legitimate late completion impossible and strand every blocker.
Keep revoked-for-use distinct from terminally-disposed; no new execution after
revocation, but cleanup can finish. Late/foreign generation evidence must never
clear a newer blocker. Preserve typed idempotent duplicate completion behavior.
Test owner/reservation/domain close followed by confirmed and unknown late finish.

2. Provider evidence is trusted adapter input, never Pack assertion
-----------------------------------------------------------------
OperationClaim.finish(effect) and retained-context cleanup evidence are internal
adapter/composition hooks. Future public owner/session close must not let a Pack
assert quiescence=confirmed to bypass protection. Issued handles and evidence
reporters must have documented roles, with no public caller-supplied authorization
boolean. In-process host/provider trust is not a hostile Python sandbox.
Keep error/outcome codes bounded and exclude opaque payloads/private labels from
repr/diagnostics. Complete immutable descriptor identity is the adapter's contract;
do not compare/hash arbitrary payloads or call payload equality under locks.
Repeated registration is either idempotent for the same issued descriptor or a
clear conflict; the core cannot certify semantic equality of opaque payloads.

3. Deferred retained-context disposal needs a concrete owner
-----------------------------------------------------------
Specify and implement a trusted disposer callback/ticket registered when the pin
is retained (or equivalent explicit adapter-owned lifecycle). pin.close requests
revocation/cleanup; it must not fabricate future cleanup evidence while in use.
After the last operation confirms quiescence, invoke the registered disposer exactly
once outside all core/host/registry locks, then publish its typed result for that
exact context ticket. Callback exceptions/unknown effects keep the proper blocker.
No slot/reset/cache mechanism belongs in ER-01. This is generic disposal of an
owned opaque resource, not snapshot persistence or provider-state introspection.
Test pin close during use followed by late operation finish, disposer reentry/
exception, double close and stale disposer results. No callback may be orphaned by
final participant close. A retained pin from a closing reservation must continue
blocking incompatible/unrelated admission until actually disposed.

4. Cancellation during transitions must complete owned cleanup
--------------------------------------------------------------
Never hand a late materialized object to cancelled/closed work. The transition
ticket owns disposal of unpublished results through adapter.release exactly once,
outside locks. Known successful cleanup may leave the domain known-empty (or closed
if shutdown won); failed/unknown cleanup leaves failed/unknown protection. No auto
retry, resurrection of old READY or generic recovery is authorized. Cancellation
outcome and resource disposition remain separate. Do not retain a permanent blocker
merely because cancellation happened when ordinary owned cleanup can establish empty.
bindStop after cancellation/owner-close must still deliver the pending stop request
once; replacement/double registration semantics must be explicit and tested.

5. Two-stage host close must really reach cleanup outside locks
--------------------------------------------------------------
Current RuntimeHost.stop holds its host lock during application/shared-service
cleanup and returns early when state is STOPPED. Do not promise that all legacy
callbacks already run outside that lock. For the new facility, close admission
under the host lock, then call finishClose after leaving it. Preserve existing
host/shared-service behavior rather than refactoring unrelated consumer teardown.
Aggregate existing errors and new pending/failed/unknown cleanup honestly; setting
host STOPPED is not proof all resources drained. Repeated stop/finishClose must
not silently skip unresolved cleanup or repeat a provider disposal. Registering a
replacement domain after failed/unknown shutdown must remain prohibited.
Test a callback that requires a second thread to enter host/domain read paths:
same-thread RLock reentry alone does not establish absence of a held lock.
If lifecycle-only host wiring cannot preserve these bounds, report exact conflict
before expanding host/Application teardown scope.
