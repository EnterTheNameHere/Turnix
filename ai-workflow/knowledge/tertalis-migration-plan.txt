03. COORDINATION TRANSITION
---------------------------
03.01. GitHub native Issues, Pull Requests, Projects, and potentially
Discussions are the candidate backend for cross-repository
coordination. The existing ai-workflow AND file inbox/mail/state stay
operational during a controlled parallel period.

03.02. Never treat two inconsistent systems as silently co-authoritative.
Record the authoritative system for each operation/phase and reconcile
mirrored data explicitly. Retire active file-based inboxes only after
successful user-observed validation across Chat, Work, and Codex.

03.03. Sessions do NOT rely on shared conversation context. Each
workstream is recoverable from explicit handoff and coordination state.
Implementers primarily re-read bounded assignments. Coordinators may
maintain broader operational notes and handoffs. Design threads may
explore conversationally; maintain recoverable overall state and
materialize outward decisions/requests/instructions when crossing
workstream boundaries.

03.04. Admission rules: one implementer per logical implementation
workspace; competing claims STOP. One coordinator may cover multiple
repositories, and a coordinator claim conflicting on ANY repository
STOPs without partial registration. Unlimited design threads, subject
to existing coordination/implementation ownership. Registration
survives inactivity. Reassignment/release is explicit. GitHub Issues
alone do not guarantee atomic cross-repository claims: enforcement
mechanism must be proven before claiming that guarantee.

03.05. Release/change logging: preserve freeform Git commits. ai-workflow
collects accepted changes and evidence, while release records establish
what actually shipped. Human-facing changelogs emphasize observable
capabilities, fixes, breaking changes, deprecations and versions;
do not equate commits or inbox messages with released features.
