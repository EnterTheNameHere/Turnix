SEMANTIC_ANCHORS
----------------
- A Job answers what requested work is waiting, running, succeeded, failed, cancelled, timed out, superseded,
  or paused at a safe point.
- A Job is an explicit observable lifecycle object and request/work handle, not merely a Promise, coroutine,
  callback, thread, task, process, model request, or transport message.
- Job identity is independent of RPC request identity, UI request identity, transport event identity,
  capability-call identity, Orchestration Unit identity, and underlying execution identity.
- Job existence, execution, or terminal outcome does not by itself create authoritative committed state.
- Job effects become authoritative only through the state, transaction, Window, capability, and persistence rules
  owned by the documents that govern those effects.
- Job lifecycle and underlying execution mechanism lifecycle remain distinct.
- A Job may exist before underlying execution exists.
- A Job reaches exactly one terminal state, and terminal state is final.
- A closed Job is not restarted; retry is modeled as new work unless later documents define a retry relationship.
- Queued is a Pending phase or event, not a terminal state.
- Paused-at-safe-point is non-terminal and may later resume or reach a valid terminal outcome.
- Progress is observational lifecycle evidence and does not commit authoritative state by itself.
- Job result exists only for Succeeded; Failed, Cancelled, TimedOut, and Superseded have no Job result.
- Cancelled, TimedOut, and Superseded are not ordinary failure errors.
- Cancellation request and cancellation outcome are distinct lifecycle events.
- Cancellation before execution does not require an execution-stop attempt.
- Cancellation after execution begins may attempt to stop the underlying execution mechanism, but Job cancellation outcome
  and execution-stop outcome remain distinct.
- Cancellation does not silently roll back committed state.
- Timeout is explicit, uses a monotonic clock, and remains distinct from cancellation, failure, and transport timeout.
- Superseded is explicit, intentional, non-error, terminal, non-restartable, and produces no Job result.
- Superseded does not roll back authoritative state that was already committed and does not create new authoritative mutation
  after terminal transition.
- Late completion after a terminal outcome does not change terminal state, produce a result, deliver a reply,
  or commit new authoritative mutation.
- Safe-point pause is explicit, non-terminal, and remains constrained by DA-18 and DA-08.
- DA-19 owns Job-domain trace reasons, while DA-03 owns the trace substrate.
- Job lifecycle is distinct from RPC transport lifecycle; transport acknowledgement does not prove a Job exists.
- A request may be rejected before a Job exists, or accepted as a Job.
- Capability invocation may complete without creating a Job; observable/long-running/cancellable/timeout-managed capability work may create or attach to a Job.

CONFUSABLE_TERMS
----------------
- TERM: Job vs Promise/coroutine/thread/task/process/model request
  DISTINCTION: A Job is the observable lifecycle object; runtime primitives may implement execution but do not define Job semantics.
- TERM: Job lifecycle vs underlying execution lifecycle
  DISTINCTION: The Job lifecycle can close independently from the execution mechanism and late completion after closure is ignored.
- TERM: jobId vs RPC request identity
  DISTINCTION: jobId identifies the Job; it does not identify the RPC request by itself.
- TERM: jobId vs Orchestration Unit identity
  DISTINCTION: jobId identifies lifecycle correlation; Orchestration Unit identity identifies execution authority.
- TERM: Job terminal outcome vs committed state
  DISTINCTION: Terminal outcome reports lifecycle result; authoritative effects are governed by state, transaction, Window, capability, and persistence rules.
- TERM: Succeeded vs committed mutation
  DISTINCTION: Job success does not by itself mean effects became authoritative committed state.
- TERM: Cancelled vs Failed
  DISTINCTION: Cancellation is intentional and not represented as ordinary failure.
- TERM: TimedOut vs Cancelled
  DISTINCTION: Timeout is explicit and does not silently convert into cancellation.
- TERM: TimedOut vs Failed
  DISTINCTION: Timeout is distinct from failure unless a concrete API maps timeout into a failure envelope while preserving TimedOut lifecycle outcome.
- TERM: Superseded vs Cancelled
  DISTINCTION: Supersession is replacement by newer accepted input/work/priority, not ordinary user cancellation.
- TERM: Superseded vs Failed
  DISTINCTION: Superseded is a first-class non-error terminal outcome.
- TERM: cancellation request vs cancellation outcome
  DISTINCTION: A cancellation request does not guarantee immediate cancellation or define the terminal outcome by itself.
- TERM: cancellation outcome vs execution-stop outcome
  DISTINCTION: A Job can reach Cancelled while execution-stop attempt result is separate evidence.
- TERM: Job timeout vs RPC transport timeout
  DISTINCTION: Job timeout applies after accepted Job work; transport timeout belongs to RPC transport lifecycle.
- TERM: progress vs authoritative mutation
  DISTINCTION: Progress is observational lifecycle evidence and does not commit state.
- TERM: queued vs Running
  DISTINCTION: Queued remains Pending until execution begins.
- TERM: paused-at-safe-point vs Cancelled
  DISTINCTION: Safe-point pause is non-terminal and does not silently cancel the Job.
- TERM: paused-at-safe-point vs Superseded
  DISTINCTION: Safe-point pause is non-terminal and does not silently supersede the Job.
- TERM: late completion vs success
  DISTINCTION: Late completion after terminal transition is ignored and does not produce a result or reply.
