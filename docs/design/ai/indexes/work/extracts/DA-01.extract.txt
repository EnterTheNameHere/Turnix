OWNERSHIP_HINTS
---------------
- Owns Turnix core identity as a continuity engine, deterministic resolver,
  policy-driven orchestrator, and explainable system.
- Owns the Core Truth Doctrine: reproducibility, reversibility, and explicit uncertainty.
- Owns locked invariants for determinism, continuity, explicitness, surfaced decisions,
  local-first assumptions, implementation-independent platform truth, and orchestration-bound guarantees.
- Owns the highest-level architectural runtime authority ordering.
- Owns project-level anchors that later DA, ODA, IS, and DD documents preserve.
- Owns the explicitly open philosophical questions for pack autonomy, AI-assisted recovery,
  and long-term local-first strictness.

SEMANTIC_ANCHORS
----------------
- Turnix platform truth is contract-based and implementation-independent.
- Implementation convenience does not override platform identity.
- Turnix does not guarantee correctness; it guarantees reproducibility, reversibility,
  and explicit uncertainty.
- Silent substitution, implicit best-guess resolution, hidden defaults, and untraceable
  decisions violate CT-D.
- Persistence failures are existential, and quarantine is preferred over overwrite when
  integrity is at risk.
- Same host language, same process, deployment topology, or implementation proximity does
  not imply same authority.
- Work outside Turnix orchestration has no Turnix behavioral guarantee unless explicitly
  guaranteed elsewhere.
- Server-scale assumptions are rejected by default unless later policy or accepted design
  explicitly permits a bounded case.
