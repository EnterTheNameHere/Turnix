===============================================================================
DESIGN AREA DA-14 - ACTIVATION, LIFECYCLE & EXECUTION GUARANTEES
===============================================================================

Status:
-------
EVOLVING

Description:
------------
Defines the activation process, final Activated state, lifecycle phase
barriers, executable Pack participation, and execution guarantees for
Turnix-controlled Pack code.

Activation is the Turnix-controlled boundary that separates inert Pack
metadata from recognized executable participation under Turnix orchestration.

Purpose:
--------
This document defines the strict boundary between inert Pack metadata,
activation-process participation, final Activated state, and later
Turnix-recognized execution.

It defines:
- the activation process and final Activated state
- activation entries and executable Pack material roles
- Turnix-facing readiness before Pack code is loaded
- Stage 0 entry evaluation
- Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3 lifecycle semantics
- phase barriers and registration publication scopes
- ordinary Pack activation completion
- cyclic dependency group admission and activation
- multiple adapter/runtime participation for one Pack
- ModCallContext use during activation-related invocations
- immediate execution and top-level side-effect boundaries
- resolve/ensure activation interface boundaries
- activation trace obligations
- activation failure, cleanup limits, and NotActivatedError handling

It does NOT define:
- generic resolution mechanics (see DA-11)
- dependency semantics and source selection (see DA-13)
- importing and module-loading boundaries (see DA-12)
- execution adapter boundary semantics (see DA-23)
- adapter trust, lifecycle, or sandboxing semantics (see DA-27)
- tracing schema, storage, or retention policy (see DA-03)
- capability schemas and permission prompting details (see DA-16)
- user decision and remembered policy handling (see DA-17)
- Job lifecycle semantics (see DA-19)
- OrchestrationUnit mutation authority (see DA-24)
- RuntimeHost command-surface authority (see DA-28)
- PackManager catalog and graph-preparation ownership (see DA-29)
- detailed ModCallContext facade semantics (see DA-30)
- concrete manifest schema or concrete public hook declaration syntax

===============================================================================
01. INTERPRETATION BASIS
===============================================================================

01.1. This document expresses normative meaning through explicit,
      deterministic prose.

01.2. Normative meaning in Turnix derives from sentence structure and
      document context rather than from RFC-style keywords.

01.3. The CORE META-RULES documents define the general rule language and
      interpretation model used by this document.

01.4. This document defines activation, lifecycle, and execution-guarantee
      rules within that shared interpretation model.


===============================================================================
02. CORE CONCEPT
===============================================================================

02.1. Activation answers exactly one question:

          "Has Turnix intentionally brought this Pack into recognized
           executable participation under Turnix orchestration?"

02.2. The activation process is the Turnix-controlled process of preparing
      and attempting to bring a Pack or cyclic activation group into
      recognized executable participation.

02.3. Final Activated state is the externally visible state reached only
      after the required activation protocol succeeds.

02.4. Final activation boundary is the point where the Pack or admitted
      cyclic activation group becomes externally Activated.

02.5. Before the activation process:
      - Pack metadata may exist
      - Pack code has no Turnix-recognized executable participation
      - native import or adapter loading does not make the Pack Activated

02.6. During the activation process, Turnix may:
      - load declared activation entries
      - run Stage 0 entry evaluation
      - invoke Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3 callbacks
      - accept provisional registrations
      - prepare cyclic-group surfaces under phase and barrier rules

02.7. After successful activation:
      - the Pack or admitted cyclic activation group is externally Activated
      - eligible public registrations are externally published
      - later runtime execution may occur through Turnix orchestration

02.8. If activation fails:
      - the Pack is not Activated
      - the cyclic group is not Activated when group activation fails
      - provisional Turnix-owned registrations are withdrawn
      - staged Turnix-owned mutation is aborted where applicable
      - the failure is explicit and trace-visible

02.9. Activation is not:
      - discovery
      - indexing
      - dependency resolution
      - importing
      - adapter selection
      - execution authorization
      - adapter loading
      - visibility granting
      - capability permission
      - RuntimeHost authority
      - Job terminal outcome
      - OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome
      - Window finalization
      - committed-state mutation
      - trace substrate ownership

02.10. Those other phases or domains may participate in activation causes,
       inputs, or evidence, but none of them implies final Activated state.


===============================================================================
03. OUTER LIFECYCLE PHASES
===============================================================================

03.1. Turnix operates with the following outer lifecycle ordering for
      Pack executable participation:

      1) Discovery
      2) Indexing
      3) Resolution
      4) Execution adapter preparation where applicable
      5) Activation process
      6) Running or Finalized

03.2. Execution adapter preparation may include adapter selection,
      execution authorization, adapter creation, adapter readiness, and
      artifact loading.

03.3. Execution adapter preparation is not final Activated state.

03.4. The activation process is the first Turnix-supported context in
      which Turnix may intentionally invoke declared Pack activation-entry
      code with ModCallContext gateway authority.

03.5. Phase 1 and Phase 2 callbacks are activation-process participation.

03.6. Phase 1 and Phase 2 callbacks are not final Activated authority.

03.7. Final Activated state is reached only after the required activation
      protocol completes successfully.

03.8. After activation, Pack code may execute during Running only through
      Turnix-controlled orchestration pathways.

03.9. Code that executes outside Turnix orchestration remains outside
      Turnix guarantees.


===============================================================================
04. READINESS BEFORE ENTRY LOADING
===============================================================================

04.1. Before Turnix asks an ExecutionAdapter to load a Pack activation
      entry, the Turnix-facing infrastructure required by Pack code is
      operational.

04.2. Turnix is ready to create and provide ModCallContext values.

04.3. Turnix is ready to receive valid capability registrations.

04.4. Turnix is ready to receive permitted service or handler
      registrations.

04.5. Turnix is ready to route supported RPC requests.

04.6. Turnix is ready to accept supported capability invocations.

04.7. Turnix is ready to apply capability permission and policy mediation.

04.8. Turnix is ready to emit structured trace evidence.

04.9. Turnix is ready to classify and return structured errors.

04.10. Turnix is ready to associate registrations and calls with the
       current activation attempt.

04.11. Turnix is ready to withdraw provisional Turnix-owned registrations
       when activation fails.

04.12. Readiness means Turnix can receive Pack participation.

04.13. Readiness does not mean every requested capability, service,
       handler, event surface, or Pack-provided surface is already
       available.

04.14. Capability-system readiness means Turnix can accept mediated
       registration and invocation requests.

04.15. Capability availability means the requested capability has been
       registered and is visible at the current lifecycle barrier.

04.16. The same distinction applies to services, handlers, events, and
       other Pack-provided surfaces.


===============================================================================
05. ACTIVATION ENTRY MODEL
===============================================================================

05.1. A Pack may participate through multiple ExecutionAdapters.

05.2. Each declared adapter/runtime participation has at most one
      activation entry.

05.3. One activation entry coordinates that adapter/runtime participation.

05.4. The activation entry may load private implementation modules owned
      by the same Pack through mechanisms supported by its
      ExecutionAdapter.

05.5. Turnix does not automatically treat every code file as an activation
      entry.

05.6. Turnix does not automatically call lifecycle hooks on every code
      file found in a Pack directory.

05.7. Turnix does not define a plural list of automatically loaded
      non-activation entries merely to preload implementation files.

05.8. Executable Pack material has three distinct roles:

      Activation entry:
      - loaded by Turnix through one ExecutionAdapter
      - participates in Pack lifecycle hooks

      Private implementation module:
      - loaded by Pack code as needed
      - does not participate in lifecycle merely because it exists
      - is not externally addressable through Turnix by default

      Exported module surface:
      - publicly addressable through a supported mediated module loader
      - loaded on demand
      - does not receive lifecycle hooks merely because it is exported

05.9. Only activation entries participate in the activation lifecycle
      protocol defined by this document.

05.10. Concrete manifest fields that declare activation entries are owned
       by manifest and Pack-definition documents.

05.11. This document defines the lifecycle meaning of activation entries,
       not their concrete declaration syntax.


===============================================================================
06. ACTIVATION PROCESS OVERVIEW
===============================================================================

06.1. The Pack code lifecycle has four observable activation moments:

      Stage 0:
      - entry evaluation by the ExecutionAdapter

      Phase 1:
      - entry-local early lifecycle hook

      Phase 2:
      - all Pack entries loaded lifecycle hook

      Phase 3:
      - canonical Pack activation through onLoad(ctx)

06.2. Stage 0 is unavoidable host-runtime behavior.

06.3. Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3 are Turnix-supported lifecycle
      phases.

06.4. The current working hook name for Phase 1 is:
      onEntryLoaded(ctx)

06.5. The current working hook name for Phase 2 is:
      onEntriesLoaded(ctx)

06.6. The canonical Phase 3 activation hook is:
      onLoad(ctx)

06.7. Phase meanings are more important than final public hook names.

06.8. Most Pack creators need only Phase 3.

06.9. Phase 1 and Phase 2 exist for advanced cross-adapter
      initialization, cyclic prerequisites, and cases that ordinary
      acyclic onLoad(ctx) ordering cannot safely express.

06.10. Earlier lifecycle phases do not by themselves make a Pack
       externally Activated.

06.11. No function, construct, or mechanism activates a Pack unless the
       Turnix activation plan recognizes it as activation participation.

06.12. Adapter implementations do not introduce activation semantics by
       themselves.

06.13. Future lifecycle hook names or activation-entry declaration forms
       preserve the same ModCallContext, phase/barrier, and publication
       boundaries defined here.


===============================================================================
07. PHASE BARRIERS AND PUBLICATION
===============================================================================

07.1. Turnix guarantees phase barriers, not callback order.

07.2. Callbacks in one phase may be invoked sequentially, concurrently, in
      adapter-defined order, in scheduler-defined order, or in another
      implementation order.

07.3. Callback order inside a phase is not part of the public guarantee
      unless a separate contract explicitly defines that order.

07.4. A callback may rely only on guarantees established by completed
      earlier phase barriers.

07.5. A callback does not rely on another callback in the same phase having
      already run.

07.6. A registration made during one phase becomes guaranteed available
      only after the barrier for that phase has completed for the relevant
      scope.

07.7. Acceptance is not publication.

07.8. Registrations associated with an activation attempt may be:
      - accepted
      - provisional
      - visible inside one Pack
      - visible inside one cyclic group
      - externally published
      - withdrawn

07.9. Stage 0 and Phase 1 registrations become guaranteed only after the
      relevant Phase 1 barrier.

07.10. Phase 2 registrations become guaranteed only after the relevant
       Phase 2 barrier.

07.11. Phase 3 registrations become externally published only after
       successful Pack activation or successful cyclic-group activation.

07.12. After ordinary Pack activation, eligible public registrations become
       available to later dependents and runtime participants.

07.13. After cyclic-group activation, eligible public registrations of
       every group member become available together.

07.14. If activation fails, provisional Turnix-owned registrations are
       withdrawn.

07.15. If activation fails, external publication does not occur.

07.16. If activation fails, later dependent activation does not begin.

07.17. Registration visibility still obeys Pack visibility, capability
       identity, service exposure rules, permission decisions, policy,
       adapter support, and other applicable Turnix boundaries.

07.18. Early publication does not bypass any of those boundaries.


===============================================================================
08. STAGE 0 ENTRY EVALUATION
===============================================================================

08.1. Stage 0 begins when the ExecutionAdapter loads or evaluates the
      declared activation entry.

08.2. Entry evaluation follows the runtime behavior defined by the
      ExecutionAdapter and host language.

08.3. Top-level or module-level side effects may occur during entry
      evaluation.

08.4. Turnix does not prohibit such host-runtime side effects.

08.5. Turnix may be unable to prevent such host-runtime side effects.

08.6. Turnix discourages relying on entry-evaluation side effects.

08.7. Entry-evaluation side effects are not the safe ordinary activation
      pattern.

08.8. During entry evaluation, Turnix does not guarantee that:
      - another adapter entry of the same Pack has loaded
      - another Pack entry has loaded
      - a dependency has completed Phase 1
      - a dependency has completed Phase 2
      - a dependency has activated
      - another entry has registered a capability
      - another Pack has registered a capability
      - a particular adapter runs before another adapter
      - a particular Pack is loaded before another Pack

08.9. The supported Turnix-facing adapter bridge is operational during
      entry evaluation.

08.10. Module-level code may technically use supported Turnix-facing
       surfaces during entry evaluation.

08.11. The creator owns ordering and availability risks created by such
       entry-evaluation use.

08.12. Turnix does not guarantee that cross-adapter or cross-Pack calls
       made during entry evaluation are valid.

08.13. Turnix-owned registrations made during entry evaluation are
       associated with the current activation attempt.

08.14. Turnix-owned registrations made during entry evaluation remain
       provisional until later publication.

08.15. Turnix guarantees cleanup only for Turnix-owned and explicitly
       controlled activation material.

08.16. Turnix does not guarantee reversal of arbitrary side effects outside
       Turnix-controlled boundaries.


===============================================================================
09. PHASE 1 ENTRY-LOCAL EARLY HOOK
===============================================================================

09.1. Phase 1 invokes the entry-local early lifecycle hook for each
      declared activation entry that defines it.

09.2. Turnix invokes Phase 1 for an activation entry only after:
      - the ExecutionAdapter loaded or evaluated that entry successfully
      - unavoidable module-level evaluation for that entry completed
      - Turnix identified the entry's supported lifecycle hooks

09.3. If an activation entry does not define the Phase 1 hook, its Phase 1
      participation is a successful no-op.

09.4. Phase 1 is the first supported early lifecycle phase.

09.5. Phase 1 may be used for:
      - minimal early capability registration
      - minimal early service or handler registration where permitted
      - adapter-local state initialization
      - a surface needed by another adapter entry
      - a surface needed to handle a declared Pack dependency cycle
      - setup that does not depend on another entry being ready

09.6. Phase 1 is not ordinary Pack activation.

09.7. Phase 1 does not begin general application work.

09.8. Phase 1 does not assume another adapter entry has loaded.

09.9. Phase 1 does not assume another Pack's Phase 1 callback has run.

09.10. Phase 1 does not assume a dependency has completed Phase 2.

09.11. Phase 1 does not assume a dependency has activated.

09.12. Phase 1 does not depend on callback ordering inside the phase.

09.13. Phase 1 does not commit persistent state merely to establish
       activation ordering.

09.14. A Phase 1 callback may use Turnix-facing infrastructure.

09.15. A Phase 1 callback may use already available external capabilities.

09.16. A Phase 1 callback may use its own module-local state.

09.17. A Phase 1 callback may use registrations made earlier only when
       their availability is guaranteed by a completed barrier.

09.18. For one ordinary Pack, the Pack Phase 1 barrier completes only
       after every declared activation entry for that Pack has loaded and
       every declared activation entry has completed Phase 1 or no-op
       Phase 1 participation.

09.19. For one cyclic dependency group, the group Phase 1 barrier
       completes only after every declared activation entry of every Pack
       in the group has loaded and completed Phase 1 or no-op Phase 1
       participation.

09.20. A completed Phase 1 barrier does not mean the Pack is Activated.

09.21. A completed Phase 1 barrier does not mean the cyclic group is
       Activated.

09.22. A completed Phase 1 barrier does not mean Phase 2 or Phase 3
       registrations are available.

09.23. A completed Phase 1 barrier does not make external dependent Packs
       eligible to use the Pack.


===============================================================================
10. PHASE 2 ALL ENTRIES LOADED HOOK
===============================================================================

10.1. Phase 2 invokes the all-entries-loaded lifecycle hook for each
      activation entry that defines it.

10.2. If an activation entry does not define the Phase 2 hook, its Phase 2
      participation is a successful no-op.

10.3. For an ordinary non-cyclic Pack, Turnix invokes Phase 2 only after:
      - the Pack Phase 1 barrier completed
      - every hard dependency required by the activation plan completed its
        Phase 2 barrier

10.4. For a cyclic dependency group, Turnix invokes Phase 2 only after:
      - the group Phase 1 barrier completed
      - every hard dependency outside the cyclic group completed its Phase 2
        barrier

10.5. Phase 2 may be used for:
      - cross-adapter coordination
      - use of Phase 1 registrations
      - registration of surfaces required during onLoad(ctx)
      - completion of cyclic prerequisites
      - validation that required early registrations exist

10.6. Phase 2 callbacks may rely on completed Phase 1 barriers within the
      applicable Pack or cyclic-group scope.

10.7. Phase 2 callbacks may not rely on another Phase 2 callback in the
      same scope having already run.

10.8. For one ordinary Pack, the Pack Phase 2 barrier completes only after
      every Phase 2 callback completed successfully or no-op Phase 2
      participation completed successfully.

10.9. For one cyclic dependency group, the group Phase 2 barrier completes
      only after every Phase 2 callback of every Pack in the group
      completed successfully or no-op Phase 2 participation completed
      successfully.

10.10. After the relevant Phase 2 barrier, accepted Phase 2 registrations
       become available within the scope allowed by the activation plan.

10.11. A completed Phase 2 barrier does not mean the Pack is Activated.

10.12. A completed Phase 2 barrier does not mean the cyclic group is
       Activated.

10.13. A completed Phase 2 barrier does not mean Phase 3 callbacks have
       run.

10.14. A completed Phase 2 barrier does not externally publish Phase 3
       registrations.


===============================================================================
11. PHASE 3 CANONICAL ONLOAD ACTIVATION
===============================================================================

11.1. Phase 3 is the normal Pack activation phase.

11.2. The canonical Phase 3 activation hook is onLoad(ctx).

11.3. Most Pack creators use only onLoad(ctx).

11.4. Phase 3 may be used for:
      - the Pack's ordinary capability registrations
      - ordinary actions and handlers
      - dependency-provided public surfaces
      - normal activation setup
      - ordinary runtime contributions
      - preparing the Pack for ordinary participation

11.5. For an ordinary acyclic Pack, Turnix invokes Phase 3 only after:
      - all entries of this Pack loaded
      - this Pack completed Phase 1
      - this Pack completed Phase 2
      - every declared hard dependency activated successfully

11.6. Ordinary acyclic onLoad(ctx) may assume every hard dependency is
      Activated.

11.7. Ordinary acyclic onLoad(ctx) may use dependency public surfaces
      published by successful dependency activation according to normal
      visibility, capability, service, permission, and policy rules.

11.8. Ordinary acyclic onLoad(ctx) may assume every adapter entry of this
      Pack exists.

11.9. Ordinary acyclic onLoad(ctx) may assume every Phase 1 and Phase 2
      callback of this Pack completed.

11.10. Ordinary acyclic onLoad(ctx) may use Phase 1 and Phase 2
       registrations that are available in the allowed scope.

11.11. No ordering is guaranteed among multiple onLoad(ctx) callbacks that
       belong to different adapter entries of the same Pack.

11.12. If one adapter entry needs a same-Pack surface during onLoad(ctx),
       that surface is established in Phase 1 or Phase 2.

11.13. Phase 3 does not create same-phase ordering dependencies between
       adapter entries.

11.14. If an activation entry does not define onLoad(ctx), its Phase 3
       participation is a successful no-op unless a Pack-kind or adapter
       contract requires the hook.

11.15. Public Phase 3 registrations become externally available only after
       the Pack or cyclic group successfully crosses the final activation
       boundary.


===============================================================================
12. ORDINARY PACK ACTIVATION COMPLETION
===============================================================================

12.1. An ordinary non-cyclic Pack becomes Activated only after:
      - every declared activation entry loaded successfully
      - every Phase 1 callback completed successfully or no-op Phase 1
        participation completed successfully
      - every Phase 2 callback completed successfully or no-op Phase 2
        participation completed successfully
      - every Phase 3 callback completed successfully or allowed no-op
        Phase 3 participation completed successfully
      - activation validation completed successfully
      - no required registration or dependency check failed

12.2. Ordinary Pack activation is atomic at the Pack boundary.

12.3. A Pack is not partially Activated.

12.4. A Pack with one successful adapter entry and one failed required
      adapter entry is not mostly activated.

12.5. No public Phase 3 registration becomes externally available merely
      because one entry completed early.

12.6. After successful ordinary Pack activation, the Pack's eligible public
      registrations become available according to visibility, capability,
      service, permission, and policy rules.

12.7. After successful ordinary Pack activation, dependent Packs may begin
      Phase 3 according to the accepted activation plan.

12.8. After successful ordinary Pack activation, the Pack may participate
      in ordinary runtime work.


===============================================================================
13. CYCLIC DEPENDENCY ACTIVATION
===============================================================================

13.1. A strongly connected component is a set of Packs where every Pack is
      reachable from every other Pack through hard dependency edges.

13.2. A non-cyclic Pack is treated as a one-Pack activation component.

13.3. A cyclic component is treated as one cyclic activation group.

13.4. The component graph formed by collapsing strongly connected
      components is acyclic.

13.5. The component graph defines the outer activation order.

13.6. Canonical Phase 3 ordering alone cannot satisfy a dependency cycle.

13.7. Turnix does not pretend that a dependency cycle has a total onLoad
      order that satisfies every member.

13.8. A cyclic dependency group is admitted only when at least one
      activation entry in the group defines a supported Phase 1 or Phase 2
      hook.

13.9. The presence of at least one early hook means one or more cycle
      participants intentionally opted into the early activation protocol.

13.10. The presence of an early hook does not prove the cycle is logically
       correct.

13.11. The presence of an early hook does not prove every required early
       surface is registered.

13.12. The presence of an early hook does not prove the selected early
       phase is sufficient.

13.13. If no Pack in the cycle provides Phase 1 or Phase 2 handling,
       Turnix rejects the cycle.

13.14. UnsupportedActivationCycle identifies a cyclic dependency group that
       cannot be satisfied through ordinary onLoad(ctx) ordering and has no
       supported early lifecycle handler.

13.15. For a cyclic activation group, Turnix may load entries in any order
       allowed by adapter availability and implementation scheduling.

13.16. No dependency order is guaranteed inside the cycle before the group
       crosses its final activation boundary.

13.17. The group Phase 1 barrier completes only after every group entry
       loaded successfully and completed Phase 1 or no-op Phase 1
       participation.

13.18. The group Phase 2 barrier completes only after every group Phase 2
       callback completed successfully or no-op Phase 2 participation
       completed successfully.

13.19. Cyclic Phase 3 begins only after:
       - every external hard dependency is Activated
       - every group activation entry loaded
       - every group Phase 1 callback completed
       - every group Phase 2 callback completed
       - all eligible Phase 1 and Phase 2 registrations inside the group
         are available within permitted scope

13.20. During cyclic Phase 3, no intra-cycle onLoad(ctx) order is
       guaranteed.

13.21. During cyclic Phase 3, no group member may assume another group
       member is already Activated.

13.22. Every intra-cycle capability, service, handler, or other surface
       required during onLoad(ctx) must have been established in Phase 1 or
       Phase 2.

13.23. If cyclic onLoad(ctx) requests an intra-cycle required surface that
       is not available, Turnix fails immediately.

13.24. Turnix does not wait for another onLoad(ctx) callback to register a
       missing cyclic surface.

13.25. Turnix does not suspend the request indefinitely.

13.26. Turnix does not retry the same phase in another order.

13.27. CyclicDependencySurfaceNotReady identifies a cyclic Phase 3 request
       for an intra-cycle surface that was not established in an earlier
       phase.

13.28. After every group onLoad(ctx) callback succeeds, every Pack in the
       group becomes Activated together.

13.29. The cyclic group is atomic at its external activation boundary.

13.30. External dependent Packs do not begin Phase 3 until the entire
       cyclic group has activated successfully.

13.31. One member failure fails the entire cyclic activation group.

13.32. Cycles weaken availability assumptions only inside the cyclic group.

13.33. Cycles do not weaken the component graph outside the cyclic group.


===============================================================================
14. MULTIPLE ADAPTERS IN ONE PACK
===============================================================================

14.1. A Pack may declare multiple activation entries, one per
      adapter/runtime participation.

14.2. Turnix does not define a universal adapter order.

14.3. Turnix may load adapter entries in any order allowed by adapter
      availability and implementation scheduling.

14.4. Phase 1 is entry-local.

14.5. Phase 2 guarantees that all Pack entries completed Phase 1.

14.6. Phase 3 guarantees that all Pack entries completed Phase 2.

14.7. One adapter's Phase 2 callback may use another adapter entry's
      Phase 1 registration only after that registration is available under
      the completed Phase 1 barrier.

14.8. One adapter's onLoad(ctx) callback may use another adapter entry's
      Phase 1 or Phase 2 registration only after that registration is
      available under the completed earlier barrier.

14.9. One adapter's onLoad(ctx) callback may not assume another adapter's
      onLoad(ctx) callback already ran.

14.10. Ordinary outward-facing functionality is normally registered during
       onLoad(ctx).

14.11. Cross-adapter prerequisites required during onLoad(ctx) are
       registered earlier in Phase 1 or Phase 2.

14.12. Early phases are exceptional tools for cross-adapter and cyclic
       activation needs.

14.13. Early phases are not mandatory ceremony for ordinary Packs.


===============================================================================
15. MODCALLCONTEXT (ctx) GATEWAY
===============================================================================

15.1. Turnix provides Pack-facing invocation authority through
      ModCallContext (ctx).

15.2. ModCallContext is created only by Turnix.

15.3. Every Turnix invocation of Pack code receives a fresh ModCallContext
      when the invoked Pack code needs platform access.

15.4. ModCallContext is bound to the acting Pack and invocation scope.

15.5. ModCallContext may also be bound to Application, ApplicationRun,
      cause/correlation, current OrchestrationUnit, current transaction,
      current Job, current ProcessingRun, current lifecycle phase,
      cancellation, deadline, and stage-specific granted operations where
      those bindings exist.

15.6. ModCallContext represents scoped gateway authority for that
      invocation.

15.7. ModCallContext is not a raw backend service object exposed to Pack
      code.

15.8. ModCallContext is not unrestricted access to Kernel internals.

15.9. ModCallContext operations route through Kernel-owned boundaries.

15.10. Code executing with a ModCallContext executes under the authority of
       the acting Pack.

15.11. Code executing with a ModCallContext is subject to the acting Pack's
       visibility, permissions, and policy.

15.12. Code executing with a ModCallContext remains constrained by
       capability, transaction, persistence, trace, adapter, lifecycle,
       cancellation, and deadline rules.

15.13. ModCallContext may be represented differently in different adapters.

15.14. Representation difference does not change authority meaning.

15.15. In-process ModCallContext calls do not bypass capability mediation,
       policy, transaction, trace, persistence, or lifecycle rules.

15.16. Pack code does not retain ModCallContext after the invocation
       completes.

15.17. Registrations retain owner, callback endpoint, registration
       metadata, and registration scope.

15.18. Registrations do not retain the ModCallContext that created them.

15.19. Later callbacks always receive a fresh ModCallContext when Pack code
       needs platform access.

15.20. Detailed ModCallContext facade semantics are owned by DA-30.


===============================================================================
16. ACTIVATION-INDEPENDENT CODE AND MODULE SURFACES
===============================================================================

16.1. Activation-independent code does not assume its own Pack is
      Activated.

16.2. Activation-independent code does not implicitly depend on ctx.

16.3. Activation-independent code does not perform Turnix side effects at
      module top level.

16.4. Activation-independent code may accept a ModCallContext explicitly as
      a parameter.

16.5. Code using a caller-provided ModCallContext executes under the
      authority of the caller Pack.

16.6. The authority is that of the caller, not the provider.

16.7. Passing ModCallContext into activation-independent code does not
      change the provider Pack's activation state.

16.8. If code requires activation-specific state of its own Pack, that code
      is initialized from that Pack's activation entry.

16.9. Private implementation modules do not participate in lifecycle merely
      because Pack code loads them.

16.10. Exported module surfaces do not participate in lifecycle merely
       because they are exported.

16.11. The activation protocol guarantees that declared activation entries
       are loaded.

16.12. The activation protocol does not guarantee that every private or
       exported code file in the Pack has been evaluated.

16.13. Pack dependency cycles and native language module-import cycles are
       different problems.

16.14. The early activation protocol resolves Pack lifecycle availability.

16.15. The early activation protocol does not repair arbitrary
       host-language import recursion.

16.16. Cross-Pack code access remains mediated through supported Turnix
       mechanisms.


===============================================================================
17. RESOLUTION AND ACTIVATION INTERFACE BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

17.1. A resolution operation may resolve metadata.

17.2. A resolution operation may perform visibility checks.

17.3. A resolution operation does not select an adapter for execution by
      itself.

17.4. A resolution operation does not authorize execution by itself.

17.5. A resolution operation does not load code by itself.

17.6. A resolution operation does not activate Packs by itself.

17.7. An ensure-activation operation ensures that the target Pack is
      Activated or fails explicitly.

17.8. An ensure-activation operation may trigger activation.

17.9. An ensure-activation operation may cause adapter-mediated activation
      when the target Pack participates through an ExecutionAdapter.

17.10. An ensure-activation operation returns activation metadata,
       diagnostics, status, or warnings.

17.11. Activation metadata is immutable and diagnostic.

17.12. An ensure-activation operation does not return executable code.

17.13. An ensure-activation operation does not return module namespaces.

17.14. An ensure-activation operation does not return runtime symbols.

17.15. An ensure-activation operation does not expose adapter internals.

17.16. An ensure-activation operation may raise NotActivatedError.

17.17. Concrete public API names for resolution and ensure-activation
       operations are owned by API-specific documents or implementation
       specifications.


===============================================================================
18. ORCHESTRATED EXECUTION GUARANTEE
===============================================================================

18.1. Turnix guarantees behavior only for execution invoked and tracked
      through Turnix orchestration.

18.2. Guaranteed execution pathways include:
      - activation lifecycle callbacks invoked by Turnix
      - capability calls dispatched by Turnix
      - Turnix event dispatch for handlers registered through Turnix
      - Turnix-managed timers and schedulers where accepted
      - IoManager-mediated operations
      - other explicitly defined Turnix-controlled pathways

18.3. Execution started outside Turnix orchestration is unsupported.

18.4. Unsupported execution may still run, but Turnix provides:
      - no activation guarantee
      - no tracing guarantee
      - no lifecycle control guarantee
      - no cleanup guarantee

18.5. Adapter-mediated execution has Turnix guarantees only when Turnix
      invokes and tracks it through Turnix orchestration.

18.6. In-process execution has no extra guarantees merely because it runs
      in the same process as trusted backend implementation code.

18.7. Same host language does not grant backend implementation authority.

18.8. Physical possibility does not create a supported Turnix contract.


===============================================================================
19. ACTIVATION TRACE OBLIGATIONS
===============================================================================

19.1. Activation lifecycle outcomes remain trace-visible.

19.2. Activation trace evidence records enough context to explain:
      - which Pack identity participated
      - which activation attempt was involved
      - which activation entry was involved
      - which adapter identity was involved when applicable
      - which dependency component was involved
      - which cyclic group was involved when applicable
      - which lifecycle phase was active
      - which callback was invoked
      - which registration was accepted, published, or withdrawn
      - which dependency or ordering condition caused waiting or failure
      - which failure caused activation to abort
      - which cleanup result occurred when cleanup was attempted

19.3. Activation-domain evidence categories include:
      - activation plan accepted
      - dependency component identified
      - cyclic group detected
      - cyclic group admitted
      - cyclic group rejected
      - activation entry load requested
      - activation entry loaded
      - activation entry load failed
      - Phase 1 callback started, completed, or failed
      - Phase 1 barrier completed
      - Phase 2 callback started, completed, or failed
      - Phase 2 barrier completed
      - Phase 3 callback started, completed, or failed
      - registration accepted
      - registration published to Pack scope
      - registration published to cyclic-group scope
      - registration published externally
      - registration withdrawn
      - cyclic dependency surface unavailable
      - Pack activation completed
      - Pack activation failed
      - cyclic-group activation completed
      - cyclic-group activation failed
      - cleanup requested, completed, or failed
      - not-activated access failure

19.4. Exact trace reason identifier names remain implementation-facing until
      the tracing documents or implementation specifications lock them.

19.5. DA-14 owns activation-domain trace reason meaning.

19.6. DA-03 owns the tracing substrate used to represent activation trace
      evidence.

19.7. DA-23 and DA-27 own adapter-domain trace evidence that may causally
      relate to activation dispatch or adapter behavior.

19.8. Activation trace evidence defines runtime causality and activation
      scope boundaries.

19.9. Activation trace evidence does not redefine import, dependency
      resolution, adapter lifecycle, capability permission, Job lifecycle,
      OrchestrationUnit lifecycle, Window finalization, committed-state
      authority, or trace substrate semantics.

19.10. Trace evidence describes what Turnix observed.

19.11. Trace evidence does not make external side effects reversible.

19.12. Trace evidence does not make retained evidence authoritative
       committed state.

19.13. Trace persistence and retention remain separate from activation
       semantics.


===============================================================================
20. ACTIVATION FAILURE AND CLEANUP LIMITS
===============================================================================

20.1. The following failures fail an ordinary Pack activation:
      - activation entry load or evaluation failure
      - unsupported entry format
      - missing required adapter
      - Phase 1 hook failure
      - Phase 2 hook failure
      - Phase 3 hook failure
      - required early registration missing
      - dependency activation failure
      - capability, service, or handler registration rejection
      - permission or policy denial where activation requires acceptance
      - activation validation failure

20.2. The following failures fail an entire cyclic activation group:
      - any member entry load or evaluation failure
      - any member Phase 1 failure
      - any member Phase 2 failure
      - any member Phase 3 failure
      - missing required early cyclic surface
      - unsupported cycle with no early handler
      - external dependency activation failure
      - cyclic-group validation failure

20.3. On activation failure, Turnix marks no failed Pack as Activated.

20.4. On cyclic-group activation failure, Turnix marks no cyclic-group
      member as Activated.

20.5. On activation failure, Turnix withdraws provisional Turnix-owned
      registrations.

20.6. On activation failure, Turnix aborts staged Turnix-owned state
      mutation where applicable.

20.7. On activation failure, Turnix prevents external dependents from
      activating.

20.8. On activation failure, Turnix classifies the failure.

20.9. On activation failure, Turnix emits trace evidence.

20.10. On activation failure, Turnix invokes supported cleanup or
       deactivation paths where available.

20.11. Turnix cannot guarantee reversal of arbitrary effects performed
       outside Turnix-controlled boundaries.

20.12. Effects outside Turnix-controlled boundaries include:
       - arbitrary network requests
       - writes outside Turnix persistence authority
       - external process side effects
       - host-language module-level side effects
       - direct unsupported backend access

20.13. Creators who perform irreversible effects before successful
       activation own those effects.

20.14. Turnix guarantees cleanup only for Turnix-owned and explicitly
       controlled activation material.

20.15. Silent fallback is forbidden.

20.16. Activation failure is not a Job terminal outcome merely because the
       activation path used adapter-executed code.

20.17. Activation failure is not an OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome
       merely because the activation path used orchestrated work.

20.18. NotActivatedError identifies a Turnix-mediated action refused
       because the target Pack is not Activated.

20.19. Pack code does not swallow NotActivatedError.

20.20. Swallowing NotActivatedError hides orchestration failure.


===============================================================================
21. ASYNC HOOK COMPLETION
===============================================================================

21.1. A lifecycle hook completes only when its returned synchronous or
      asynchronous result completes according to the adapter contract.

21.2. Required setup is awaited by the hook.

21.3. Starting detached background work and returning does not extend the
      phase barrier.

21.4. Detached work started outside Turnix orchestration does not receive
      Turnix lifecycle guarantees by implication.

21.5. A Pack does not publish a capability at a barrier while its
      implementation is still being prepared by untracked detached work.

21.6. If a capability is published at a barrier, it is callable according
      to the guarantees of that barrier.

21.7. Hook timeout, cancellation, and host-disconnect behavior remain
      explicit activation-policy topics.

21.8. No hidden retry or alternate callback order is implied.


===============================================================================
22. EXPLICITLY OPEN QUESTIONS
===============================================================================

22.1. The final public names for Phase 1 and Phase 2 hooks remain open.

22.2. The exact manifest schema for declaring activation entries and hooks
      remains open.

22.3. Exact adapter-mediated activation diagnostics remain open.

22.4. Activation retry semantics remain open.

22.5. Category-based activation ordering remains open.

22.6. Exact cleanup or deactivation hooks remain open.

22.7. Exact timeout, cancellation, and host-disconnect policy for
      activation hooks remain open.

22.8. Placeholder registration or late-binding protocols for early phases
      remain open.

22.9. Open questions do not weaken the activation boundary, final
      Activated-state boundary, phase-barrier model, or publication rules
      defined by this document.


===============================================================================
23. SUMMARY
===============================================================================

23.1. Activation is executable Pack participation under Turnix
      orchestration.

23.2. The activation process may contain Stage 0, Phase 1, Phase 2, and
      Phase 3 before final Activated state is reached.

23.3. Final Activated state is externally visible only after the Pack or
      admitted cyclic activation group crosses its activation boundary.

23.4. Turnix guarantees phase barriers, not same-phase callback order.

23.5. A Pack may have multiple adapter/runtime participations.

23.6. A Pack or cyclic activation group is not partially Activated.

23.7. Private implementation modules and exported module surfaces are not
      activation entries merely because they exist or are exported.

23.8. ModCallContext is a fresh invocation-scoped gateway, not raw backend
      internals.

23.9. Activation and later runtime execution are distinct.

23.10. Immediate execution and top-level side effects are unsupported as
       activation authority.

23.11. Execution has Turnix guarantees only when invoked and tracked
       through Turnix orchestration.

23.12. Activation failures are explicit, trace-visible, and do not silently
       fall back.

23.13. DA-14 owns activation lifecycle semantics while leaving resolution,
       dependencies, importing, adapters, capabilities, policy, Jobs,
       OrchestrationUnits, RuntimeHost, PackManager, and ModCallContext
       facade details to their owning documents.
