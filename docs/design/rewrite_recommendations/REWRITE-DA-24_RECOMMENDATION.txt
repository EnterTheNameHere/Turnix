SUMMARY
=======

DA-24 is Dv3-compatible.

No heavy rewrite is recommended.

DA-24 defines how Turnix identifies and controls execution that may mutate
Turnix-managed state.

Preserve the central rule:

    OrchestrationUnit coordinates mutation authority.

    DA-04 still owns committed-state truth.

Main required clarification:

    Preserve the intended meaning of "execution-authority unit".

    It means an OrchestrationUnit owns or coordinates a mutation flow.

    It does not mean arbitrary contained code receives direct committed-state
    authority.

Overall recommendation:

    KEEP

ACTUAL DA-24 CHANGE 1: PRESERVE ORCHESTRATIONUNIT AUTHORITY STOP-LINE
=====================================================================

DA-24 should contain a compact source-level boundary statement.

Recommended source-level boundary:

    OrchestrationUnit is a kernel-tracked execution-authority unit that may own
    or coordinate mutation-bearing work.

    OrchestrationUnit authority is scoped authority to coordinate a mutation
    flow.

    It is not general backend authority.

    It is not direct committed-state authority for arbitrary contained code.

    It does not replace DA-04 transaction and commit boundaries.

    It does not replace Job, Capability, RPC, ExecutionAdapter, RuntimeHost,
    Window, AppInstance, debugger, policy, or PackManager semantics.

This source-level boundary belongs in DA-24 because DA-24 owns
OrchestrationUnit lifecycle and mutation-authority semantics.

Detailed ownership mapping belongs in the guardrail section, not in the source
document.


PRESERVATION RULE 1: MUTATION AUTHORITY DOES NOT BYPASS COMMIT AUTHORITY
========================================================================

Preserve DA-24's mutation authority model.

Bad model:

    OrchestrationUnit runs work.

    Therefore it can mutate truth directly.

Correct model:

    OrchestrationUnit owns or coordinates mutation flow.

    Mutation still crosses DA-04 transaction and commit boundaries.

OrchestrationUnit may be where decisions happen, where work is sequenced, where
transactions are opened, where tools, models, adapters, PackManager operations,
RuntimeHost operations, or capability calls are coordinated, and where outcomes
are resolved.

Committed truth still belongs to the DA-04 commit model.


PRESERVATION RULE 2: ADAPTER-EXECUTED CODE DOES NOT DIRECTLY MUTATE COMMITTED STATE
==================================================================================

Preserve ExecutionAdapter / OrchestrationUnit separation.

Adapter-executed mutation seeking Turnix guarantees re-enters through:

    - orchestration
    - capability
    - transaction
    - commit boundaries

Same-language or same-process execution does not bypass committed-state
authority.

Direct in-process calls may carry a mutation request, but they do not become
mutation authority by themselves.

Adapter lifecycle state does not become OrchestrationUnit lifecycle state by
itself.

Adapter failure does not become OrchestrationUnit failure by default.

DA-23 owns abstract ExecutionAdapter authority boundaries.

DA-27 owns adapter lifecycle, trust, isolation, and adapter failure/disconnect
semantics.


PRESERVATION RULE 3: RUNTIMEHOST OPERATIONS DO NOT BECOME ORCHESTRATION OUTCOMES BY DEFAULT
===========================================================================================

Preserve RuntimeHost / OrchestrationUnit separation.

RuntimeHost command acceptance does not imply OrchestrationUnit completion.

RuntimeHost operation outcome does not by itself define OrchestrationUnit
terminal outcome unless the owning workflow maps those outcomes explicitly.

This prevents CLI, command surface, RuntimeHost operation, transport ACK, entry
surface behavior, or management surface behavior from becoming orchestration
outcome authority.

DA-28 owns RuntimeHost semantics.


PRESERVATION RULE 4: JOB AND ORCHESTRATIONUNIT REMAIN SEPARATE
==============================================================

Preserve Job / OrchestrationUnit separation.

A Job terminal outcome is not automatically an OrchestrationUnit terminal
outcome.

An OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome is not automatically a Job terminal
outcome.

Correct Dv3 distinction:

    OrchestrationUnit = kernel-tracked execution-authority unit that owns or
    coordinates mutation flow.

    Job = observable lifecycle handle for requested work.

Avoid the weaker wording:

    Job = bounded operation

A Job may represent bounded requested work, but DA-19 defines Job as the
observable lifecycle handle, not the execution primitive or operation itself.


PRESERVATION RULE 5: CAPABILITY AND ORCHESTRATIONUNIT REMAIN SEPARATE
=====================================================================

Preserve Capability / OrchestrationUnit separation.

A capability request is not automatically an OrchestrationUnit.

A capability result is not automatically an OrchestrationUnit outcome.

Capability-mediated work may create, attach to, or participate in Jobs or
OrchestrationUnits only through the owning runtime rules.

Correct Dv3 distinction:

    Capability = named, permissioned, Turnix-mediated operation.

    OrchestrationUnit = kernel-tracked execution-authority unit that owns or
    coordinates mutation flow.

Capability permission does not grant committed-state authority by itself.

Job-backed capability execution remains capability-mediated and does not become
OrchestrationUnit authority by implication.


PRESERVATION RULE 6: RPC DOES NOT DEFINE ORCHESTRATION OUTCOME
==============================================================

Preserve RPC / OrchestrationUnit separation.

RPC request is not automatically an OrchestrationUnit.

RPC acknowledgement is not OrchestrationUnit completion.

RPC request acceptance is not OrchestrationUnit success unless the owning
operation contract explicitly defines acceptance as the complete result.

Transport timeout, disconnect, and cancellation-message handling do not directly
define OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome.

If RPC or transport behavior affects OrchestrationUnit state, the owning
workflow and DA-24 semantics define the OrchestrationUnit outcome.

DA-21 owns RPC, channel, transport, timeout, disconnect, and cancellation
message semantics.


PRESERVATION RULE 7: WINDOW FINALIZATION DOES NOT DEFINE ORCHESTRATION OUTCOME
==============================================================================

Preserve Window / OrchestrationUnit separation.

Window finalization is not OrchestrationUnit outcome by default.

SafePointWindow creation does not by itself mean OrchestrationUnit Completed.

AbortedWindow creation does not by itself mean OrchestrationUnit Failed,
Cancelled, or Superseded.

OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome does not by itself create SafePointWindow or
AbortedWindow.

Window finalization and OrchestrationUnit outcome may be causally related, but
the relationship must be explicit in the owning workflow.

DA-08 owns Window, ActiveWindow, SafePointWindow, AbortedWindow, journal
evidence, and finalized runtime history semantics.


PRESERVATION RULE 8: ORCHESTRATION OUTCOMES STAY DISTINCT
=========================================================

Preserve OrchestrationUnit terminal outcomes:

    - Completed
    - Failed
    - Cancelled
    - Superseded

Preserve Superseded as a non-error terminal outcome.

Superseded is:

    - not failure
    - not timeout
    - not rollback by itself
    - does not roll back authoritative state already committed

Open transactional staging is resolved by DA-04.

Job terminal outcomes, adapter outcomes, RuntimeHost outcomes, RPC outcomes,
Window outcomes, and policy decisions do not automatically substitute for these
OrchestrationUnit outcomes.


PRESERVATION RULE 9: SINGLE-LANE APPINSTANCE ORCHESTRATION REMAINS DEFAULT
==========================================================================

Preserve the single-lane default.

Turnix uses deterministic single-lane orchestration within one AppInstance
unless an explicit concurrency mode is defined elsewhere.

Turnix does not execute multiple OrchestrationUnits concurrently when those
units may mutate the same AppInstance state.

Awaited model calls, adapter calls, transport calls, RuntimeHost calls, or other
I/O may suspend the current unit.

Suspension does not authorize another state-mutating unit to run concurrently
inside the same AppInstance unless explicit concurrency mode permits it.

Reason:

    Async/suspension behavior must not accidentally become concurrency
    authority.

DA-20 owns AppInstance lifecycle and runtime identity.

DA-04 owns committed-state mutation authority.


PRESERVATION RULE 10: ORCHESTRATIONUNIT TRACE OWNERSHIP STAYS DOMAIN-SPECIFIC
=============================================================================

Preserve OrchestrationUnit trace ownership.

DA-24 owns OrchestrationUnit trace reason identifiers.

DA-03 owns the trace substrate.

DA-24 trace evidence does not replace:

    - DA-04 transaction trace evidence
    - DA-08 Window trace/evidence
    - DA-16 capability trace evidence
    - DA-17 decision/policy trace evidence
    - DA-19 Job trace evidence
    - DA-20 AppInstance trace evidence
    - DA-21 RPC trace evidence
    - DA-23 / DA-27 adapter trace evidence
    - DA-28 RuntimeHost trace evidence
    - DA-29 PackManager trace evidence

Trace wording should follow the DA-03 cross-document trace wording check.


WATCH ITEM: EXECUTION-AUTHORITY WORDING
=======================================

The phrase "execution-authority unit" is useful, but later readers could misread
it as:

    anything inside the unit has direct authority

Preserve this intended meaning:

    OrchestrationUnit has authority to coordinate or own a mutation flow.

    It does not grant arbitrary contained code direct committed-state authority.

This is especially important for:

    - adapter-executed code
    - same-language Python pack code
    - model/tool callbacks
    - future plugin-like behavior
    - PackManager-triggered graph or lifecycle operations
    - RuntimeHost-triggered operations
    - capability-backed operations


WATCH ITEM: DEBUGGER SEMANTICS
==============================

DA-18 should preserve safe-point pause and mutation-boundary semantics.

Debugger pause must not become OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome.

Debugger breakpoint behavior must not grant mutation authority.

Debugger tooling that initiates mutation must still use DA-04 transaction and
commit boundaries.

It exists to prevent architectural drift during rewrite when the editor knows
DA-24 but may not have every related document loaded.

Use these as guardrails only.

Orchestration-boundary guardrails:

    OrchestrationUnit coordinates mutation authority.

    OrchestrationUnit is not arbitrary contained-code authority.

    OrchestrationUnit is not general backend authority.

    OrchestrationUnit is not committed-state truth.

    OrchestrationUnit is not transaction.

    OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome is not automatic transaction commit.

    OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome is not automatic transaction abort.

    OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome is not automatic Job terminal outcome.

    Job terminal outcome is not automatic OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome.

    Capability request is not automatically OrchestrationUnit.

    Capability result is not automatically OrchestrationUnit outcome.

    RPC request is not automatically OrchestrationUnit.

    RPC acknowledgement is not OrchestrationUnit completion.

    RuntimeHost command acceptance is not OrchestrationUnit completion.

    RuntimeHost operation outcome is not OrchestrationUnit outcome by default.

    Adapter execution acceptance is not OrchestrationUnit completion.

    Adapter execution failure is not OrchestrationUnit failure by default.

    Window finalization is not OrchestrationUnit outcome by default.

    SafePointWindow is not OrchestrationUnit completion by default.

    AbortedWindow is not OrchestrationUnit failure by default.

    Browser/frontend state loss is not OrchestrationUnit outcome.

    PackManager-triggered graph or lifecycle operations do not bypass
    OrchestrationUnit authority boundaries.

    Same-language execution does not bypass OrchestrationUnit authority rules.

    Same-process execution does not bypass OrchestrationUnit authority rules.

    Suspension/await does not create concurrency authority.

    Single-lane AppInstance orchestration remains the default unless explicit
    concurrency mode permits otherwise.

Owning-document guardrails:

    DA-04 owns memory, state, transaction, commit mechanics, and committed-state

    DA-08 owns Window, ActiveWindow, SafePointWindow, AbortedWindow, journal

    DA-16 owns service, capability, and permission semantics.

    DA-17 owns user decision and remembered policy semantics.

    DA-18 owns debugger pause, breakpoint, and execution-control behavior.

    DA-19 owns Job lifecycle, Job timeout, Job cancellation, Job progress, Job
    terminal outcome, and Job result semantics.

    DA-20 owns AppInstance lifecycle and runtime identity semantics.

    DA-21 owns RPC, channel, transport, timeout, disconnect, and cancellation

    DA-22 owns UI, View, and frontend runtime semantics.

    DA-23 owns abstract execution adapter boundary semantics.

    DA-27 owns adapter lifecycle, trust, isolation, failure, disconnect, and

    DA-28 owns RuntimeHost identity, workspace ownership, startup modes, entry

Local rewrite rule:

    DA-24 may name these concepts when needed to protect OrchestrationUnit
    lifecycle and mutation-authority boundaries.

    DA-24 should not define their detailed behavior.

    DA-24 should remain the OrchestrationUnit lifecycle, mutation-authority,

CURRENT FINAL RECOMMENDATION
============================

Keep DA-24.

No heavy rewrite is recommended.

Preserve the core rule:

    OrchestrationUnit coordinates mutation authority.

    DA-04 still owns committed-state truth.

Preserve execution-authority wording as coordination authority, not arbitrary
contained-code authority.

Preserve OrchestrationUnit separation from:

    - DA-04 transaction and commit authority
    - DA-08 Window finalization
    - DA-16 capability semantics
    - DA-17 policy and decision semantics
    - DA-19 Job lifecycle
    - DA-20 AppInstance lifecycle
    - DA-21 RPC and transport semantics
    - DA-23 / DA-27 adapter boundary and lifecycle semantics
    - DA-28 RuntimeHost operations and command surfaces
    - DA-29 PackManager graph-management semantics

Do not expand DA-24 into detailed memory/state/transaction mechanics, trace
substrate, Window finalization, capability/permission, user-decision policy,
debugger execution-control, Job lifecycle, AppInstance lifecycle,
RPC/channel/transport, UI/View, ExecutionAdapter boundary, adapter lifecycle,
RuntimeHost command-surface, or PackManager graph-management semantics.
