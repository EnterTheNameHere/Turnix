SUMMARY
=======

DA-17 is Dv3-compatible.

No heavy rewrite is recommended.

Keep DA-17 as the Kernel-mediated user-decision and policy-handling document.

Preserve the central rule:

    Turnix does not make ambiguous decisions silently.

    Turnix does not delegate authority implicitly.

    Policy reduces repeated friction; it does not redefine authority.

Decision handling exists only where Turnix cannot proceed safely or
deterministically on its own.

Decisions affecting identity, persistence, authority, safety, work lifecycle,
or known content equivalence must remain explicit and trace-backed.

Overall recommendation:

    KEEP WITH SMALL WORK-LIFECYCLE KEY / CROSS-SCOPE PROMOTION WATCH

======================

KEEP WITH SMALL WORK-LIFECYCLE KEY / CROSS-SCOPE PROMOTION WATCH

DA-17 should not be rewritten heavily.

Future edits should preserve:

ACTUAL DA-17 CHANGE 1: POLICY DOES NOT DEFINE DOMAIN OUTCOME
===========================================================

DA-17 should contain a compact source-level boundary statement.

Recommended source-level boundary:

    Policy decisions choose allowed responses where Turnix cannot safely or
    deterministically proceed on its own.

    Policy does not redefine authority.

    Policy does not directly define Job terminal states, RPC/transport outcomes,
    OrchestrationUnit terminal outcomes, AppInstance lifecycle, adapter
    lifecycle, RuntimeHost lifecycle, Window finalization, PackManager operation
    outcomes, capability permission, or committed-state truth.

    If a policy decision affects another domain, the owning domain defines the
    resulting domain outcome.

    Correlation between a policy decision and a domain outcome must be explicit.

This belongs in DA-17 because DA-17 owns decision and remembered-policy
semantics.

Detailed ownership mapping belongs in the guardrail section, not in the source
document.


PRESERVATION RULE 1: DECISION AUTHORITY IS KERNEL-MEDIATED
==========================================================

Preserve the rule:

    User interaction is a system concern.

User interaction is not:

    - UI-specific
    - mod-specific
    - frontend-specific

User interaction is:

    - Kernel-mediated
    - policy-driven
    - serializable
    - trace-backed

All decision points originate in the Kernel.

Mods and packs may trigger decision points.

Mods and packs do not own, implement, or bypass decision handling.

Frontends present decisions; they do not own decision semantics.


PRESERVATION RULE 2: DECISION TYPES REMAIN DISTINCT
===================================================

Preserve distinct decision-point types:

    - Resolution decisions
    - Permission decisions
    - Recovery decisions
    - Configuration-scope decisions
    - Fingerprint-mismatch decisions
    - Work-lifecycle policy decisions

Do not merge these into one generic prompt model.

Each decision type has different authority, persistence, trace, and policy
scope implications.


PRESERVATION RULE 3: PERMISSION DECISIONS ARE NOT READ PROMPTS
==============================================================

Preserve the read-access rule.

Permission decisions apply only to actions that:

    - mutate persisted state
    - delete persisted state
    - intentionally cross ownership boundaries

Permission decisions are not raised for:

    - computed-only effects
    - runtime-only transforms
    - observation or watching of values

Read access is excluded from permission decisions:

    - reads either succeed or return NotFoundOrPermissionDenied
    - user interaction is never used for reads
    - prompting, confirmation, and policy do not apply to reads

This preserves determinism, prevents probing, and avoids decision spam.

Do not add "ask user for read access" behavior unless a later source document
explicitly redefines this security model.


PRESERVATION RULE 4: REMEMBERED CAPABILITY PERMISSION KEYS ARE EXACT
====================================================================

Preserve exact capability permission keying.

For capability-based permission decisions, remembered permission identity uses:

    - packCanonicalId
    - exact versioned capability id

A capability permission for:

    storage.export@1

is remembered against:

    storage.export@1

not against:

    storage.export
    storage
    wildcard family
    prefix family
    versionless family

DA-17 must not introduce:

    - wildcard capability permission matching
    - prefix capability permission matching
    - broader family matching
    - versionless fallback lookup

The pack version may be comparison metadata.

The pack version is not part of the permission policy identity key.


PRESERVATION RULE 5: FINGERPRINT POLICY KEYS ARE EXACT MISMATCH PAIRS
=====================================================================

Preserve exact fingerprint-mismatch keying.

Remembered fingerprint-mismatch policy is keyed by:

    - canonicalPackId
    - packVersion
    - expectedFingerprint
    - actualFingerprint

expectedFingerprint and actualFingerprint are full fingerprint strings.

The fingerprint algorithm/version is part of the full fingerprint string.

Fingerprint-mismatch policy is specific to the exact mismatch pair.

Remembering one fingerprint mismatch does not bless all future mismatches for
the same pack or version.

For-this-run fingerprint acceptance does not update persisted policy and does
not update an expected fingerprint.

Remembering a fingerprint mismatch records acceptance of the specific mismatch
under the selected policy scope.

Origin alone is not authenticity proof.


PRESERVATION RULE 6: POLICY SCOPE PRECEDENCE IS RUN, SAVE, GLOBAL
=================================================================

Preserve policy evaluation order:

    1) run policy
    2) save policy
    3) global policy

Run policy overrides save policy.

Save policy overrides global policy.

Run policy is memory-only and ends with the current Turnix run.

Save policy is persisted inside save-bound policy storage defined by DA-06.

Global policy is persisted inside global policy storage defined by DA-06.

Silent or implicit policy creation is forbidden.

Persisted policies must remain:

    - explicit
    - inspectable
    - editable through Turnix management surfaces


PRESERVATION RULE 7: POLICY DOES NOT REDEFINE AUTHORITY
=======================================================

Preserve the rule:

    Policies influence default responses.

    Policies do not redefine authority.

Policies do not:

    - override ownership rules
    - bypass access levels
    - grant mutation rights forbidden by system rules
    - convert Job terminal outcomes into ordinary failures
    - treat transport timeout as Job timeout
    - treat Job timeout as transport timeout
    - silently create work
    - silently cancel work
    - silently supersede work
    - silently detach work
    - silently retain result
    - silently discard result
    - silently finalize a Window
    - silently mutate committed state
    - silently authorize adapter execution
    - silently accept RuntimeHost command authority
    - silently grant PackManager write or repair authority

Policy exists to reduce friction, not weaken safety or determinism.


PRESERVATION RULE 8: PAUSES ARE SCOPED AND DO NOT IMPLY DOMAIN OUTCOMES
======================================================================

Preserve scoped pause behavior.

When a decision is required:

    - the triggering execution unit pauses
    - unrelated execution may continue
    - frontends are notified through a Kernel-mediated event
    - execution resumes only after decision handling completes

Rules:

    - no busy waiting
    - no polling
    - no global freeze

While a decision is pending:

    - no persisted mutation may be committed
    - no configuration write may be finalized
    - no SavePack mutation may complete
    - no Job or OrchestrationUnit affected by the decision may silently cross
      the decision-controlled boundary
    - no Window finalization affected by the decision may silently occur
    - no RuntimeHost operation affected by the decision may silently complete
    - no adapter execution authorization affected by the decision may silently
      proceed

A decision pause does not by itself cancel, supersede, or time out a Job.

A decision pause does not by itself complete, fail, cancel, or supersede an
OrchestrationUnit.

A decision pause does not by itself finalize a Window.

Lifecycle changes caused by decision timeout or policy are owned by the
affected domain documents.


PRESERVATION RULE 9: WORK-LIFECYCLE POLICY IS DISTINCT FROM JOB LIFECYCLE
=========================================================================

Preserve the work-lifecycle boundary.

Work-lifecycle policy decisions may affect:

    - Jobs
    - OrchestrationUnits
    - RPC request handling
    - scheduler-owned work
    - debugger-controlled work
    - adapter-mediated work
    - RuntimeHost operations
    - AppInstance startup or shutdown work
    - PackManager-triggered operations
    - Window-related finalization workflows

Possible outcomes include:

    - continue work
    - cancel work
    - supersede work
    - detach work from the transport or frontend that caused it
    - retain result for later retrieval
    - discard result when no consumer remains
    - refuse unsafe continuation
    - abort startup or affected work

Work-lifecycle policy decisions do not define Job terminal states.

DA-19 defines Job lifecycle states, terminal outcomes, cancellation, timeout,
supersession, progress, and late completion semantics.

Work-lifecycle policy decisions do not define RPC transport mechanics.

DA-21 defines RPC, RPCChannel, transport, timeout, disconnect, cancellation
message, and acknowledgement semantics.

Work-lifecycle policy decisions do not define capability permission semantics.

A remembered work-lifecycle policy does not imply a remembered capability
permission decision.

A remembered capability permission decision does not imply a remembered
work-lifecycle policy.


PRESERVATION RULE 10: WORK-LIFECYCLE POLICIES ARE NOT KEYED BY JOBID ALONE
==========================================================================

Preserve the rule:

    A remembered work-lifecycle policy is not keyed by jobId alone.

jobId identifies one Job lifecycle.

A remembered work-lifecycle policy governs future matching decisions, not only
the original Job instance.

Remembered work-lifecycle policy subject identity may include:

    - work kind
    - owner domain
    - capability identity when capability work is involved
    - RPC route or request class when RPC work is involved
    - scheduler policy class when scheduler work is involved
    - adapter kind or adapter execution class when adapter-mediated work is
      involved
    - RuntimeHost operation class when host work is involved
    - AppInstance lifecycle operation class when AppInstance work is involved
    - disconnect condition when disconnect handling is involved
    - supersession target class when supersession handling is involved

A remembered policy that applies to cancellation does not imply a remembered
policy that applies to supersession, timeout, disconnect, detachment, result
retention, result discard, adapter failure, RuntimeHost failure, or Window
finalization.

A remembered policy that applies to transport disconnect does not imply a
remembered policy that applies to Job timeout.


PRESERVATION RULE 11: FRONTENDS PRESENT, KERNEL DECIDES
=======================================================

Preserve frontend responsibility.

Frontends:

    - present clear explanation
    - show consequences
    - indicate scope of effect
    - offer only valid choices

Frontends do not:

    - invent defaults
    - auto-confirm without policy
    - bypass Kernel authority
    - obscure the impact of denial
    - turn presentation state into policy state

For fingerprint-mismatch decisions, frontends may show origin context, but must
not present origin as authenticity proof.

For work-lifecycle policy decisions, frontends distinguish:

    - continuing work
    - cancelling work
    - superseding work
    - detaching work from current frontend or transport
    - retaining result for later retrieval
    - discarding result when no consumer remains
    - refusing unsafe continuation
    - aborting startup or affected work

Frontends do not present Superseded as failure.

Frontends do not present transport timeout as Job timeout.

Frontends do not present Job timeout as transport timeout.

If multiple frontends are connected:

    - the decision is shown on all connected frontends
    - the first valid response wins
    - all other pending presentations are dismissed

The Kernel remains the single arbiter.


PRESERVATION RULE 12: MODS AND PACKS DO NOT HANDLE DECISIONS DIRECTLY
=====================================================================

Preserve mod and pack involvement boundary.

Mods and packs may trigger decision points.

Mods and packs do not handle decision points directly.

Mods and packs do not block while awaiting frontend interaction.

Mods and packs do not invent policy defaults.

Mods and packs do not persist policy decisions directly.

Mods and packs observe decision outcomes only through Turnix-provided context
and results.


PRESERVATION RULE 13: DECISION FAILURE IS SAFE AND NON-MUTATING
===============================================================

Preserve failure behavior.

If a frontend disconnects during a decision:

    - affected execution remains paused
    - timeout policy may apply
    - user may reconnect through another valid frontend

If the decision handler fails:

    - execution aborts safely
    - failure is trace-logged
    - persisted state remains unchanged

If a frontend or transport disconnects while a Job exists, policy decides
whether the affected Job:

    - continues detached
    - is cancelled
    - is superseded
    - retains result for later retrieval
    - discards result if no consumer remains

Disconnect handling does not silently cancel, supersede, detach, retain, or
discard a Job without explicit policy.

Disconnect handling does not silently define adapter, RuntimeHost,
OrchestrationUnit, AppInstance, Window, PackManager, or committed-state outcome.


WATCH ITEM: WORK-LIFECYCLE POLICY KEY SCHEMA
============================================

DA-17 leaves the exact work-lifecycle policy-key schema open.

Future rewrite should preserve the current boundary:

    - not keyed by jobId alone
    - policy subjects are explicit
    - cancellation, supersession, timeout, and disconnect policies remain
      distinct unless an owning document explicitly defines a safe shared key
    - transport disconnect policy is not Job timeout policy
    - Job timeout policy is not transport disconnect policy
    - adapter failure policy is not transport disconnect policy by default
    - RuntimeHost operation policy is not adapter lifecycle policy by default
    - AppInstance lifecycle policy is not Job lifecycle policy by default
    - Window finalization policy is not Job terminal outcome policy by default

This matters for DA-19, DA-20, DA-21, DA-23, DA-24, DA-27, DA-28, DA-29,
DA-18, IS-01, IS-02, schedulers, and capability-backed work.


WATCH ITEM: CROSS-SCOPE COPY AND PROMOTION
==========================================

DA-17 leaves cross-scope copy and promotion open.

Future rewrite should preserve these constraints:

    - computed-only or derived values are rejected cleanly
    - owner-domain boundaries may impose additional permission checks
    - overwrite and conflict behavior must be explicit
    - source-layer removal vs preservation must be explicit
    - batching must define atomicity
    - trace shape must distinguish copy, promotion, overwrite, refusal, discard,
      and conflict outcomes
    - policy remembering for copy/promotion must not silently broaden authority

Do not introduce implicit promotion or hidden cross-scope copying.


WATCH ITEM: TRACE WORDING
=========================

Follow the DA-03 cross-document trace wording check.

Decision trace requirements are decision-domain emission obligations.

DA-03 owns trace substrate, retention, and loss semantics.

User-facing explanations are derived from trace data.

User-facing surfaces do not invent rationale.

Do not let trace wording imply that trace evidence is committed state or that
trace persistence is guaranteed forever.

Use these as guardrails only.

Decision and policy guardrails:

    Policy does not redefine authority.

    Policy does not define committed-state truth.

    Policy does not directly define Job terminal state.

    Policy does not directly define OrchestrationUnit terminal outcome.

    Policy does not directly define RPC or transport outcome.

    Policy does not directly define adapter lifecycle outcome.

    Policy does not directly define RuntimeHost lifecycle or operation outcome.

    Policy does not directly define AppInstance lifecycle outcome.

    Policy does not directly define Window finalization.

    Policy does not directly define PackManager operation outcome.

    Remembered decisions must not silently broaden across capability families,
    versions, packs, SavePacks, RuntimeHosts, AppInstances, owners, work kinds,
    adapters, RPC routes, or transports.

    Pack-provided hints are not binding policy.

    Mods and packs do not own decision handling.

    Frontends do not invent defaults or auto-confirm without policy.

    Reads are not prompted.

    Wildcard capability permission matching is forbidden.

    Versionless capability permission fallback is forbidden.

    Origin is not authenticity proof.

    Fingerprint acceptance is not general trust.

    Transport disconnect is not Job timeout.

    Job timeout is not transport timeout.

    Superseded is not failure.

    Work is not silently cancelled, superseded, detached, retained, or discarded
    without policy.

    Pending decisions do not commit persisted mutation across the

Owning-document guardrails:

Local rewrite rule:

    DA-17 may name these concepts when needed to protect decision and policy
    handling boundaries.

    DA-17 should not define their detailed behavior.

    DA-17 should remain the Kernel-mediated user-decision, policy precedence,
    policy persistence, remembered permission, fingerprint mismatch,
    work-lifecycle policy, frontend-presentation, and decision-domain trace

CURRENT FINAL RECOMMENDATION
============================

Keep DA-17.

No heavy rewrite is recommended.

Recommended future edits:

    - keep DA-17 focused on Kernel-mediated decision and policy handling
    - preserve exact remembered permission and fingerprint policy keys
    - preserve work-lifecycle policy separation from Job lifecycle, RPC transport,
      OrchestrationUnit lifecycle, adapter lifecycle, RuntimeHost lifecycle,
      AppInstance lifecycle, Window finalization, PackManager operation, and
      committed-state semantics
    - preserve no-prompt-for-read-access rule
    - preserve frontend-as-presentation-only rule
    - preserve mod-trigger-but-not-handle rule
    - define work-lifecycle policy-key schema later in the owning workflow or
      implementation specification
    - define cross-scope copy/promotion later without implicit authority
      broadening
