===============================================================================
DESIGN AREA DA-29 - PACK MANAGEMENT, CATALOG OPERATIONS AND GRAPH PREPARATION
===============================================================================

Status:
-------
EVOLVING

Description:
------------
Defines PackManager as the official Turnix pack-management and graph-
preparation domain, defines pack catalog operation semantics, separates
PackManager tooling from discovery, resolution, importing, activation,
capability authority, and mechanical loading, and defines how advisory pack
relations, hard dependencies, user policy, SaveBundle graph evidence, retained
Pack material, selected graph intent, and activation plans are handled before
activation.

This document owns PackManager semantic boundaries. It consumes root
Discovery, Pack identity, generic resolution, importing, dependency,
activation, service, capability, permission, policy, RuntimeHost, Application,
ApplicationRun, SaveBundle, ModCallContext, View, and adapter semantics defined
by their owning design areas.

Purpose:
--------
This document defines how Turnix inspects, presents, prepares, modifies, and
explains pack catalogs, selected pack graphs, and activation plans without
allowing helpful tooling to become hidden loader, resolver, activation, import,
persistence, policy, user-decision, lifecycle, or capability authority.

It defines:
- PackManager as an official Turnix management and graph-preparation domain
- pack catalog operation meaning
- ListPacks as the general Pack-listing primitive
- AppPack, ViewPack, ContentPack, and ModPack listing as filters over Pack kind
- SaveBundle-related listing as a separate persistence-aware catalog operation
  over durable Application persistence closures and retained graph evidence
- PackManager scope across RuntimeHost, Workspace, AppPack, AppDefinition,
  SaveBundle, Application, ApplicationRun, tooling, and test contexts
- PackManager relationship to roots, Discovery, visibility, generic
  resolution, dependencies, advisory pack relations, imports, activation,
  capabilities, decisions, persistence, RuntimeHost, Application,
  ApplicationRun, SaveBundle, Views, and adapters
- selected pack graph and activation-plan preparation meaning
- activation-plan information boundaries
- cyclic activation group detection and admission boundaries
- mechanical loader separation from graph preparation
- advisory conflict, suggestion, and compatibility presentation semantics
- hard dependency handling boundary
- user and policy override boundary
- expected-break semantics for explicitly accepted risky graphs
- explicit graph-repair semantics
- PackManager view and command/capability access boundaries
- PackManager trace reason ownership
- explicitly unsupported PackManager bypass behavior

It does NOT define:
- root discovery boundaries or filesystem scanning rules (see DA-09)
- Pack identity, hierarchy, or visibility semantics (see DA-10)
- generic PackRequest resolution mechanics (see DA-11)
- importing, module loading, or code-boundary rules (see DA-12)
- hard dependency, provider-role, pinned, copied, or advisory-relation field
  semantics (see DA-13)
- activation lifecycle semantics (see DA-14)
- ModCallContext gateway semantics (see DA-30)
- visibility/export access-control mechanics beyond DA-10 and DA-15
- service, capability, permission, or Job-backed capability semantics
  (see DA-16)
- user decision and remembered policy semantics (see DA-17)
- Job lifecycle, timeout, cancellation, progress, or result semantics
  (see DA-19)
- Application or ApplicationRun lifecycle or runtime identity semantics
  (see DA-20)
- RPC, channel, or transport semantics (see DA-21)
- UI, View, or frontend runtime semantics (see DA-22)
- execution adapter boundary or adapter lifecycle semantics (see DA-23 and
  DA-27)
- RuntimeHost lifecycle, startup mode, command surface, or Workspace ownership
  semantics (see DA-28)
- SaveBundle persistence-carrier semantics beyond PackManager graph evidence
- concrete pack catalog result envelopes
- concrete SaveBundle catalog result envelopes
- concrete resolved graph schema
- concrete activation-plan schema
- concrete activation executor implementation
- concrete mechanical loader implementation
- concrete PackManager UI layout
- concrete internet search, registry, package-index, install, update, or
  delete protocols
- concrete command syntax or RPC envelope fields

01. INTERPRETATION BASIS
===============================================================================

01.01. This document expresses normative meaning through explicit,
      deterministic prose.

01.02. Normative meaning in Turnix derives from sentence structure and
      document context rather than from RFC-style keywords.

01.03. The CORE META-RULES documents define the general rule language and
      interpretation model used by this document.

01.04. This document defines PackManager, pack catalog, SaveBundle-related
      catalog, selected graph, and graph-preparation semantics within that
      shared interpretation model.

01.05. This document uses canonical architecture type names for:
      - Application
      - ApplicationRun
      - SaveBundle
      - ModCallContext

01.06. Terms not defined in this document do not act as aliases unless their
      owning documents define that alias explicitly.

01.07. "Activation plan" means selected graph intent refined for activation
      order and activation readiness.

01.08. "Activation plan" does not mean activation itself.

01.09. "Activation plan" does not mean mechanical loading.

01.10. "Mechanical loader" means the component or adapter-facing mechanism
       that loads or evaluates what it is explicitly instructed to load.

01.11. Mechanical loading does not imply graph preparation, dependency
       repair, cyclic activation admission, or final Activated state.


===============================================================================
02. CORE CONCEPT
===============================================================================

02.01. Pack management answers:

          "How does Turnix help users and tools inspect, assemble, repair,
           update, and explain pack graphs without hiding resolution,
           activation, import, capability, policy, persistence, or loader
           authority behind convenience?"

02.02. PackManager is the official Turnix pack-management and graph-
      preparation domain.

02.03. PackManager prepares, explains, validates, repairs, and manages
      selected pack graph intent.

02.04. PackManager may prepare activation plans from selected pack graph
      intent.

02.05. PackManager is helpful tooling and a management authority surface.

02.06. PackManager is not Kernel authority by itself.

02.07. PackManager is not Discovery.

02.08. PackManager is not generic resolution.

02.09. PackManager is not importing.

02.10. PackManager is not activation.

02.11. PackManager is not an execution adapter.

02.12. PackManager is not a mechanical loader.

02.13. PackManager is not an activation executor.

02.14. PackManager is not capability permission.

02.15. PackManager is not policy authority by itself.

02.16. PackManager is not user-decision authority by itself.

02.17. PackManager is not persistence authority by itself.

02.18. PackManager is not Application lifecycle authority.

02.19. PackManager is not ApplicationRun lifecycle authority.

02.20. PackManager is not SaveBundle authority by itself.

02.21. PackManager is not ModCallContext gateway authority.

02.22. PackManager is not a View by itself.

02.23. PackManager may expose Views, command surfaces, capability surfaces,
       RPC operations, and test surfaces that present or request PackManager
       operations.

02.24. Those surfaces do not become authority merely because they can present
       or request PackManager behavior.

02.25. PackManager helps choose and explain what Turnix will try.

02.26. Turnix still performs only the accepted graph, accepted activation
       plan, policy, and operation requested through Kernel-mediated
       boundaries.

02.27. PackManager repair means explicit, scoped, policy- or user-accepted
       graph-preparation repair.

02.28. PackManager repair does not mean automatic hidden correction, Kernel
       authority, Discovery, import, activation, mechanical loading,
       capability permission, persistence authority, policy authority,
       user-decision authority, durable Application authority, ApplicationRun
       authority, RuntimeHost authority, View authority, or adapter execution
       authority.

02.29. Later sections refine and apply these rules.

02.30. Later sections do not weaken these rules.


===============================================================================
03. PACKMANAGER IDENTITY AND ROLE
===============================================================================

03.01. PackManager is a Turnix-recognized management domain for pack catalogs,
      pack sources, selected graphs, activation-plan preparation,
      compatibility evidence, advisory relations, retained graph evidence,
      and graph-preparation workflows.

03.02. PackManager may be implemented by first-party Turnix tooling.

03.03. PackManager may have multiple entry surfaces.

03.04. PackManager entry surfaces may include:
      - RuntimeHost command surface
      - Launcher View
      - dedicated PackManager View
      - AI assistant or buddy View
      - developer tool View
      - application-embedded View
      - CLI surface
      - LAN or remote client surface
      - test surface

03.05. A PackManager View is a presentation and interaction surface.

03.06. A PackManager View is not PackManager authority by itself.

03.07. A Pack or View displaying PackManager UI does not own PackManager
      authority merely because it displays that UI.

03.08. A game, application, launcher, or tool may request PackManager behavior
      only through Turnix-mediated command, capability, RPC, or equivalent
      Kernel-owned surfaces.

03.09. PackManager operations may exist before any Application exists.

03.10. PackManager operations may also be requested from an ApplicationRun
       when policy, capability, RuntimeHost, and authority boundaries permit
       that request.

03.11. PackManager is official Turnix tooling, not third-party hidden runtime
       behavior.

03.12. Official tooling status does not permit PackManager to bypass Kernel,
       persistence, policy, capability, resolution, activation, adapter, or
       trace boundaries.


===============================================================================
04. PACK CATALOG OPERATIONS
===============================================================================

04.01. A pack catalog is the Turnix-visible population of discovered and
      indexed Pack descriptors available to a catalog operation under an
      explicit catalog context.

04.02. The pack catalog is derived from Discovery and indexing.

04.03. The pack catalog does not perform Discovery by being read.

04.04. Catalog operations do not lazily rescan the filesystem unless a
      separate explicit rescan operation is requested and accepted.

04.05. ListPacks is the general Pack-listing primitive.

04.06. ListPacks may be filtered by Pack kind.

04.07. AppPack listing is ListPacks filtered to AppPack kind.

04.08. ViewPack listing is ListPacks filtered to ViewPack kind.

04.09. ContentPack listing is ListPacks filtered to ContentPack kind.

04.10. ModPack listing is ListPacks filtered to ModPack kind.

04.11. SaveBundle listing is not ListPacks filtered by Pack kind.

04.12. SaveBundle-related listing is a separate persistence-aware catalog
       operation over durable Application persistence closures and their
       retained Pack graph evidence.

04.13. SaveBundle-related listing may expose:
       - SaveBundle identity when available
       - Application identity when available
       - retained selected graph evidence
       - last-used graph evidence
       - last-known-good graph evidence
       - copied Pack material
       - pinned Pack bindings
       - embedded or retained Pack material
       - SaveBundle compatibility evidence
       - repair evidence when available

04.14. SaveBundle itself is not a Pack.

04.15. Pack listing remains about Packs.

04.16. SaveBundle listing remains about durable Application persistence
       closures.

04.17. ListPacks may also be filtered by:
       - root or root class
       - Workspace
       - active ApplicationRun context
       - visibility context
       - AppPack compatibility context
       - SaveBundle compatibility context
       - search text
       - author
       - version or version range
       - installation source
       - trust or source classification
       - enabled, disabled, or selected graph state

04.18. A ListPacks result means the listed Pack descriptors are visible to
       that catalog operation under the supplied catalog context.

04.19. A listed Pack is not necessarily selected for a graph.

04.20. A listed Pack is not necessarily a dependency outcome.

04.21. A listed Pack is not necessarily activated.

04.22. A listed Pack is not necessarily importable.

04.23. A listed Pack does not grant capability permission.

04.24. Catalog presentation preserves the distinction between existence,
       visibility, resolution eligibility, dependency satisfaction, selected
       graph membership, activation state, and capability availability.


===============================================================================
05. PACKMANAGER SCOPES
===============================================================================

05.01. PackManager operations occur inside an explicit management scope.

05.02. PackManager scopes may include:
      - RuntimeHost scope
      - Workspace scope
      - AppPack scope
      - AppDefinition scope
      - SaveBundle scope
      - Application scope
      - ApplicationRun scope
      - tooling scope
      - test scope

05.03. RuntimeHost scope concerns pack catalogs, roots, installed Packs,
      package sources, and selected graph operations available to the running
      RuntimeHost.

05.04. Workspace scope concerns pack catalogs, configuration, source state, and
      installed or available Pack material associated with one Workspace.

05.05. AppPack scope concerns graph preparation, dependency checks,
      activation-plan preparation, advisory-relation presentation, and
      compatibility for one AppPack.

05.06. AppDefinition scope concerns graph preparation for an accepted
      Application definition source that is not necessarily a discovered
      AppPack catalog entry.

05.07. SaveBundle scope concerns retained graph evidence, copied Pack material,
      pinned Pack bindings, retained Pack material, compatibility checks,
      repair, export, and import for one SaveBundle.

05.08. Application scope concerns durable Application identity, saved graph
      lineage, migrations, and SaveBundle-bound graph planning.

05.09. ApplicationRun scope concerns active graph inspection, active activation
      evidence, pending graph changes, and operations that may affect running
      Pack material.

05.10. Tooling scope concerns development, review, diagnostics, authoring,
       package-index work, test behavior, or maintenance behavior not
       directly owned by one ApplicationRun.

05.11. Test scope does not weaken runtime authority boundaries.

05.12. Scope does not grant authority by itself.

05.13. Each operation remains constrained by the owning authority documents for
       the resources it touches.


===============================================================================
06. RELATION TO ROOTS AND DISCOVERY
===============================================================================

06.01. Root and Discovery semantics are defined by DA-09.

06.02. PackManager consumes discovered and indexed Pack descriptors.

06.03. PackManager does not redefine root classes.

06.04. PackManager does not redefine content-root, userdata-root, or saves-root
      meaning.

06.05. PackManager may request explicit rescan, install, update, remove,
      repair, or source-refresh operations when those operations are defined
      and authorized.

06.06. Those operations remain subject to root, persistence, policy, and
      authority boundaries.

06.07. PackManager does not silently write into first-party, third-party,
      custom, userdata, or saves roots.

06.08. PackManager may be the authorized tool surface that performs pack
      installation, update, deletion, or replacement when the relevant root
      and policy rules permit that operation.

06.09. PackManager installation authority does not make runtime Pack code a
      root writer.

06.10. PackManager root modifications are trace-visible.


===============================================================================
07. RELATION TO PACK IDENTITY AND VISIBILITY
===============================================================================

07.01. Pack identity, hierarchy, Pack kind, and visibility semantics are
      defined by DA-10.

07.02. PackManager does not redefine Pack identity.

07.03. PackManager does not redefine Pack kind.

07.04. PackManager does not redefine visibility.

07.05. PackManager may present visibility graphs, hidden Packs, shadowed Packs,
      SaveBundle-retained Packs, and visibility failures when the operation
      and policy permit that presentation.

07.06. Visibility presentation does not grant visibility.

07.07. Visibility does not grant capability authority.

07.08. PackManager does not use visibility as capability authority.

07.09. PackManager may explain that a Pack exists but is not visible from a
      requested context.

07.10. PackManager may help users or tooling choose a graph that respects
       visibility constraints.

07.11. PackManager does not silently bypass visibility constraints to make a
       graph work.


===============================================================================
08. RELATION TO GENERIC RESOLUTION
===============================================================================

08.01. Generic resolution semantics are defined by DA-11.

08.02. PackManager consumes generic resolution outcomes.

08.03. PackManager may call generic resolution with an explicit resolution
      context.

08.04. PackManager does not redefine PackRequest grammar.

08.05. PackManager does not merge manifests.

08.06. PackManager does not turn resolution into Discovery.

08.07. PackManager does not silently switch resolution contexts.

08.08. PackManager may present resolution candidates, hard rejections, soft
      constraints, ambiguities, selected candidates, and resolution failures.

08.09. PackManager may ask user or policy to resolve ambiguity when the owning
      resolution consumer permits that path.

08.10. PackManager may persist accepted ambiguity decisions only through the
       surrounding policy or consumer authority that owns such persistence.

08.11. PackManager does not persist resolution decisions by hidden side
       effect.


===============================================================================
09. RELATION TO DEPENDENCIES AND ADVISORY PACK RELATIONS
===============================================================================

09.01. Hard dependency and advisory pack-relation semantics are defined by
      DA-13.

09.02. PackManager consumes hard dependency declarations.

09.03. PackManager consumes provider-role metadata.

09.04. PackManager consumes advisory pack relations.

09.05. PackManager may present:
      - missing hard dependencies
      - unsatisfied version requirements
      - copied Pack dependencies in a SaveBundle
      - pinned Pack dependencies in a SaveBundle
      - provider-role availability
      - supportedPacks compatibility evidence
      - conflictingPacks warnings
      - recommendedPacks suggestions

09.06. PackManager does not convert advisory pack relations into hard
      dependencies.

09.07. PackManager does not convert advisory pack relations into hard
      activation blockers by itself.

09.08. PackManager does not convert advisory pack relations into loader rules
      by itself.

09.09. PackManager may let policy or user decision turn an advisory warning
      into a graph-selection decision for a specific selected graph.

09.10. Such a graph-selection decision is explicit, scoped, trace-visible, and
       does not modify the manifest advisory relation.

09.11. Hard dependency failure remains hard failure unless an accepted policy
       or user decision explicitly resolves it under DA-13 and DA-17.

09.12. Advisory conflict override is not the same as hard dependency
       satisfaction.

09.13. PackManager may explain both categories, but it does not collapse them.

09.14. A hard dependency edge may contribute to activation ordering.

09.15. A hard dependency edge may contribute to strongly connected component
       detection.

09.16. Strongly connected component detection does not weaken hard dependency
       semantics.

09.17. Strongly connected component detection does not prove that a dependency
       cycle is activatable.

09.18. Cyclic activation group admission remains distinct from hard dependency
       declaration.


===============================================================================
10. SELECTED PACK GRAPH
===============================================================================

10.01. A selected pack graph is the explicit graph intent that Turnix will use
      for later runtime or tooling operations.

10.02. A selected pack graph may include:
      - graph identity when defined
      - graph purpose
      - AppPack identity when applicable
      - AppDefinition identity when applicable
      - Application identity when applicable
      - ApplicationRun identity when applicable
      - SaveBundle identity when applicable
      - selected Pack identities
      - selected versions
      - selected source locations or content identities when applicable
      - dependency edges
      - provider-role choices when applicable
      - advisory warnings
      - accepted overrides
      - policy decisions
      - user decisions
      - compatibility evidence
      - SaveBundle-retained Pack references
      - copied Pack references
      - pinned Pack references
      - origin or provenance evidence when available

10.03. A selected pack graph is information about intended graph membership
      and graph relationships.

10.04. A selected pack graph is not activation.

10.05. A selected pack graph is not importing.

10.06. A selected pack graph is not adapter artifact loading.

10.07. A selected pack graph is not ModCallContext gateway authority.

10.08. PackManager may prepare, present, repair, and retain selected pack graph
      intent where the operation and policy permit that behavior.

10.09. PackManager does not execute Pack code by preparing a selected pack
      graph.

10.10. PackManager does not import Pack code by preparing a selected pack
       graph.

10.11. PackManager does not activate Packs by preparing a selected pack graph.

10.12. PackManager does not grant capability permission by preparing a
       selected pack graph.

10.13. PackManager does not grant execution authorization by preparing a
       selected pack graph.

10.14. A selected pack graph may still be invalid for activation.

10.15. A selected pack graph may still fail during activation.

10.16. Selected graph failure is classified according to the owning domain
       where the failure occurs.


===============================================================================
11. ACTIVATION PLAN
===============================================================================

11.01. An activation plan is selected graph intent refined for activation order
      and activation readiness.

11.02. PackManager may prepare an activation plan.

11.03. PackManager owns activation-plan preparation meaning.

11.04. DA-14 owns activation lifecycle semantics.

11.05. DA-14 owns the final Activated state boundary.

11.06. DA-23 and DA-27 own adapter boundary and adapter lifecycle semantics.

11.07. An activation plan may include:
      - accepted selected graph identity when defined
      - graph purpose
      - AppPack identity when applicable
      - AppDefinition identity when applicable
      - Application identity when applicable
      - ApplicationRun identity when applicable
      - SaveBundle identity when applicable
      - selected Pack identities
      - selected versions
      - dependency edges
      - activation components
      - strongly connected components when detected
      - cyclic activation group candidates
      - admitted cyclic activation groups
      - rejected cyclic activation groups
      - external dependency order
      - activation readiness diagnostics
      - adapter participation evidence when available
      - advisory warnings relevant to activation
      - accepted graph overrides
      - policy decisions
      - user decisions
      - expected-break evidence when risk was accepted
      - provenance evidence when available

11.08. An activation plan is information.

11.09. An activation plan is graph intent.

11.10. An activation plan is ordering evidence.

11.11. An activation plan is not activation.

11.12. An activation plan is not a loader.

11.13. An activation plan is not adapter execution.

11.14. An activation plan is not module import.

11.15. An activation plan is not ModCallContext gateway authority.

11.16. An activation plan is not capability permission.

11.17. An activation plan is not proof that activation will succeed.

11.18. Activation consumes an accepted activation plan through DA-14 and
       related execution-boundary documents.

11.19. Activation may fail after consuming an accepted activation plan.

11.20. Such failure is activation evidence, not proof that PackManager owned
       activation.

11.21. PackManager may contribute diagnostic context to explain activation
       failure where graph evidence is relevant.

11.22. PackManager diagnostics do not replace activation trace evidence.

11.23. PackManager warnings do not become activation outcomes.

11.24. An activation plan can make loading predictable without making the
       loader responsible for dependency order.

11.25. An activation plan can expose missing dependencies without repairing
       them by itself.

11.26. An activation plan can expose conflicts without resolving them by
       itself.

11.27. An activation plan can record accepted risky graph intent without
       making that graph safe.

11.28. An activation plan can identify expected-break evidence without
       converting failure into success.


===============================================================================
12. CYCLIC ACTIVATION GROUP PREPARATION
===============================================================================

12.01. Dependency graph preparation may detect strongly connected components.

12.02. A strongly connected component is a graph-preparation fact.

12.03. A strongly connected component is not cyclic activation by itself.

12.04. A cyclic activation group is a strongly connected component admitted by
      the accepted activation plan for cyclic activation handling under DA-14.

12.05. PackManager may identify cyclic activation group candidates.

12.06. PackManager may present cyclic activation group candidates to a policy
      or user-decision surface where decision handling is required.

12.07. PackManager may record that a cyclic activation group candidate was
      admitted.

12.08. PackManager may record that a cyclic activation group candidate was
      rejected.

12.09. Cyclic activation group admission does not activate Packs.

12.10. Cyclic activation group admission does not call onLoad(ctx).

12.11. Cyclic activation group admission does not grant ModCallContext gateway
       authority.

12.12. Cyclic activation group admission does not prove that the group can
       successfully activate.

12.13. Cyclic activation group admission means that activation may attempt
       DA-14 cyclic activation behavior for that group.

12.14. DA-14 owns Phase 1, Phase 2, Phase 3, phase barriers, cyclic surface
       readiness, and final cyclic-group activation semantics.

12.15. PackManager does not define lifecycle hook behavior.

12.16. PackManager does not define lifecycle callback order.

12.17. PackManager does not define cyclic Phase 3 execution behavior.

12.18. PackManager does not retry cyclic activation in alternate orders.

12.19. PackManager does not convert a dependency cycle into an acyclic order
       by naming it cyclic.

12.20. PackManager preserves the distinction between:
       - cycle detection
       - cyclic activation group candidate
       - cyclic activation group admission
       - DA-14 cyclic activation execution
       - DA-14 cyclic activation success or failure


===============================================================================
13. MECHANICAL LOADING BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

13.01. Mechanical loading means loading or evaluating a requested artifact,
      module, or activation entry through the applicable loader or adapter
      path.

13.02. Mechanical loading semantics are governed by DA-12, DA-14, DA-23, and
      DA-27.

13.03. PackManager is not a mechanical loader.

13.04. A mechanical loader is not PackManager.

13.05. A mechanical loader does not prepare selected pack graphs by itself.

13.06. A mechanical loader does not prepare activation plans by itself.

13.07. A mechanical loader does not resolve graph ambiguity by itself.

13.08. A mechanical loader does not repair missing dependencies by itself.

13.09. A mechanical loader does not select replacement Packs by itself.

13.10. A mechanical loader does not convert advisory relations into loader
       rules by itself.

13.11. A mechanical loader does not decide cyclic activation admission by
       itself.

13.12. A mechanical loader does not own activation order.

13.13. A mechanical loader loads what Turnix instructs it to load under the
       applicable authority boundary.

13.14. Loading material in an order not supported by the accepted activation
       plan may fail.

13.15. Such failure is valid boundary behavior.

13.16. Such failure is not hidden graph repair.

13.17. Such failure is not PackManager silently changing the graph.

13.18. Such failure is not proof that the loader owns graph preparation.

13.19. PackManager prepares graph and activation-plan information so later
       loading and activation can be requested predictably.

13.20. Activation and adapter execution still own their own runtime failures.


===============================================================================
14. GRAPH REPAIR AND RISKY GRAPH SEMANTICS
===============================================================================

14.01. PackManager repair is an explicit graph-preparation operation.

14.02. PackManager repair operates inside a declared scope.

14.03. PackManager repair requires policy or user acceptance when it changes
      selected graph intent, retained graph evidence, copied Pack material,
      pinned Pack bindings, package sources, or persisted configuration.

14.04. PackManager repair may suggest graph changes, replacement Packs,
      version changes, source changes, copied Pack updates, pinned binding
      changes, or compatibility decisions when the owning documents permit
      those operations.

14.05. PackManager repair does not silently correct graphs.

14.06. PackManager repair does not silently modify Pack manifests.

14.07. PackManager repair does not silently mutate SaveBundle graph evidence.

14.08. PackManager repair does not silently update running Pack material.

14.09. PackManager repair does not make a graph safe by itself.

14.10. PackManager repair does not satisfy hard dependencies unless the
       accepted repair selects an allowed dependency-resolution path.

14.11. PackManager may detect graphs with warnings, advisory conflicts,
       unsupported combinations, missing optional material, soft constraints,
       or other risk evidence.

14.12. PackManager may present those risks to the user or policy surface.

14.13. User or policy may explicitly accept a risky graph when the governing
       documents permit that choice.

14.14. An accepted risky graph remains trace-visible.

14.15. Risk acceptance does not make the graph safe.

14.16. Risk acceptance does not modify Pack metadata.

14.17. Risk acceptance does not satisfy hard dependencies unless the accepted
       decision explicitly resolves the hard dependency failure through an
       allowed path.

14.18. Risk acceptance does not grant capability permission.

14.19. Risk acceptance does not grant execution authorization for unsafe code.

14.20. If an accepted risky graph later fails, the failure is expected
       evidence in the sense that the risk was known and recorded.

14.21. Expected-break evidence does not convert failure into success.

14.22. Expected-break evidence helps diagnostics explain that the run used an
       explicitly accepted risky graph.

14.23. PackManager does not hide failure merely because risk was accepted.


===============================================================================
15. RELATION TO IMPORTING AND MODULE LOADING
===============================================================================

15.01. Importing and module-loading semantics are defined by DA-12.

15.02. PackManager does not import Pack code as part of catalog listing.

15.03. PackManager does not import Pack code as part of graph preparation.

15.04. PackManager does not import Pack code as part of activation-plan
      preparation.

15.05. PackManager does not use native imports to inspect Pack runtime
      symbols.

15.06. PackManager does not bypass turnixImport, dependency consistency,
      visibility, activation, or execution authority.

15.07. PackManager may inspect metadata, manifests, indexes, graph evidence,
      and trace evidence through authorized non-executing paths.

15.08. Metadata inspection does not become code execution.

15.09. Code import or adapter artifact loading remains governed by DA-12,
      DA-14, DA-23, and DA-27.

15.10. Module loading does not establish selected graph validity by itself.

15.11. Module loading does not establish activation-plan correctness by
       itself.

15.12. Module loading does not repair graph-preparation failures.

15.13. Module loading does not repair dependency cycles.


===============================================================================
16. RELATION TO ACTIVATION
===============================================================================

16.01. Activation semantics are defined by DA-14.

16.02. PackManager may influence activation order by preparing the accepted
      graph or activation plan.

16.03. PackManager does not activate Packs by itself.

16.04. PackManager does not call onLoad(ctx) by itself.

16.05. PackManager does not create ModCallContext gateway authority by itself.

16.06. PackManager may present activation readiness, activation ordering,
      dependency blocking, cyclic activation grouping, and activation failure
      diagnostics.

16.07. Activation failure remains activation-domain evidence under DA-14.

16.08. PackManager diagnostics do not replace activation trace evidence.

16.09. PackManager warnings do not become activation outcomes.

16.10. An activation executor does not perform PackManager compatibility
       planning by itself.

16.11. An activation executor may consume an accepted activation plan.

16.12. Consuming an accepted activation plan does not make the activation
       executor PackManager.

16.13. Mechanical loading, adapter artifact loading, importing, and activation
       execution do not perform PackManager policy or graph-preparation work
       by themselves.

16.14. Activation may fail when the accepted activation plan is incomplete,
       invalid for the current runtime, or impossible to execute.

16.15. Activation-plan failure during activation remains activation-domain
       evidence with PackManager graph evidence as context.

16.16. Activation success does not retroactively make PackManager the owner of
       lifecycle semantics.


===============================================================================
17. RELATION TO SERVICES, CAPABILITIES AND PERMISSIONS
===============================================================================

17.01. Service, capability, and permission semantics are defined by DA-16.

17.02. PackManager operations may be exposed through RuntimeHost commands.

17.03. PackManager operations may be exposed through capabilities when Pack,
      View, adapter-executed code, or cross-Pack runtime behavior requests
      PackManager behavior.

17.04. PackManager operations may be exposed through RPC or equivalent mediated
      request surfaces.

17.05. PackManager operations that install, update, remove, download, search
      external sources, modify selected graphs, prepare activation plans, copy
      Packs into SaveBundles, export SaveBundles, or change persisted state
      require appropriate permission, policy, persistence, and trace handling.

17.06. A PackManager service, if one exists internally, is runtime wiring only.

17.07. A PackManager service is not a cross-Pack authority bypass.

17.08. AppPacks, ViewPacks, tools, tests, AI buddy surfaces, games, or launcher
      surfaces do not receive raw PackManager authority merely because they
      can display PackManager UI.

17.09. Capability-mediated PackManager operations remain capability-mediated.

17.10. RuntimeHost-command PackManager operations remain RuntimeHost-command
       operations and do not become Pack authority.

17.11. PackManager does not grant capability permission.

17.12. PackManager does not resolve capability export conflicts silently.

17.13. PackManager may help present capability conflicts, but DA-16 governs
       the conflict outcome.


===============================================================================
18. RELATION TO POLICY AND USER DECISIONS
===============================================================================

18.01. User decision and remembered policy semantics are defined by DA-17.

18.02. PackManager may request decisions for graph ambiguity, advisory risk,
      source trust, unsafe operation, install/update/delete confirmation, hard
      dependency resolution where allowed, cyclic activation group admission
      where required, or SaveBundle graph repair.

18.03. PackManager does not own remembered-decision precedence.

18.04. PackManager does not silently remember user decisions.

18.05. PackManager does not silently broaden remembered decisions to other
      Packs, other versions, other roots, other sources, other SaveBundles,
      other Applications, other ApplicationRuns, or other trust categories.

18.06. PackManager decision prompts preserve the distinction between:
      - advisory warning and hard dependency failure
      - risk acceptance and safety
      - source trust and capability permission
      - graph selection and activation success
      - cyclic group admission and cyclic activation success
      - install/update permission and runtime execution permission
      - for-this-run and remembered decisions

18.07. PackManager decisions remain trace-visible.


===============================================================================
19. RELATION TO PERSISTENCE, CONFIGURATION AND STATE
===============================================================================

19.01. Authoritative state and transaction semantics are defined by DA-04.

19.02. Configuration and schema semantics are defined by DA-06.

19.03. Persistent I/O authority is defined by DA-07.

19.04. PackManager operations may read or write:
      - catalog caches
      - package source configuration
      - installed Pack records
      - selected graph records
      - activation-plan records where defined
      - SaveBundle graph evidence
      - copied Pack material where permitted
      - retained Pack material where permitted
      - pinned Pack references where permitted
      - user decisions through DA-17-owned mechanisms
      - tool state through the owning state/configuration mechanism

19.05. PackManager writes occur only through Kernel-mediated persistence,
      configuration, transaction, and policy boundaries.

19.06. PackManager does not write into Pack content roots unless the operation
      is an authorized pack-management operation for that root.

19.07. PackManager does not make runtime Pack code a writer for installed Pack
      roots.

19.08. PackManager does not mutate SaveBundle material except through
      SaveBundle, persistence, policy, and authority rules.

19.09. PackManager persistence failure is explicit.

19.10. PackManager persistence failure does not silently become successful
       graph preparation.

19.11. PackManager persistence failure does not silently become successful
       activation-plan preparation.


===============================================================================
20. RELATION TO RUNTIMEHOST, APPLICATION, APPLICATIONRUN AND SAVEBUNDLE
===============================================================================

20.01. RuntimeHost semantics are defined by DA-28.

20.02. Application and ApplicationRun lifecycle semantics are defined by DA-20.

20.03. SaveBundle binding and writable ownership semantics are defined by
      Application, SaveBundle, state, and persistence documents.

20.04. PackManager may operate at RuntimeHost scope before any Application
      exists.

20.05. PackManager may prepare a graph for Application creation or for a
      future ApplicationRun, depending on the operation.

20.06. PackManager may prepare an activation plan for Application creation or
      for a future ApplicationRun, depending on the operation.

20.07. PackManager may check last-used or last-known-good graph evidence for a
      SaveBundle before ApplicationRun creation.

20.08. PackManager may inspect durable graph evidence for an Application when
      policy permits that inspection.

20.09. PackManager may inspect active graph evidence for an ApplicationRun when
      policy permits that inspection.

20.10. PackManager may prepare graph changes for future ApplicationRun load,
       restart, or migration.

20.11. PackManager does not silently mutate ApplicationRun active graph state.

20.12. PackManager does not silently update Packs that are active in a running
       ApplicationRun.

20.13. PackManager does not silently make changes to a SaveBundle-owned copied
       Pack graph.

20.14. PackManager graph changes affecting a running ApplicationRun require
       explicit lifecycle, policy, and trace handling.

20.15. SaveBundle last-known-good fallback remains governed by DA-13 and
       activation behavior.


===============================================================================
21. RELATION TO VIEWS, UI AND FRONTEND RUNTIME
===============================================================================

21.01. UI, View, and frontend runtime semantics are defined by DA-22.

21.02. PackManager may expose one or more Views.

21.03. PackManager Views are presentation and interaction surfaces.

21.04. PackManager Views may be displayed by:
      - Turnix Default Launcher
      - dedicated PackManager tooling
      - AI buddy surfaces
      - application surfaces
      - developer tools
      - test tools

21.05. Displaying a PackManager View does not grant pack-management authority
      to the displaying surface.

21.06. Frontend PackManager code remains adapter-executed frontend code.

21.07. Frontend PackManager code does not directly mutate pack catalogs,
      selected graphs, activation plans, roots, SaveBundles, Applications,
      ApplicationRuns, or configuration.

21.08. Frontend PackManager commands become meaningful only when Kernel-owned
      authority accepts the corresponding command, capability request, RPC
      message, decision, or runtime operation.

21.09. PackManager UI may present advisory warnings, hard failures, risk
      decisions, graph previews, activation-plan previews, source trust,
      install/update/delete actions, active graph state, and SaveBundle graph
      evidence.

21.10. PackManager UI does not own the semantics it presents.


===============================================================================
22. RELATION TO EXECUTION ADAPTERS
===============================================================================

22.01. Execution adapter boundary semantics are defined by DA-23.

22.02. Adapter lifecycle, trust, isolation, and adapter-domain trace semantics
      are defined by DA-27.

22.03. PackManager does not gain adapter authority by selecting or presenting
      an adapter-related graph.

22.04. Adapter selection for executable artifacts remains governed by DA-23 and
      DA-27.

22.05. PackManager may present adapter requirements, adapter availability,
      adapter trust warnings, or adapter incompatibilities.

22.06. PackManager presentation of adapter evidence does not authorize adapter
      execution.

22.07. Adapter execution authorization remains distinct from PackManager graph
      preparation.

22.08. Adapter failure does not become PackManager failure by default.

22.09. PackManager graph problems do not become adapter failure by default.

22.10. PackManager activation-plan preparation may include adapter
       participation evidence when available.

22.11. Adapter participation evidence does not make PackManager the owner of
       adapter loading.

22.12. Adapter artifact loading does not prepare activation plans by itself.

===============================================================================
26. WHAT IS EXPLICITLY NOT SUPPORTED
===============================================================================

26.01. The following are not supported:
      - PackManager replacing Kernel authority
      - PackManager replacing Discovery
      - PackManager replacing generic resolution
      - PackManager redefining Pack identity or visibility
      - PackManager redefining hard dependency semantics
      - PackManager converting advisory relations into hard dependencies by
        itself
      - PackManager converting advisory relations into activation blockers by
        itself
      - PackManager importing Pack code during catalog listing or graph
        preparation
      - PackManager importing Pack code during activation-plan preparation
      - PackManager activating Packs by preparing a graph
      - PackManager activating Packs by preparing an activation plan
      - PackManager calling onLoad(ctx) by itself
      - PackManager creating ModCallContext gateway authority by itself
      - PackManager granting capability permission
      - PackManager bypassing capability mediation for cross-Pack operations
      - PackManager exposing raw management authority through a View
      - PackManager service objects being used as cross-Pack authority bypass
      - PackManager silently writing roots, configuration, SaveBundles,
        selected graphs, activation plans, or policy decisions
      - PackManager silently resolving ambiguity
      - PackManager silently accepting risky graphs
      - PackManager silently admitting cyclic activation groups
      - PackManager silently broadening remembered decisions
      - PackManager silently updating active Packs used by a running
        ApplicationRun
      - PackManager treating SaveBundle as a Pack kind
      - PackManager treating SaveBundle listing as ListPacks filtered by Pack
        kind
      - PackManager warnings being treated as activation outcomes
      - activation plans being treated as activation success
      - selected graphs being treated as loader instructions by themselves
      - mechanical loaders, importers, adapter artifact loaders, or activation
        executors performing PackManager policy or graph-preparation work by
        themselves

===============================================================================
28. LOCKED SUMMARY
===============================================================================

28.01. LOCKED:
      - PackManager is the official Turnix pack-management and graph-
        preparation domain.
      - PackManager prepares, explains, validates, repairs, and manages
        selected pack graph intent.
      - PackManager may prepare selected pack graphs and activation plans.
      - Activation plan means selected graph intent refined for activation
        order and activation readiness.
      - Activation plan is information, graph intent, and ordering evidence.
      - Activation plan is not activation, loading, importing, adapter
        execution, ModCallContext gateway authority, or capability permission.
      - PackManager is not Kernel authority by itself.
      - PackManager is not Discovery, generic resolution, importing,
        activation, adapter execution, mechanical loading, activation
        execution, capability permission, policy authority, user-decision
        authority, persistence authority, Application lifecycle authority,
        ApplicationRun lifecycle authority, SaveBundle authority,
        ModCallContext gateway authority, or a View by itself.
      - PackManager operations may exist before any Application exists.
      - PackManager operations may be requested from an ApplicationRun only
        through permitted mediated surfaces.
      - ListPacks is the general Pack-listing primitive.
      - AppPack, ViewPack, ContentPack, and ModPack listings are filters over
        Pack kind.
      - SaveBundle-related listing is a separate persistence-aware catalog
        operation and not a Pack-kind filter.
      - SaveBundle itself is not a Pack.
      - Catalog operations do not lazily rescan the filesystem unless explicit
        rescan is requested and accepted.
      - Catalog presentation preserves distinctions between existence,
        visibility, resolution eligibility, dependency satisfaction, selected
        graph membership, activation state, and capability availability.
      - PackManager scopes are explicit.
      - PackManager consumes DA-09 roots/Discovery, DA-10
        identity/visibility, DA-11 resolution, DA-13 dependency/advisory
        metadata, DA-14 activation, DA-16 capability boundaries, DA-17
        policy, DA-20 Application and ApplicationRun semantics, DA-22 UI,
        DA-23/DA-27 adapter, DA-28 RuntimeHost, and DA-30 ModCallContext
        semantics.
      - PackManager does not silently bypass any of those semantics.
      - Advisory pack relations remain advisory unless explicit policy or
        user decision makes them part of selected graph handling.
      - Hard dependency failure remains hard failure unless an accepted
        policy or user decision explicitly resolves it under the owning
        documents.
      - PackManager may detect strongly connected components.
      - Strongly connected component detection is not cyclic activation by
        itself.
      - A cyclic activation group is a strongly connected component admitted
        by the accepted activation plan for DA-14 cyclic activation handling.
      - Cyclic activation group admission does not prove activation success.
      - PackManager repair is explicit, scoped, policy- or user-accepted
        graph-preparation repair.
      - Risk acceptance does not make a risky graph safe.
      - Expected-break evidence does not convert failure into success.
      - PackManager graph preparation does not activate, import, execute, or
        grant ModCallContext gateway authority.
      - Mechanical loaders load what they are instructed to load under the
        applicable authority boundary.
      - Mechanical loaders do not own dependency ordering, activation ordering,
        graph preparation, graph repair, or cyclic activation admission.
      - Loading material in an order not supported by the accepted activation
        plan may fail by design.
      - PackManager operations that perform powerful or persistent behavior
        require appropriate permission, policy, persistence, and trace
        handling.
      - PackManager Views are presentation and interaction surfaces, not raw
        management authority.
      - DA-29 owns PackManager-domain trace reason identifiers.
      - DA-03 owns the trace substrate, not PackManager semantics.

28.02. OPEN:
      - concrete operation identifiers, scope vocabulary, ListPacks envelopes,
        SaveBundle listing envelopes, catalog fields, selected graph schema,
        activation-plan schema, activation-plan identity, activation-plan
        preview UI, capability identifiers, command syntax, RPC fields, View
        embedding, package source/search/install/update/remove protocols,
        SaveBundle export, active ApplicationRun graph-change policy, and
        PackManager trace field schema.
