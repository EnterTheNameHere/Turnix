===============================================================================
DESIGN AREA DA-17 - USER INTERACTION, DECISION POINTS & POLICY HANDLING
===============================================================================

Status:
-------
EVOLVING

Description:
------------
Defines how Turnix involves the user in decisions, when execution is allowed
to pause, how decisions are persisted, and how policy reduces repeated
friction without hiding uncertainty or redefining authority.

Purpose:
--------
This document defines the Turnix decision-handling model for situations where
the system cannot proceed safely or deterministically on its own.

It defines:
- user interaction as a Kernel-mediated system concern
- decision-point types
- hard and soft decision handling
- scoped pause-and-resume behavior
- policy handling and policy persistence
- run, save, and global policy scope precedence
- remembered permission-policy key semantics
- remembered fingerprint-mismatch-policy key semantics
- work-lifecycle policy decisions
- trace-backed decision explanation
- frontend responsibility at decision time
- failure behavior while a decision is pending

It does NOT define:
- committed-state truth, transactions, or authoritative mutation (see DA-04)
- configuration storage file placement (see DA-06)
- persistent-I/O authority (see DA-07)
- generic resolution mechanics (see DA-11)
- importing and module-loading rules (see DA-12)
- activation lifecycle or execution guarantees (see DA-14)
- capability schemas and permission-class definitions (see DA-16)
- debugging, inspection, breakpoint, or execution-control behavior (see DA-18)
- Job lifecycle, Job terminal states, or Job result semantics (see DA-19)
- Application or ApplicationRun lifecycle semantics (see DA-20)
- RPC, channel, transport, timeout, disconnect, or message-flow semantics
  (see DA-21)
- UI, View, frontend runtime, or multi-client presentation mechanics
  (see DA-22)
- adapter lifecycle, trust, isolation, or failure semantics (see DA-27)
- OrchestrationUnit lifecycle or mutation authority (see DA-24)
- Window finalization or artifact/export semantics (see DA-08 and DA-25)
- comparison, Replay, Resume, Retry, or retained-evidence workflow semantics
  (see DA-26)
- RuntimeHost lifecycle, command surfaces, or workspace ownership (see DA-28)
- PackManager catalog, repair, graph-preparation, or operation outcomes
  (see DA-29)
- trace substrate, retention, loss, trace DAG, event schema, or span semantics
  (see DA-03)

===============================================================================
01. INTERPRETATION BASIS
===============================================================================

01.01. This document expresses normative meaning through explicit,
      deterministic prose.

01.02. Normative meaning in Turnix derives from sentence structure and
      document context rather than from RFC-style keywords.

01.03. The CORE META-RULES documents define the general rule language and
      interpretation model used by this document.

01.04. This document defines user interaction, decision points, and policy
      handling within that shared interpretation model.


===============================================================================
02. CORE INTENT
===============================================================================

02.01. Turnix does not make ambiguous decisions silently.

02.02. Turnix does not delegate authority implicitly.

02.03. Once a decision is made, later behavior remains deterministic within
      the scope governed by that decision.

02.04. User interaction exists only to resolve situations where Turnix cannot
      proceed safely or deterministically on its own.

02.05. These situations include:
      - ambiguity
      - permission
      - conflict
      - recovery
      - suspicious content divergence
      - work-lifecycle policy choice

02.06. If a decision affects identity, persistence, authority, safety, work
      lifecycle, or known content equivalence, the decision is explicit and
      trace-backed.

02.07. Decision handling reduces uncertainty by recording an explicit user or
      policy outcome.

02.08. Decision handling does not prove authenticity, trust, or correctness.

02.09. Policy may influence work lifecycle only through explicit policy
      outcomes.

02.10. Policy does not silently create, cancel, supersede, detach, retain,
       or discard work.

02.11. Remembered policy reduces repeated friction.

02.12. Remembered policy does not define domain outcomes, committed state,
       Job outcomes, Window outcomes, Replay, Resume, Retry, artifact export,
       or retained-evidence workflow results.

02.13. DA-06 owns policy storage location and top-level persistent file
       structure.

02.14. DA-17 owns decision semantics and remembered-policy meaning.


===============================================================================
03. AUTHORITY BOUNDARIES
===============================================================================

03.01. User interaction is not:
      - UI-specific
      - mod-specific
      - frontend-specific

03.02. User interaction is:
      - Kernel-mediated
      - policy-driven
      - serializable
      - trace-backed

03.03. All decision points originate in the Kernel.

03.04. Mods and packs may trigger decision points.

03.05. Mods and packs do not own, implement, or bypass decision handling.

03.06. Policy decisions choose allowed responses where Turnix cannot safely
      or deterministically proceed on its own.

03.07. Policy does not redefine authority.

03.08. Policy does not directly define:
      - committed-state truth
      - Job terminal states
      - RPC or transport outcomes
      - OrchestrationUnit terminal outcomes
      - Application or ApplicationRun lifecycle
      - adapter lifecycle
      - RuntimeHost lifecycle
      - Window finalization
      - PackManager operation outcomes
      - Replay, Resume, or Retry workflow outcomes
      - artifact or export authority
      - capability permission semantics

03.09. If a policy decision affects another domain, the owning domain defines
      the resulting domain outcome.

03.10. Correlation between a policy decision and a domain outcome is
       explicit.

03.11. Frontend presentation state is not policy state.

03.12. Policy scope is not ownership authority.

03.13. Persisted policy records are not hidden authority merely because they
       are persisted.


===============================================================================
04. DECISION POINT TYPES
===============================================================================

04.01. Turnix recognizes the following decision-point types:
      - Resolution decisions
      - Permission decisions
      - Recovery decisions
      - Configuration-scope decisions
      - Fingerprint-mismatch decisions
      - Work-lifecycle policy decisions

04.02 Resolution decisions
--------------------------

04.02.01. Resolution decisions occur when Turnix cannot deterministically
        select one safe option.

04.02.02. Examples include:
        - multiple eligible packs
        - semantic-version mismatch override
        - pinned pack missing
        - last-known-good fallback
        - conflicting pack requests

04.02.03. Resolution-decision outcomes may include:
        - chosen pack or version
        - persisted resolution rule when applicable
        - trace evidence explaining why the selected option was chosen

04.03 Permission decisions
--------------------------

04.03.01. Permission decisions occur when an action would cross an authority
        or trust boundary.

04.03.02. Examples include:
        - external network access
        - filesystem writes through IoManager
        - mod installation
        - invoking a hard-classified capability

04.03.03. Permission decisions apply only to actions that:
        - mutate persisted state
        - delete persisted state
        - intentionally cross ownership boundaries

04.03.04. Permission decisions are not raised for:
        - computed-only effects
        - runtime-only transforms
        - observation or watching of values

04.03.05. Read access is excluded from permission decisions:
        - reads either succeed or return NotFoundOrPermissionDenied
        - user interaction is never used for reads
        - prompting, confirmation, and policy do not apply to reads

04.03.06. This preserves determinism, prevents probing, and avoids decision
        spam.

04.03.07. Permission-decision outcomes may include:
        - allow
        - deny
        - for this run
        - remember

04.03.08. For capability-based permission decisions, the remembered permission
        identity uses the exact versioned capability identifier.

04.03.09. A capability permission for storage.export@1 is remembered against
        storage.export@1, not against storage.export, storage, or a wildcard
        family.

04.03.10. This document does not introduce wildcard, prefix, or versionless
         fallback lookup for capability permission policies.

04.04 Recovery decisions
------------------------

04.04.01. Recovery decisions occur when persisted state cannot be used safely.

04.04.02. Examples include:
        - corrupted config file
        - unreadable SaveBundle
        - failed migration
        - checksum mismatch
        - missing dependency for a SaveBundle

04.04.03. Recovery-decision outcomes may include:
        - restore from backup
        - reset to defaults
        - quarantine or rename broken data
        - abort startup of the affected unit

04.04.04. Recovery decisions reference:
        - last-known-good state
        - available backups
        - scope of impact such as engine, pack, or save-bound state

04.05 Configuration-scope decisions
-----------------------------------

04.05.01. Configuration-scope decisions occur when persistence intent is
        ambiguous rather than unsafe.

04.05.02. These decisions answer:
        "Where should this value live, if it is persisted at all?"

04.05.03. Examples include:
        - modifying a value that exists in both global and save scope
        - promoting a save-local value to pack-global or engine scope
        - deleting a value that shadows defaults
        - attempting to persist a value produced only by computation

04.05.04. Configuration-scope decisions exclude:
        - computed-only values
        - derived runtime effects
        - accessibility or reactive transforms

04.05.05. Configuration-scope-decision outcomes may include:
        - chosen scope such as engine, pack-global, or save-bound
        - persisted rule when applicable
        - trace evidence linking the decision to the key and scope

04.06 Fingerprint-mismatch decisions
------------------------------------

04.06.01. Fingerprint-mismatch decisions occur when Turnix has an expected
        pack fingerprint and the computed fingerprint differs.

04.06.02. Fingerprint mismatch means content divergence from an expected pack
        representation.

04.06.03. Fingerprint mismatch does not prove malicious action or
        authenticity failure.

04.06.04. Fingerprint-mismatch decision triggers are:
        - same canonicalPackId and same packVersion with different
          fingerprints
        - SaveBundle-associated expected fingerprint mismatch
        - SaveBundle-local retained Pack fingerprint mismatch
        - manifest-declared dependency fingerprint mismatch

04.06.05. Manifest-declared dependency fingerprint mismatch applies only when
        the manifest actually declares an expected fingerprint.

04.06.06. Origin-only collision is not a fingerprint-mismatch decision
        trigger.

04.06.07. A first-party-looking source, repo source, third-party source,
        custom source, save-local source, or externally-managed content
        source does not by itself prove which candidate is authentic.

04.06.08. Candidate origin may be shown as context to the user, but origin
        alone is not treated as proof.

04.06.09. Fingerprint-mismatch-decision outcomes may include:
        - allow
        - deny
        - for this run
        - remember

04.06.10. Allowing for this run does not update the stored expected
         fingerprint.

04.06.11. Remembering a fingerprint-mismatch decision records acceptance of
         the specific mismatch under the selected policy scope.

04.07 Work-lifecycle policy decisions
-------------------------------------

04.07.01. Work-lifecycle policy decisions occur when Turnix must choose how
        accepted or in-flight work proceeds after a lifecycle-relevant
        condition.

04.07.02. Work-lifecycle policy decisions may be correlated with:
        - Jobs
        - OrchestrationUnits
        - RPC requests
        - scheduler-owned work
        - debugger-controlled work
        - adapter-mediated work
        - RuntimeHost operations
        - Windows
        - PackManager operations
        - Replay, Resume, or Retry workflow requests

04.07.03. Work-lifecycle policy decisions may be triggered by:
        - cancellation request
        - timeout boundary
        - supersession condition
        - frontend or transport disconnect
        - scheduler replacement
        - debugger control action
        - adapter failure or disconnect
        - RuntimeHost shutdown
        - system shutdown

04.07.04. Work-lifecycle policy outcomes may include:
        - continue work
        - cancel work
        - supersede work
        - detach work from the transport or frontend that caused it
        - retain result for later retrieval
        - discard result when no consumer remains
        - refuse unsafe continuation
        - abort startup or affected work

04.07.05. Work-lifecycle policy decisions do not define Job terminal states.

04.07.06. DA-19 defines Job lifecycle states, terminal outcomes,
        cancellation, timeout, supersession, progress, result, and late
        completion semantics.

04.07.07. Work-lifecycle policy decisions do not define RPC or transport
        mechanics.

04.07.08. DA-21 defines RPC, RPCChannel, transport, timeout, disconnect,
        cancellation message, and acknowledgement semantics.

04.07.09. Work-lifecycle policy decisions do not define capability permission
        semantics.

04.07.10. DA-16 defines capability permission and mediation semantics.

04.07.11. DA-18 defines debugger pause, breakpoint, inspection, and
         execution-control behavior.

04.07.12. DA-22 defines UI, View, frontend runtime, frontend presentation,
         frontend disconnect, and multi-client presentation semantics.

04.07.13. DA-24 defines OrchestrationUnit lifecycle, mutation authority, and
         terminal outcomes.

04.07.14. DA-25 defines Window artifacts, Window manifests, Window exports,
         optional digest representation, partial export, missing material,
         and offline inspection artifact semantics.

04.07.15. DA-26 defines comparison, ComparisonResult, Replay, Resume, Retry,
         and retained-evidence workflow semantics.

04.07.16. DA-27 defines adapter lifecycle, trust, isolation, failure,
         disconnect, and adapter-domain trace semantics.

04.07.17. DA-28 defines RuntimeHost lifecycle, startup modes, command
         surfaces, workspace ownership, local-first, and LAN exposure
         semantics.

04.07.18. DA-29 defines PackManager catalog, graph-preparation, repair,
         View, write, and PackManager-domain trace semantics.

04.07.19. A policy outcome that affects a Job remains trace-linked to the
         Job lifecycle evidence when a Job exists.

04.07.20. A policy outcome that affects an OrchestrationUnit remains
         trace-linked to OrchestrationUnit evidence when such a unit exists.


===============================================================================
05. HARD AND SOFT DECISIONS
===============================================================================

05.01. Turnix distinguishes between soft decisions and hard decisions.

05.02 Soft decisions
--------------------

05.02.01. Soft-decision characteristics are:
        - execution may continue if denied
        - denial does not break identity
        - policy may auto-allow where permitted

05.02.02. Examples include:
        - version mismatch warnings
        - optional capability usage
        - non-critical override refusals
        - replacing preview work with newer preview work

05.03 Hard decisions
--------------------

05.03.01. Hard-decision characteristics are:
        - execution pauses
        - denial blocks progress
        - user response is mandatory unless an accepted policy already
          supplies the outcome

05.03.02. Examples include:
        - replacing a pinned pack
        - destructive migration
        - restoring from backup
        - persisting a change that alters identity
        - deciding whether work with committed side effects may continue
          detached after transport disconnect

05.03.03. Hard decisions always:
        - generate trace events
        - require explicit acknowledgement of the effective outcome

05.03.04. Fingerprint-mismatch decisions may be hard or soft depending on the
        surrounding consumer context and impact.

05.03.05. SaveBundle reproducibility or exact dependency expectations may
        require hard handling.

05.03.06. Work-lifecycle policy decisions may be hard or soft depending on
        whether the affected work can safely continue, cancel, supersede, or
        detach without changing authority, persistence, or safety meaning.


===============================================================================
06. PAUSING MODEL
===============================================================================

06.01. When a decision is required:
      - the triggering execution unit pauses
      - unrelated execution may continue
      - frontends are notified through a Kernel-mediated event
      - execution resumes only after decision handling completes

06.02. During a decision pause:
      - no busy waiting occurs
      - no polling occurs
      - no global freeze occurs

06.03. Pause remains scoped and trace-linked.

06.04. While a decision is pending:
      - no persisted mutation may be committed
      - no configuration write may be finalized
      - no SaveBundle mutation may complete
      - no Job affected by the decision may silently cross the
        decision-controlled boundary
      - no OrchestrationUnit affected by the decision may silently cross the
        decision-controlled boundary

06.05. Computed or runtime-only effects may continue if they do not persist
      state and do not cross the paused authority boundary.

06.06. A decision pause does not by itself cancel, supersede, or time out a
      Job.

06.07. Job lifecycle changes caused by decision timeout or policy are owned
      by DA-19 and remain trace-visible.

06.08. A decision pause does not by itself define Window finalization.

06.09. Window finalization remains owned by DA-08 and related artifact/export
      documents.


===============================================================================
07. POLICY SYSTEM
===============================================================================

07.01. Policies define how Turnix behaves when a decision arises.

07.02. Policy sources include:
      - global defaults
      - user-defined overrides
      - pack-provided hints that remain non-binding

07.03. Policies may express:
      - ask always
      - allow for this run
      - deny for this run
      - allow and remember
      - deny and remember
      - continue work
      - cancel work
      - supersede work
      - detach work
      - retain result
      - discard result

07.04. Policies influence default responses.

07.05. Policies do not redefine authority.

07.06. Policies do not:
      - override ownership rules
      - bypass access levels
      - grant mutation rights forbidden by system rules
      - convert Job terminal outcomes into ordinary failures
      - treat transport timeout as Job timeout
      - treat Job timeout as transport timeout
      - treat frontend disconnect as ApplicationRun termination
      - treat adapter failure as Kernel failure
      - treat RuntimeHost shutdown as Job cancellation by itself

07.07. Policy exists to reduce friction rather than to weaken safety or
      determinism.

07.08. Policy evaluation order is:
      1) run policy
      2) save policy
      3) global policy

07.09. Run policy overrides save policy.

07.10. Save policy overrides global policy.

07.11. Run policy is memory-only and ends with the current ApplicationRun or
       bounded run context that created it.

07.12. Save policy is persisted inside the save-bound policy storage defined
       by DA-06.

07.13. Global policy is persisted inside the global policy storage defined
       by DA-06.

07.14. Work-lifecycle policy evaluation records the policy source that
       supplied or influenced the outcome.

07.15. A policy source that influences an outcome does not own the target
       domain outcome.


===============================================================================
08. POLICY PERSISTENCE
===============================================================================

08.01. When a user makes a remembered decision:
      - it is persisted centrally in the selected scope
      - future matching decisions become deterministic within that scope
      - trace evidence links the decision to its origin

08.02. Persisted policies are:
      - explicit
      - inspectable
      - editable through Turnix management surfaces

08.03. Silent or implicit policy creation is forbidden.

08.04. Run-scope policy is not persisted.

08.05. Save-scope and global-scope policy storage locations are defined by
      DA-06.

08.06. Policy storage location and top-level persisted file structure are
      defined by DA-06.

08.07. Decision meaning, remembered-policy matching, policy scope precedence,
      permission policy keys, fingerprint policy keys, and work-lifecycle
      policy semantics are defined by DA-17.

08.08. Storing a policy record does not by itself create a valid policy
      outcome outside DA-17 semantics.

08.09. Persisted policy files contain distinct remembered-decision classes
      for permission policies and fingerprint policies.

08.10. Work-lifecycle policies are stored separately from permission and
       fingerprint policies when persisted policy storage supports them.

08.11. A remembered work-lifecycle policy does not imply a remembered
       capability permission decision.

08.12. A remembered capability permission decision does not imply a
       remembered work-lifecycle policy.

08.13. Exact JSON record layout inside policy files is defined outside this
       document and remains storage-format detail unless another document
       locks it.


===============================================================================
09. PERMISSION POLICY KEYS
===============================================================================

09.01. Remembered general permission policy is keyed by:
      - packCanonicalId
      - exact versioned capability id

09.02. The exact versioned capability id is the capability id that caused the
      permission decision.

09.03. Versionless capability family lookup is not used for remembered
      permission policy.

09.04. Wildcard capability matching is not used for remembered permission
      policy.

09.05. Broader prefix lookup is not used for remembered permission policy.

09.06. Permission policy records store comparison metadata, including:
      - tierMode
      - acceptedAtPackVersion
      - remembered outcome

09.07. tierMode controls when the user is asked again for pack-version
      changes.

09.08. Pedantic tier asks again when the pack major version changes.

09.09. Second tier asks again when the pack minor version changes.

09.10. Normal tier asks again only when no remembered decision applies.

09.11. The pack version is comparison metadata.

09.12. The pack version is not part of the permission policy identity key.

09.13. Capability permission semantics remain owned by DA-16.


===============================================================================
10. FINGERPRINT POLICY KEYS
===============================================================================

10.01. Remembered fingerprint-mismatch policy is keyed by:
      - canonicalPackId
      - packVersion
      - expectedFingerprint
      - actualFingerprint

10.02. expectedFingerprint and actualFingerprint are full fingerprint strings.

10.03. The fingerprint algorithm/version is part of the full fingerprint
      string rather than a separate policy-key field.

10.04. Fingerprint-mismatch policy is specific to the exact mismatch pair.

10.05. Remembering one fingerprint mismatch does not bless all future
      mismatches for the same pack or version.

10.06. For-this-run fingerprint acceptance does not update persisted policy
      and does not update an expected fingerprint.

10.07. Remembered fingerprint acceptance may update the applicable policy
      record for the selected scope.

10.08. SaveBundle-associated fingerprint mismatch defaults to save-scope
      persistence when the user chooses remember, unless a higher explicit
      policy says otherwise.

10.09. General permission decisions default to global-scope persistence when
      the user chooses remember, unless a higher explicit policy says
      otherwise.

10.10. Origin context is comparison context.

10.11. Origin context is not part of the fingerprint policy identity key
       unless the fingerprint string itself includes origin-bound material.


===============================================================================
11. WORK-LIFECYCLE POLICY KEYS
===============================================================================

11.01. Remembered work-lifecycle policy is keyed by the explicit policy
      subject defined by the workflow or implementation that raises the
      work-lifecycle decision.

11.02. Work-lifecycle policy subject identity may include:
      - work kind
      - owner domain
      - capability identity when capability work is involved
      - RPC route or request class when RPC work is involved
      - scheduler policy class when scheduler work is involved
      - disconnect condition when disconnect handling is involved
      - supersession target class when supersession handling is involved
      - adapter condition when adapter work is involved
      - RuntimeHost condition when host work is involved

11.03. A remembered work-lifecycle policy is not keyed by jobId alone.

11.04. jobId identifies one Job lifecycle.

11.05. A remembered work-lifecycle policy governs future matching decisions,
      not only the original Job instance.

11.06. A remembered policy that applies to cancellation does not imply a
      remembered policy that applies to supersession, timeout, disconnect,
      detachment, result retention, result discard, adapter failure,
      RuntimeHost failure, Window finalization, Replay, Resume, or Retry.

11.07. A remembered policy that applies to transport disconnect does not
      imply a remembered policy that applies to Job timeout.

11.08. A remembered policy that applies to adapter failure does not imply a
      remembered policy that applies to RuntimeHost failure.

11.09. The exact work-lifecycle policy-key schema remains OPEN until the
      owning workflow or implementation specification defines it.


===============================================================================
12. TRACE INTEGRATION
===============================================================================

12.01. Every decision point emits trace events including:
      - decision type
      - triggering cause
      - affected scope such as engine, pack, or save-bound scope
      - available options
      - selected outcome
      - policy involvement when applicable

12.02. Fingerprint-mismatch decisions additionally trace:
      - canonicalPackId
      - packVersion
      - expectedFingerprint
      - actualFingerprint
      - candidate origin context when available

12.03. Permission decisions additionally trace:
      - packCanonicalId
      - exact versioned capability id when capability-based
      - tierMode when policy participates
      - acceptedAtPackVersion when a remembered policy participates

12.04. Work-lifecycle policy decisions additionally trace when available:
      - jobId
      - orchestrationUnitId
      - RPC request identity
      - capability identity
      - cancellation source
      - timeout policy source
      - supersession source
      - disconnect condition
      - selected lifecycle policy outcome
      - whether result retention or discard was selected
      - affected domain owner

12.05. Decision trace requirements are decision-domain emission obligations.

12.06. DA-03 owns trace substrate, retention, loss, trace DAG, event
      structure, and span semantics.

12.07. User-facing explanations are derived from retained trace and policy
      evidence where retained evidence exists.

12.08. User-facing surfaces do not invent rationale.

12.09. Trace evidence is not committed state.

12.10. Trace loss does not redefine committed-state truth.

12.11. All decisions remain traceable and attributable through retained
       decision evidence where retained evidence exists.

12.12. Retained decision evidence may support explanation and offline
       inspection where retained evidence exists.

12.13. Decision traceability does not imply DA-26 Replay, Resume, Retry, or
       deterministic re-execution.

12.14. For denied mutation attempts, trace additionally records:
       - owner domain
       - caller domain
       - access level
       - authority failure reason

12.15. Trace emission for denied mutation attempts applies even when no user
       interaction occurs.

12.16. DA-19 defines Job lifecycle trace obligations when a
       work-lifecycle policy decision affects a Job.

12.17. DA-21 defines RPC and transport trace obligations when a
       work-lifecycle policy decision is caused by transport behavior.

12.18. DA-24 defines OrchestrationUnit trace obligations when a policy
       decision affects an OrchestrationUnit.


===============================================================================
13. FRONTEND RESPONSIBILITY
===============================================================================

13.01. Frontends present decisions and collect valid responses.

13.02. Frontends:
      - present clear explanation
      - show consequences
      - indicate scope of effect
      - offer only valid choices

13.03. Frontends do not:
      - invent defaults
      - auto-confirm without policy
      - bypass Kernel authority
      - obscure the impact of denial

13.04. The Kernel remains the decision arbiter.

13.05. Frontend presentation state is not policy state.

13.06. DA-22 owns the UI and frontend runtime mechanics of presenting and
      dismissing decision surfaces.

13.07. For fingerprint-mismatch decisions, frontends may show origin
      context, but do not present origin as authenticity proof.

13.08. For work-lifecycle policy decisions, frontends distinguish:
      - continuing work
      - cancelling work
      - superseding work
      - detaching work from the current frontend or transport
      - retaining result for later retrieval
      - discarding result when no consumer remains

13.09. Frontends do not present Superseded as failure.

13.10. Frontends do not present transport timeout as Job timeout.

13.11. Frontends do not present Job timeout as transport timeout.

13.12. Frontends do not present frontend disconnect as ApplicationRun
       termination unless the owning lifecycle domain produced that outcome.


===============================================================================
14. MULTIPLE FRONTENDS
===============================================================================

14.01. If multiple frontends are connected:
      - the decision is shown on all connected frontends
      - the first valid response wins
      - all other pending presentations are dismissed

14.02. The Kernel remains the single arbiter.

14.03. DA-22 owns multi-client presentation mechanics.

14.04. This document owns the decision meaning of the selected response.


===============================================================================
15. FAILURE MODES
===============================================================================

15.01. If a frontend disconnects during a decision:
      - the affected execution remains paused
      - timeout policy may apply
      - the user may reconnect through another valid frontend

15.02. If the decision handler fails:
      - execution aborts safely
      - failure is trace-logged
      - persisted state remains unchanged

15.03. If a frontend or transport disconnects while a Job exists, policy may
      choose whether the affected Job:
      - continues detached
      - is cancelled
      - is superseded
      - retains result for later retrieval
      - discards result if no consumer remains

15.04. Disconnect handling does not silently cancel, supersede, detach,
      retain, or discard a Job without explicit policy.

15.05. The transport disconnect mechanics are defined by DA-21.

15.06. The Job lifecycle outcome is defined by DA-19 when the policy outcome
      affects a Job.

15.07. The OrchestrationUnit lifecycle outcome is defined by DA-24 when the
      policy outcome affects an OrchestrationUnit.

15.08. The adapter lifecycle outcome is defined by DA-27 when the policy
      outcome affects adapter-mediated work.

15.09. The RuntimeHost lifecycle outcome is defined by DA-28 when the policy
      outcome affects host shutdown or command-surface behavior.

15.10. The Window finalization outcome is defined by DA-08 when the policy
       outcome affects Window finalization.

15.11. The PackManager operation outcome is defined by DA-29 when the policy
       outcome affects PackManager work.


===============================================================================
16. MOD AND PACK INVOLVEMENT
===============================================================================

16.01. Mods and packs may trigger decision points.

16.02. Mods and packs do not handle decision points directly.

16.03. Mods and packs do not block while awaiting frontend interaction.

16.04. Mods and packs do not invent policy defaults.

16.05. Mods and packs do not persist policy decisions directly.

16.06. Mods and packs observe decision outcomes only through Turnix-provided
      context and results.

16.07. The ability to trigger a decision point does not grant decision
      authority.

16.08. The ability to observe a decision outcome does not grant policy
      storage authority.


===============================================================================
17. RELATION TO DEBUGGING
===============================================================================

17.01. Decision points:
      - are trace-visible
      - may trigger breakpoints
      - remain inspectable after the fact

17.02. Debuggers may simulate decisions only in development mode.

17.03. Debugger actions that request cancellation, supersession,
      pause-at-safe-point, or continuation remain work-lifecycle policy
      decisions when policy must choose an outcome.

17.04. DA-18 defines debugger breakpoint, inspection, and execution-control
      behavior.

17.05. Debugger control does not bypass Kernel-mediated decision authority.

17.06. Debugger simulation does not create remembered policy unless the
      normal policy persistence path explicitly records such a policy.


===============================================================================
18. WHAT IS EXPLICITLY NOT SUPPORTED
===============================================================================

18.01. The following are not supported:
      - silent auto-approval without policy
      - console-based prompting
      - blocking the entire system for frontend interaction
      - mod-defined decision UIs
      - pack-defined decision UIs
      - persistence of computed-only values
      - prompting the user to allow read access to restricted state
      - treating origin alone as authenticity proof
      - wildcard capability permission matching
      - versionless capability permission fallback
      - silent work cancellation without policy or lifecycle authority
      - silent supersession without policy or lifecycle authority
      - silent detached work after transport disconnect
      - silent result retention without policy or lifecycle authority
      - silent result discard without policy or lifecycle authority
      - treating policy as committed-state truth
      - treating trace evidence as committed-state truth
      - treating frontend presentation state as policy state
      - treating stored policy files as policy meaning outside DA-17 semantics


===============================================================================
19. EXPLICITLY OPEN QUESTIONS
===============================================================================

19.01. OPEN questions include:
      - bulk decision-management user interfaces
      - policy import and export
      - scripted policy rules
      - exact work-lifecycle policy-key schema
      - exact user-facing wording for detach, retain-result, and
        discard-result decisions

19.02. Cross-scope copy and promotion are accepted conceptually, but the
      concrete API and user-interaction model remain OPEN.

19.03. OPEN cross-scope copy and promotion questions include:
      - exact API shape
      - whether copy and promotion are direct API calls, decision-driven
        flows, or both
      - naming of copy, promote, clone, move, or related operations
      - overwrite and conflict behavior when the target scope already
        contains a value
      - whether the operation is exact value copy, structural subtree copy,
        or re-materialization through normal config materialization rules
      - whether subtree copy or promotion is supported
      - whether metadata, revision markers, migration markers, or version
        fields are copied, recomputed, or omitted
      - whether copy and promotion may occur between all scope pairs or only
        some scope pairs
      - whether owner-domain boundaries impose additional permission checks
      - trace shape for copy, promotion, overwrite, refusal, discard, and
        conflict outcomes
      - interaction with existing higher-layer or lower-layer overrides
      - whether promoting a value also removes or preserves the source-layer
        value
      - whether copy and promotion may be batched
      - whether copy and promotion may be policy-remembered
      - how user-facing wording distinguishes:
        - keep local only
        - promote to broader scope
        - copy without removing source
        - move semantics if move is ever allowed
      - how computed-only or derived values are rejected cleanly in these
        flows
      - failure behavior when part of the requested change cannot be applied
      - whether the operation is atomic when multiple keys or subtrees are
        involved

19.04. Cross-scope copy and promotion must not introduce implicit promotion or
      hidden cross-scope copying.

19.05. Policy remembering for copy or promotion does not silently broaden
      authority.


===============================================================================
20. LOCKED SUMMARY
===============================================================================

20.01. LOCKED:
      - decision authority is Kernel-mediated
      - decision types remain distinct
      - work-lifecycle policy decisions are distinct decision points
      - configuration-scope resolution is explicit
      - fingerprint-mismatch decision handling is explicit and trace-backed
      - origin alone is not authenticity proof
      - execution pauses and resumes in a scoped way
      - policy is persistent and inspectable where persisted
      - policy does not redefine authority
      - policy does not define domain outcomes
      - policy does not silently create, cancel, supersede, detach, retain,
        or discard work
      - run policy overrides save policy
      - save policy overrides global policy
      - DA-06 owns policy storage location and top-level persistent file
        structure
      - DA-17 owns decision semantics and remembered-policy meaning
      - permission policy keys use packCanonicalId and exact versioned
        capability id
      - fingerprint policy keys use canonicalPackId, packVersion,
        expectedFingerprint, and actualFingerprint
      - work-lifecycle policies are distinct from permission and fingerprint
        policies
      - remembered work-lifecycle policy is not keyed by jobId alone
      - explanations are derived only from retained trace and policy evidence
        where retained evidence exists
      - frontend surfaces do not present Superseded as failure
      - frontend surfaces do not conflate transport timeout and Job timeout
      - mods and packs may trigger but do not own decisions

20.02. OPEN:
      - policy automation
      - user-experience refinement only
      - detailed cross-scope copy and promotion model
      - exact work-lifecycle policy-key schema
