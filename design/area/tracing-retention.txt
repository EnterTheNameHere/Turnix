===============================================================================
DESIGN AREA DA-03 - TRACE RETENTION AND STORAGE
===============================================================================

Status:
-------
EVOLVING

Description:
------------
Defines implementation-independent persistence, retention, continuity, loss,
and storage-boundary semantics for Actant trace evidence.

Purpose:
--------
This document separates persistent trace history from runtime trace emission
and from tooling that interprets retained evidence.

It does NOT define runtime TraceRecord creation, graph materialization,
debugger behavior, or one concrete storage technology.


===============================================================================
01. CORE BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

01.01. Trace emission, record creation, destination delivery, durable
      persistence, historical retention, and tooling interpretation are
      distinct operations.

01.02. Runtime tracing creates and publishes trace evidence.

01.03. A persistent trace destination or equivalent storage component creates
      persistent trace history from evidence delivered to it according to its
      persistence contract.

01.04. Trace tooling consumes persistent history and retention metadata but
      does not own persistence merely because it queries or visualizes it.

01.05. Persistent trace history is evidence storage, not authoritative
      committed state merely because it is durable.

01.06. Retention may intentionally remove detailed evidence. It does not
      silently convert a relationship that storage could previously account for
      into an unexplained hole merely because the target was collected.


===============================================================================
02. PERSISTENT TRACE HISTORY
===============================================================================

02.01. Persistent trace history consists of retained TraceRecords,
      TraceTypeDefinitions, and storage metadata required to interpret retained
      evidence honestly.

02.02. Persistent storage preserves semantic identities and fields of retained
      trace evidence.

02.03. Storage encoding, physical layout, database technology, indexing, and
      compression do not change trace evidence semantics.

02.04. Storage does not mutate producer-emitted evidence and present the
      mutation as original evidence.

02.05. Storage-specific metadata remains distinguishable from TraceRecords.

02.06. Storage metadata may compactly preserve knowledge about evidence that is
      no longer retained without preserving the removed TraceRecord itself.


===============================================================================
03. RETENTION POLICY
===============================================================================

03.01. Trace retention is policy-driven unless another design establishes a
      stronger persistence requirement for a defined evidence class.

03.02. A retention policy may define bounded history, rotation, pruning,
      quotas, age limits, scope limits, or intentional discard.

03.03. Retention policy defines preservation and removal behavior explicitly
      enough that consumers do not need to infer the policy from missing
      records.

03.04. Retention policy does not create committed-state authority.

03.05. An emission obligation is not a persistence guarantee.

03.06. A persistence guarantee exists only when an owning design explicitly
      requires evidence to remain available under defined conditions.

03.07. A policy may consider evidence collectible even when that evidence once
      participated in structural or causal relationships, provided enough
      knowledge survives to represent the intentional absence honestly.


===============================================================================
04. DELIVERY, PERSISTENCE, AND LOSS
===============================================================================

04.01. Failure to deliver evidence to a persistent destination, failure to make
      delivered evidence durable, later intentional collection of durable
      evidence, and unexplained disappearance are distinct forms of evidence
      unavailability.

04.02. Runtime tracing owns truthful reporting of known live publication or
      destination-delivery failure within its reporting capability.

04.03. Persistent storage owns truthful representation of known persistence,
      pruning, rotation, collection, corruption, import, export, and storage-
      loss limitations within its contract.

04.04. Storage does not claim persistence when persistence is known to have
      failed.

04.05. Storage does not claim historical completeness for a scope when it knows
      evidence in that scope was removed, lost, corrupted, or never
      successfully retained.

04.06. Loss of non-authoritative trace history weakens evidential completeness;
      it does not by itself change committed-state correctness.

04.07. Multiple destinations may possess different evidence sets. A failure or
      collection decision in one destination does not imply that equivalent
      evidence is unavailable from every other destination.

04.08. Retention does not require a globally perfect evidence set across all
      destinations. Each persistent history represents its own availability,
      collection, and loss semantics honestly.


===============================================================================
05. KNOWN ABSENCE AND UNEXPLAINED ABSENCE
===============================================================================

05.01. When persistent history knows why previously available or expected
      evidence is unavailable, it preserves enough metadata to represent that
      known condition according to its storage contract.

05.02. Known conditions may include intentional retention collection, pruning,
      rotation, quota dropping, failed persistence, known delivery failure,
      detected corruption, partial import/export, or unavailable storage
      segments.

05.03. Storage and tooling do not convert unknown absence into a fabricated
      explanation.

05.04. A missing producer-local sequence value may demonstrate a continuity gap
      in the available history. The gap alone does not prove whether evidence
      was undelivered, unretained, collected, filtered, outside selected scope,
      or lost for another reason.

05.05. Stronger availability or loss metadata may explain a continuity gap when
      available and applicable.

05.06. An absence that persistent history cannot account for remains an
      unexplained absence. It is not silently classified as ordinary retention.

05.07. Unexplained absence is diagnostically meaningful because it may indicate
      missing knowledge, broken history, unexpected delivery/storage behavior,
      or evidence outside the history's knowledge boundary.


===============================================================================
06. COMPLETENESS
===============================================================================

06.01. Completeness is evaluated for an identified evidence scope.

06.02. Persistent history does not imply global completeness merely because no
      known loss marker is present.

06.03. Storage distinguishes, where evidence permits:
      - available evidence
      - intentionally collected or removed evidence
      - known unavailable or failed evidence
      - unresolved or unexplained absence
      - outside-retained-scope evidence
      - storage-unavailable conditions

06.04. A completeness claim does not extend beyond the scope and guarantees
      from which it was established.

06.05. Trace tooling preserves storage completeness and uncertainty semantics
      when interpreting retained evidence.

06.06. Knowing that evidence was intentionally removed is weaker than retaining
      the evidence itself, but stronger than an unexplained hole. These states
      remain distinguishable.


===============================================================================
07. CONTINUITY AND ORDERING
===============================================================================

07.01. Persistent history may use trace producer identity and producer-local
      sequence to evaluate producer-local continuity.

07.02. Persistent storage preserves producer sequence semantics; it does not
      replace producer creation order with persistence or storage order.

07.03. Producer-local continuity does not establish cross-producer ordering or
      causality.

07.04. Replacement producer identities remain separate sequence and continuity
      domains.

07.05. Explicit relationships or future synchronized-producer evidence may
      relate histories without pretending that independent producer sequences
      form one sequence.

07.06. Continuity metadata may identify known storage, collection, or pruning
      boundaries.

07.07. Continuity metadata does not fabricate unavailable TraceRecords.


===============================================================================
08. RETENTION AND REFERENCED EVIDENCE
===============================================================================

08.01. Retention policy may remove evidence referenced by surviving records.

08.02. Removal does not authorize storage to rewrite surviving producer-emitted
      records to hide or alter the reference.

08.03. If a referenced target is intentionally removed by retention, surviving
      history preserves enough information for resolution to distinguish:
      - target available
      - target known removed by retention
      - target known unavailable or failed
      - target unresolved for an unexplained reason

08.04. A surviving reference to a target intentionally removed by the same
      persistent history does not degrade into an unexplained dangling link.

08.05. The retained representation of known removal need not be a TraceRecord.
      It may be storage metadata, a compact tombstone, an identity/range index,
      or another representation satisfying the semantic requirement.

08.06. The design does not require recursive preservation of every historical
      removal marker. Storage may compact or replace removal metadata provided
      surviving relationships remain classifiable according to 08.03.

08.07. A retention or export policy promising structurally complete span
      evidence defines how required structural dependencies are preserved.

08.08. When promised completeness cannot be preserved, resulting history is
      represented as incomplete rather than repaired with fabricated evidence.

08.09. Explicit causal references receive the same preservation treatment as
      structural references. Removing their targets does not rewrite the
      original causal assertion.

08.10. A reference that cannot be resolved and cannot be classified as known
      removed, known unavailable, outside a declared boundary, or otherwise
      accounted for remains explicitly unresolved. Tooling may treat this as a
      possible evidence-integrity or knowledge-gap condition.


===============================================================================
09. PERSISTENT DESTINATION CONTRACT
===============================================================================

09.01. A persistent destination consumes portable trace definitions and records
      according to the tracing destination contract.

09.02. Successful live delivery and successful durable retention are distinct
      states unless the destination contract explicitly makes them the same
      operation.

09.03. A persistent destination defines the point at which evidence is
      considered persisted under its durability contract.

09.04. Persistence acknowledgement, batching, buffering, flushing,
      transactions, recovery, and durability levels belong to persistent
      destination design or implementation specification.

09.05. A persistent destination does not silently weaken a persistence
      guarantee established by an owning design.

09.06. Ordinary tracing does not require every persistent destination to
      succeed before runtime work may continue. Stronger behavior exists only
      where a destination or owning design explicitly establishes it.


===============================================================================
10. STORAGE SELF-DIAGNOSTICS
===============================================================================

10.01. Persistent tracing infrastructure is itself diagnosable.

10.02. Meaningful storage, collection, pruning, persistence, continuity, and
       failure conditions should be represented through trace evidence or
       equivalent tracing-system diagnostic evidence when the tracing system is
       capable of recording them.

10.03. This principle does not require recursively tracing every internal
       storage operation or producing an infinite chain of diagnostics about
       diagnostics.

10.04. When ordinary tracing cannot record a tracing-system failure, an
       implementation may use an emergency or fallback diagnostic path.

10.05. The design does not require a specific fallback medium. A fallback may
       itself be unavailable; such a limit is a real boundary of obtainable
       evidence rather than something tracing fabricates around.

10.06. Concrete substrate-level trace types for retention/storage diagnostics
       remain OPEN. Legacy reason identifiers are not carried forward merely to
       preserve their names.


===============================================================================
11. STORAGE ACCESS BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

11.01. Persistent trace history is exposed through structured evidence access
       preserving TraceRecord and TraceTypeDefinition semantics.

11.02. Human-formatted terminal or log output is not the canonical persistent
       trace evidence interface.

11.03. A storage reader exposes known retention boundaries, incompleteness,
       collection/removal knowledge, and storage metadata needed for truthful
       interpretation.

11.04. A defined trace file or storage format may itself be a valid structured
       evidence interface. Tracing does not prohibit direct access merely
       because the representation is file-backed.

11.05. Access-control and permission requirements belong to the designs that
       own those concerns; they are not implied by the word "controlled" in
       legacy tracing documentation.

11.06. Concrete reader APIs, query languages, transport protocols, and storage
       schemas remain implementation or interoperability concerns unless
       standardized separately.


===============================================================================
12. TOOLING BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

12.01. Tooling may query, select, reconstruct, compare, and visualize
       persistent trace history.

12.02. Tooling interprets retention and completeness metadata defined by the
       storage contract.

12.03. Tooling does not redefine storage loss as producer-emitted evidence.

12.04. Tooling-derived continuity or causal findings remain derived unless they
       correspond to explicit retained evidence or storage metadata.

12.05. A tooling UI may configure retention policy without acquiring ownership
       of retention semantics.

12.06. Tooling preserves the distinction between known removal/unavailability
       and an unexplained unresolved relationship.


===============================================================================
13. OPEN RETENTION DESIGN
===============================================================================

13.01. OPEN:
       - concrete persistent trace destination architecture
       - retention-policy configuration model
       - durability levels
       - batching and acknowledgement semantics
       - storage continuity and removal-metadata model
       - persistent trace database and index strategy
       - pruning granularity
       - corruption detection and recovery behavior
       - cross-storage history migration
       - storage/export completeness metadata interchange
       - concrete tracing-system storage diagnostic trace types
       - preservation rules for future designated trace-root regions


===============================================================================
14. LOCKED MIGRATION DECISIONS
===============================================================================

14.01. LOCKED:
       - emission, creation, delivery, persistence, retention, and tooling
         interpretation are distinct
       - persistent trace history is non-authoritative evidence storage
       - retention is policy-driven unless a stronger guarantee is established
       - emission obligation is distinct from persistence guarantee
       - ordinary tracing does not require one globally perfect history across
         all destinations
       - known removal or unavailability is not represented as unexplained
         absence when the persistent history can account for it
       - unexplained absence is not assigned a fabricated cause
       - intentional collection may remove evidence while preserving compact
         knowledge sufficient to classify surviving references
       - removal metadata may itself be compacted; recursive tombstone history
         is not required
       - evidence loss weakens evidential completeness rather than committed-
         state correctness by itself
       - producer sequence semantics survive persistence unchanged
       - producer-local sequence may expose continuity gaps but does not explain
         their cause
       - independent producer sequences do not become globally ordered through
         storage
       - storage metadata remains distinguishable from producer-emitted evidence
       - pruning does not rewrite surviving evidence to hide structural or
         causal references
       - tracing storage is diagnosable without requiring infinite recursive
         tracing of its own diagnostics
       - structured evidence access, not a blanket prohibition on file-backed
         access, is the tracing requirement
       - tooling consumes retention semantics but does not own persistence
