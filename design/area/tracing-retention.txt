===============================================================================
DESIGN AREA DA-03 - TRACE RETENTION AND STORAGE
===============================================================================

Status:
-------
EVOLVING

Description:
------------
Defines implementation-independent retention, persistence, continuity, loss,
and storage-boundary semantics for Actant trace evidence.

Purpose:
--------
This document separates historical trace retention from runtime trace emission
and from tooling that interprets retained evidence.

It defines:
- persistent trace history semantics
- retention policy boundaries
- pruning, rotation, dropping, and known loss
- trace-history completeness claims
- continuity evidence and continuity uncertainty
- persistent destination responsibilities
- storage/tooling boundaries

It does NOT define:
- runtime TraceRecord creation or publication
- portable TraceRecord structure
- full trace graph materialization
- trace visualization or debugger behavior
- one concrete database, file format, codec, or index strategy


===============================================================================
01. CORE BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

01.01. Trace emission, destination delivery, historical retention, and tooling
      interpretation are distinct operations.

01.02. Runtime tracing creates and publishes trace evidence.

01.03. A persistent trace destination or equivalent storage component creates
      persistent trace history from evidence successfully delivered to it.

01.04. Trace tooling consumes persistent history and retention metadata. Trace
      tooling does not own the persistence operation merely because it reads,
      queries, or visualizes persisted evidence.

01.05. Persistent trace history is evidence storage. It is not authoritative
      committed state merely because it is durable.


===============================================================================
02. PERSISTENT TRACE HISTORY
===============================================================================

02.01. Persistent trace history consists of retained TraceRecords,
      TraceTypeDefinitions, and storage metadata required to interpret the
      history honestly.

02.02. Persistent storage preserves the semantic identities and fields of
      retained trace evidence.

02.03. Storage encoding, physical layout, database technology, indexing, and
      compression do not change trace evidence semantics.

02.04. A storage implementation does not mutate producer-emitted evidence and
      present the mutation as the original evidence.

02.05. Storage-specific metadata remains distinguishable from producer-emitted
      TraceRecords.


===============================================================================
03. RETENTION POLICY
===============================================================================

03.01. Trace retention is policy-driven unless another design establishes a
      stronger persistence requirement for a defined evidence class.

03.02. A retention policy may define bounded history, rotation, pruning,
      quotas, age limits, scope limits, or intentional discard.

03.03. A retention policy defines its preservation and removal behavior
      explicitly enough that consumers do not need to infer the policy from
      missing records.

03.04. Retention policy does not create committed-state authority for retained
      trace evidence.

03.05. An emission obligation is not a persistence guarantee.

03.06. A persistence guarantee exists only when an owning design explicitly
      requires evidence to remain available under defined conditions.


===============================================================================
04. DELIVERY FAILURE AND RETENTION LOSS
===============================================================================

04.01. Failure to deliver a TraceRecord to a persistent destination and later
      removal of a successfully persisted TraceRecord are different forms of
      evidence unavailability.

04.02. Runtime tracing owns truthful reporting of known live publication or
      destination-delivery failure within its failure-reporting capability.

04.03. Persistent storage owns truthful representation of known persistence,
      pruning, rotation, corruption, import, export, or storage-loss
      limitations within its retained metadata and evidence capabilities.

04.04. A storage component does not claim that evidence was persisted when the
      persistence operation is known to have failed.

04.05. A storage component does not claim historical completeness for a scope
      when it knows evidence in that scope was removed, lost, corrupted, or
      never successfully retained.

04.06. Failure or loss of non-authoritative trace history weakens evidential
      completeness. It does not by itself change committed-state correctness.


===============================================================================
05. KNOWN LOSS AND UNKNOWN ABSENCE
===============================================================================

05.01. Known trace-history loss is represented explicitly when the storage
      system retains enough information to know that loss occurred.

05.02. Known loss may include:
      - retention-policy pruning
      - rotation
      - quota or pressure dropping
      - failed persistence
      - detected corruption
      - partial import
      - partial export
      - unavailable storage segments

05.03. Storage and tooling do not convert unknown absence into a fabricated
      explanation for why evidence is unavailable.

05.04. A missing producer-local sequence value may demonstrate a continuity
      gap in the available history. The gap alone does not prove whether the
      record was undelivered, unretained, pruned, filtered, excluded from the
      selected scope, or lost for another reason.

05.05. Stronger loss metadata may explain a continuity gap when that metadata
      is available and applicable.


===============================================================================
06. COMPLETENESS
===============================================================================

06.01. Completeness is always evaluated for an identified evidence scope.

06.02. Persistent history does not imply global completeness merely because
      no known loss marker is present.

06.03. A storage implementation distinguishes, where its evidence permits:
      - complete for the claimed scope
      - known incomplete
      - continuity uncertain
      - outside retained scope
      - unavailable due to storage failure

06.04. A completeness claim does not extend beyond the scope and guarantees
      from which that claim was established.

06.05. Trace tooling preserves storage completeness and uncertainty semantics
      when presenting or reconstructing retained evidence.


===============================================================================
07. CONTINUITY
===============================================================================

07.01. Persistent history may use producer identity and producer-local sequence
      to evaluate producer-local continuity.

07.02. Producer-local continuity does not establish cross-producer ordering or
      causality.

07.03. Replacement producer identities remain separate continuity domains.

07.04. Explicit predecessor or runtime correlation evidence may allow tooling
      to relate producer histories without merging their producer-local
      sequences.

07.05. Continuity metadata may identify known storage boundaries, pruning
      boundaries, or other retained-history boundaries.

07.06. Continuity metadata does not fabricate records that are unavailable.


===============================================================================
08. RETENTION AND STRUCTURAL DEPENDENCIES
===============================================================================

08.01. Retention policy may remove evidence that other retained records
      reference.

08.02. Removal of referenced evidence does not authorize storage to rewrite
      surviving producer-emitted records to remove the reference.

08.03. Storage metadata may record the known removal boundary so tooling can
      distinguish a known-retention loss from an unexplained unresolved
      reference.

08.04. A retention or export policy that promises structurally complete span
      evidence defines how corresponding span starts and other required
      structural dependencies are preserved.

08.05. When such structural completeness cannot be preserved, the resulting
      history or artifact is represented as incomplete rather than repaired by
      fabricated trace evidence.


===============================================================================
09. PERSISTENT DESTINATION CONTRACT
===============================================================================

09.01. A persistent trace destination consumes the same portable trace
      definitions and records as other trace destinations.

09.02. Successful live delivery to a persistent destination and successful
      durable retention are distinct states unless the destination contract
      explicitly makes them the same operation.

09.03. A persistent destination defines the point at which evidence is
      considered persisted under its durability contract.

09.04. Persistence acknowledgement semantics, batching, buffering, flushing,
      transactions, recovery, and durability levels belong to the persistent
      destination design or implementation specification.

09.05. A persistent destination does not silently weaken a persistence
      guarantee established by an owning design.


===============================================================================
10. STORAGE ACCESS BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

10.01. Persistent trace history is exposed to consumers through structured
       evidence access that preserves TraceRecord and TraceTypeDefinition
       semantics.

10.02. Human-formatted log or terminal output is not the canonical persistent
       trace evidence interface.

10.03. A storage reader exposes known retention boundaries, incompleteness,
       and storage metadata needed for truthful interpretation.

10.04. Concrete reader APIs, query languages, transport protocols, and storage
       schemas remain implementation or interoperability concerns unless
       standardized separately.


===============================================================================
11. TOOLING BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

11.01. Trace tooling may query, select, reconstruct, compare, and visualize
       persistent trace history.

11.02. Tooling interprets retention and completeness metadata defined by the
       storage contract.

11.03. Tooling does not redefine storage loss as producer-emitted evidence.

11.04. Tooling-derived continuity findings remain derived interpretation unless
       they correspond to explicit retained storage metadata or TraceRecords.

11.05. Tooling does not acquire ownership of retention policy merely because
       it provides a user interface for configuring that policy.


===============================================================================
12. OPEN RETENTION DESIGN
===============================================================================

12.01. OPEN:
       - concrete persistent trace destination architecture
       - retention-policy configuration model
       - durability levels
       - batching and acknowledgement semantics
       - storage continuity metadata model
       - persistent trace database and index strategy
       - pruning granularity
       - corruption detection and recovery behavior
       - cross-storage history migration
       - storage/export completeness metadata interchange


===============================================================================
13. LOCKED MIGRATION DECISIONS
===============================================================================

13.01. LOCKED:
       - emission, delivery, retention, and tooling interpretation are distinct
       - persistent trace history is non-authoritative evidence storage
       - retention is policy-driven unless a stronger persistence guarantee is
         explicitly established
       - emission obligation is distinct from persistence guarantee
       - known retention loss is not represented as completeness
       - unknown absence is not assigned a fabricated cause
       - evidence loss weakens evidential completeness rather than committed-
         state correctness by itself
       - producer-local sequence may expose continuity gaps but does not by
         itself explain their cause
       - storage metadata remains distinguishable from producer-emitted
         evidence
       - pruning does not rewrite surviving producer evidence to hide broken
         references
       - tooling consumes retention semantics but does not own persistence
         merely because it interprets persisted evidence
