===============================================================================
DESIGN AREA DA-03 - TRACE RETENTION AND STORAGE
===============================================================================

Status:
-------
EVOLVING

Description:
------------
Defines implementation-independent persistence, retention, continuity, loss,
and storage-boundary semantics for Actant trace evidence.

Purpose:
--------
This document separates persistent trace history from runtime trace emission
and from tooling that interprets retained evidence.

It does NOT define runtime TraceRecord creation, graph materialization,
debugger behavior, or one concrete storage technology.


===============================================================================
01. CORE BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

01.01. Trace emission, record creation, destination delivery, durable
      persistence, historical retention, and tooling interpretation are
      distinct operations.

01.02. Runtime tracing creates and publishes trace evidence.

01.03. A persistent trace destination or equivalent storage component creates
      persistent trace history from evidence delivered to it according to its
      persistence contract.

01.04. Trace tooling consumes persistent history and retention metadata but
      does not own persistence merely because it queries or visualizes it.

01.05. Persistent trace history is evidence storage, not authoritative
      committed state merely because it is durable.


===============================================================================
02. PERSISTENT TRACE HISTORY
===============================================================================

02.01. Persistent trace history consists of retained TraceRecords,
      TraceTypeDefinitions, and storage metadata required to interpret retained
      evidence honestly.

02.02. Persistent storage preserves semantic identities and fields of retained
      trace evidence.

02.03. Storage encoding, physical layout, database technology, indexing, and
      compression do not change trace evidence semantics.

02.04. Storage does not mutate producer-emitted evidence and present the
      mutation as original evidence.

02.05. Storage-specific metadata remains distinguishable from TraceRecords.


===============================================================================
03. RETENTION POLICY
===============================================================================

03.01. Trace retention is policy-driven unless another design establishes a
      stronger persistence requirement for a defined evidence class.

03.02. A retention policy may define bounded history, rotation, pruning,
      quotas, age limits, scope limits, or intentional discard.

03.03. Retention policy defines preservation and removal behavior explicitly
      enough that consumers do not need to infer the policy from missing
      records.

03.04. Retention policy does not create committed-state authority.

03.05. An emission obligation is not a persistence guarantee.

03.06. A persistence guarantee exists only when an owning design explicitly
      requires evidence to remain available under defined conditions.


===============================================================================
04. DELIVERY, PERSISTENCE, AND LOSS
===============================================================================

04.01. Failure to deliver evidence to a persistent destination, failure to make
      delivered evidence durable, and later removal of durable evidence are
      distinct forms of evidence unavailability.

04.02. Runtime tracing owns truthful reporting of known live publication or
      destination-delivery failure within its reporting capability.

04.03. Persistent storage owns truthful representation of known persistence,
      pruning, rotation, corruption, import, export, and storage-loss
      limitations within its contract.

04.04. Storage does not claim persistence when persistence is known to have
      failed.

04.05. Storage does not claim historical completeness for a scope when it knows
      evidence in that scope was removed, lost, corrupted, or never
      successfully retained.

04.06. Loss of non-authoritative trace history weakens evidential completeness;
      it does not by itself change committed-state correctness.


===============================================================================
05. KNOWN LOSS AND UNKNOWN ABSENCE
===============================================================================

05.01. Known trace-history loss is represented explicitly when storage retains
      enough information to know that loss occurred.

05.02. Known loss may include retention pruning, rotation, quota dropping,
      failed persistence, detected corruption, partial import/export, or
      unavailable storage segments.

05.03. Storage and tooling do not convert unknown absence into a fabricated
      explanation.

05.04. A missing producer-local sequence value may demonstrate a continuity gap
      in the available history. The gap alone does not prove whether evidence
      was undelivered, unretained, pruned, filtered, outside selected scope, or
      lost for another reason.

05.05. Stronger loss metadata may explain a continuity gap when available and
      applicable.


===============================================================================
06. COMPLETENESS
===============================================================================

06.01. Completeness is evaluated for an identified evidence scope.

06.02. Persistent history does not imply global completeness merely because no
      known loss marker is present.

06.03. Storage distinguishes, where evidence permits, complete-for-scope,
      known-incomplete, continuity-uncertain, outside-retained-scope, and
      storage-unavailable conditions.

06.04. A completeness claim does not extend beyond the scope and guarantees
      from which it was established.

06.05. Trace tooling preserves storage completeness and uncertainty semantics
      when interpreting retained evidence.


===============================================================================
07. CONTINUITY AND ORDERING
===============================================================================

07.01. Persistent history may use trace producer identity and producer-local
      sequence to evaluate producer-local continuity.

07.02. Persistent storage preserves producer sequence semantics; it does not
      replace producer creation order with persistence or storage order.

07.03. Producer-local continuity does not establish cross-producer ordering or
      causality.

07.04. Replacement producer identities remain separate sequence and continuity
      domains.

07.05. Explicit relationships or future synchronized-producer evidence may
      relate histories without pretending that independent producer sequences
      form one sequence.

07.06. Continuity metadata may identify known storage or pruning boundaries.

07.07. Continuity metadata does not fabricate unavailable TraceRecords.


===============================================================================
08. RETENTION AND REFERENCED EVIDENCE
===============================================================================

08.01. Retention policy may remove evidence referenced by surviving records.

08.02. Removal does not authorize storage to rewrite surviving producer-emitted
      records to hide the reference.

08.03. Storage metadata may record known removal boundaries so tooling can
      distinguish known retention loss from an unexplained unresolved
      reference.

08.04. A retention or export policy promising structurally complete span
      evidence defines how required structural dependencies are preserved.

08.05. When promised completeness cannot be preserved, resulting history is
      represented as incomplete rather than repaired with fabricated evidence.

08.06. Explicit causal references receive the same preservation treatment:
      removal of their targets leaves unresolved causal evidence rather than
      causing surviving records to be rewritten.


===============================================================================
09. PERSISTENT DESTINATION CONTRACT
===============================================================================

09.01. A persistent destination consumes portable trace definitions and records
      according to the tracing destination contract.

09.02. Successful live delivery and successful durable retention are distinct
      states unless the destination contract explicitly makes them the same
      operation.

09.03. A persistent destination defines the point at which evidence is
      considered persisted under its durability contract.

09.04. Persistence acknowledgement, batching, buffering, flushing,
      transactions, recovery, and durability levels belong to persistent
      destination design or implementation specification.

09.05. A persistent destination does not silently weaken a persistence
      guarantee established by an owning design.


===============================================================================
10. STORAGE ACCESS BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

10.01. Persistent trace history is exposed through structured evidence access
       preserving TraceRecord and TraceTypeDefinition semantics.

10.02. Human-formatted terminal or log output is not the canonical persistent
       trace evidence interface.

10.03. A storage reader exposes known retention boundaries, incompleteness, and
       storage metadata needed for truthful interpretation.

10.04. Concrete reader APIs, query languages, transport protocols, and storage
       schemas remain implementation or interoperability concerns unless
       standardized separately.


===============================================================================
11. TOOLING BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

11.01. Tooling may query, select, reconstruct, compare, and visualize
       persistent trace history.

11.02. Tooling interprets retention and completeness metadata defined by the
       storage contract.

11.03. Tooling does not redefine storage loss as producer-emitted evidence.

11.04. Tooling-derived continuity or causal findings remain derived unless they
       correspond to explicit retained evidence or storage metadata.

11.05. A tooling UI may configure retention policy without acquiring ownership
       of retention semantics.


===============================================================================
12. OPEN RETENTION DESIGN
===============================================================================

12.01. OPEN:
       - concrete persistent trace destination architecture
       - retention-policy configuration model
       - durability levels
       - batching and acknowledgement semantics
       - storage continuity metadata model
       - persistent trace database and index strategy
       - pruning granularity
       - corruption detection and recovery behavior
       - cross-storage history migration
       - storage/export completeness metadata interchange
       - preservation rules for future designated trace-root regions


===============================================================================
13. LOCKED MIGRATION DECISIONS
===============================================================================

13.01. LOCKED:
       - emission, creation, delivery, persistence, retention, and tooling
         interpretation are distinct
       - persistent trace history is non-authoritative evidence storage
       - retention is policy-driven unless a stronger guarantee is established
       - emission obligation is distinct from persistence guarantee
       - known retention loss is not represented as completeness
       - unknown absence is not assigned a fabricated cause
       - evidence loss weakens evidential completeness rather than committed-
         state correctness by itself
       - producer sequence semantics survive persistence unchanged
       - producer-local sequence may expose continuity gaps but does not explain
         their cause
       - independent producer sequences do not become globally ordered through
         storage
       - storage metadata remains distinguishable from producer-emitted evidence
       - pruning does not rewrite surviving evidence to hide broken structural
         or causal references
       - tooling consumes retention semantics but does not own persistence
