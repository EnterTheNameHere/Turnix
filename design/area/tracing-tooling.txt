===============================================================================
DESIGN AREA DA-03 - TRACE TOOLING
===============================================================================

Status:
-------
EVOLVING

Description:
------------
Defines collection-level interpretation of trace evidence, graph
materialization, causal analysis, diagnostic navigation, and debugger
consumption boundaries.

Purpose:
--------
This document owns semantics that require a collection of trace evidence rather
than one runtime emission.

Persistent history, retention, pruning, durability, storage loss, and storage
completeness are defined in tracing-retention.txt. This document consumes those
semantics but does not own them.

This document does not define debugger pause, breakpoint, mutation, or runtime
control semantics.


===============================================================================
01. CORE CONCEPT
===============================================================================

01.01. Trace tooling consumes TraceRecords, TraceTypeDefinitions, and when
      applicable retention/storage metadata.

01.02. Tooling may consume evidence live, from persistent history, from an
      artifact, or from another structured evidence surface.

01.03. A tooling interpretation is derived from an available evidence set.

01.04. Materialized graphs and causal explanations do not replace source
      TraceRecords.

01.05. Tooling preserves the distinction between producer-emitted evidence,
      explicit producer-emitted causality, storage/retention metadata, and
      tooling-derived interpretation.


===============================================================================
02. AVAILABLE EVIDENCE SET
===============================================================================

02.01. Every tooling interpretation operates over an identified available
      evidence set.

02.02. An available evidence set may be complete for a selected scope or may
      be incomplete.

02.03. Tooling does not assume that its available evidence contains every
      record ever emitted by Actant.

02.04. Tooling preserves distinctions supplied by evidence and retention
      metadata between:
      - available evidence
      - evidence known to have been removed by retention
      - evidence known to be unavailable or failed
      - evidence outside the selected or retained scope
      - unexplained unresolved references
      - malformed or inconsistent evidence

02.05. Tooling does not collapse those conditions into a false claim that the
      underlying runtime action did not occur.

02.06. An unexplained unresolved reference is not silently normalized into
      ordinary retention. It represents a knowledge gap or possible evidence-
      integrity problem until stronger evidence accounts for it.


===============================================================================
03. FULL TRACE GRAPH
===============================================================================

03.01. Trace tooling may materialize a full trace graph containing the
      relationship categories supported by the available evidence.

03.02. The full trace graph is not necessarily a DAG.

03.03. Relationship categories remain semantically distinct. They may include:
      - span membership
      - span start-to-end lifecycle relationship
      - parent-span structural nesting
      - explicit causal references
      - producer-local ordering
      - runtime correlations
      - domain-specific relationships whose semantics are defined elsewhere

03.04. Ordering and correlation relationships do not become causal edges merely
      because they are represented in the same graph.

03.05. Tooling does not flatten distinct relationships into one generic parent
      relation when doing so would lose meaning.

03.06. A graph may contain non-record nodes or markers representing external
      identities, known removed targets, known unavailable targets, or
      unexplained unresolved targets when necessary to preserve truthful
      relationships. Such nodes are not fabricated TraceRecords.

03.07. A relationship whose target was knowingly collected remains navigable to
      an explicit known-removed state rather than terminating at an unexplained
      empty location.


===============================================================================
04. ORDER RECONSTRUCTION
===============================================================================

04.01. Producer-local sequence establishes total creation order only within one
      trace producer.

04.02. Tooling may select records belonging to one span and one producer and
      order those records by that producer's sequence.

04.03. Unrelated records created by the same producer may lie between selected
      span records in producer sequence without becoming span members.

04.04. Independent producer sequences do not establish a cross-producer total
      order.

04.05. Tooling does not manufacture cross-producer order by numerically
      comparing independent producer sequence values.

04.06. Tooling does not use wall-clock or monotonic timestamp proximity as a
      substitute for an ordering guarantee that the evidence does not provide.

04.07. A stronger synchronized-producer contract may eventually provide
      cross-producer ordering evidence. Tooling uses such ordering only when
      that stronger contract is explicitly represented and applicable.

04.08. Arrival order at live tooling or storage does not redefine producer
      creation order.


===============================================================================
05. CAUSAL INTERPRETATION
===============================================================================

05.01. Tooling distinguishes explicit causal assertions from derived causal
      interpretation.

05.02. Explicit causal references are producer-emitted causal evidence.

05.03. Tooling may derive a local causal explanation from a combination of:
      - structural span membership
      - same-producer creation order
      - semantic trace types
      - outcomes
      - error evidence
      - domain evidence

05.04. For example, an error that is the final same-producer record in a span
      immediately before that span records a failed outcome may be presented as
      an inferred local explanation when the available evidence supports that
      interpretation.

05.05. Such an inference does not retroactively create an explicit causal
      reference in the source TraceRecord.

05.06. Tooling may also derive causal explanations from patterns or knowledge
      discovered after the original evidence was emitted.

05.07. Later-derived causality remains explicitly distinguishable from what the
      emitter knew and asserted at emission time.

05.08. Tooling does not infer a causal relationship solely because two records
      are close in wall-clock or monotonic time.

05.09. When evidence is insufficient to distinguish causality from mere
      adjacency, tooling represents the explanation as uncertain or leaves it
      unresolved rather than fabricating certainty.


===============================================================================
06. CAUSAL DAG PROJECTION
===============================================================================

06.01. Tooling may derive a causal DAG projection from relationships whose
      semantics are causal for that projection.

06.02. Explicit causal references may participate directly in the causal DAG.

06.03. Tooling-derived causal edges may also participate in an analytical DAG
      when the view explicitly identifies them as derived rather than emitted.

06.04. A view may choose to show only explicit causal evidence, only a defined
      class of derived causality, or both, but does not erase their provenance.

06.05. Tooling validates acyclicity for a causal DAG projection.

06.06. A causal cycle is reported as inconsistent evidence, incompatible
      derived interpretation, or an invalid projection as applicable. Tooling
      does not silently remove edges merely to obtain a DAG.

06.07. Full Trace Graph and Trace Causal DAG are distinct concepts.


===============================================================================
07. GRAPH ROOTS AND TRACE ROOTS
===============================================================================

07.01. A graph root is defined relative to a materialized graph or projection
      and its available evidence set.

07.02. A causal DAG root has no resolved causal parent inside that projection.

07.03. Multiple unconnected causal roots are valid.

07.04. A node may appear as a causal root because causal predecessor evidence
      lies outside the available evidence set.

07.05. Graph-root status does not by itself prove global independence.

07.06. Structurally parentless span, designated trace root, bounded trace
      region, and kernel-run trace root are not treated as synonyms.

07.07. The exact designated trace-root and bounded-region model remains OPEN
      pending dedicated design analysis.

07.08. Tooling does not infer designated trace-root status merely because a
      span has no structural parent unless the future trace-root contract
      explicitly defines that rule.


===============================================================================
08. SPAN RECONSTRUCTION
===============================================================================

08.01. Tooling reconstructs span structure from span identity, span-start
      identity, parent-span identity, span family, and structural record kind.

08.02. A spanEnd whose corresponding start is available is linked to that
      start.

08.03. A child span whose parent is available is linked structurally to that
      parent span.

08.04. If referenced structural evidence is unavailable, tooling preserves the
      relationship and its known availability state rather than inventing
      missing records.

08.05. A dependency known to have been removed by retention is presented as
      known removed, not as an unexplained unresolved dependency.

08.06. A dependency whose absence cannot be accounted for remains explicitly
      unresolved and may be surfaced as a possible integrity or knowledge-gap
      condition.

08.07. Incremental live presentation state is not authoritative trace state.

08.08. Later evidence may refine an earlier incomplete graph without changing
      original TraceRecords.


===============================================================================
09. RETENTION CONSUMPTION BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

09.01. Persistent trace history and its retention semantics are owned by
      tracing-retention.txt.

09.02. Tooling consumes retention boundaries, continuity metadata, known
      collection/removal declarations, known-loss declarations, durability
      information when relevant, and completeness claims exposed by persistent
      history.

09.03. Tooling preserves those semantics when selecting, reconstructing, or
      presenting retained evidence.

09.04. Tooling may calculate continuity findings from retained producer-local
      sequence evidence, but a sequence gap alone does not explain why evidence
      is absent.

09.05. Providing a user interface for retention policy does not transfer
      ownership of retention semantics to tooling.


===============================================================================
10. STRUCTURED ACCESS
===============================================================================

10.01. Trace tooling consumes structured trace evidence rather than depending
       on scraping human-formatted terminal output.

10.02. Tooling access may be provided through live destinations or
       subscriptions, persistent readers, runtime APIs, debugger integration,
       defined trace files, or trace artifacts.

10.03. A concrete access protocol is not defined here.

10.04. Access surfaces preserve known incompleteness, known removal, and
       unresolved relationships.

10.05. Access-control and permission semantics belong to their owning designs;
       tracing does not derive such semantics merely from how evidence is
       physically stored.


===============================================================================
11. PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSTICS
===============================================================================

11.01. Tooling may present evidence as timelines, trees, graphs, tables,
       filtered streams, span views, diagnostic explanations, or other derived
       views.

11.02. Presentation may simplify information without changing underlying
       evidence meaning.

11.03. Presentation distinguishes:
       - source trace evidence
       - explicit emitted causal assertions
       - locally inferred explanations
       - later analytical or pattern-derived explanations
       - known removed or unavailable evidence
       - unresolved or uncertain interpretation

11.04. Presentation does not fabricate missing records or hide known evidence
       loss or collection.

11.05. Human-readable summaries do not replace the source evidence they
       summarize.


===============================================================================
12. LIVE AND OFFLINE TOOLING
===============================================================================

12.01. Live tooling may participate as a destination or consume a
       destination-backed stream.

12.02. Live tooling may incrementally update materialized structures as records
       arrive.

12.03. Arrival order does not redefine trace ordering semantics.

12.04. Live graphs may temporarily contain unresolved references that later
       become resolved or otherwise accounted for.

12.05. Offline tooling consumes retained evidence without requiring original
       producers to remain active.

12.06. Cross-run or cross-session stitching derives from explicit identities,
       correlations, references, applicable ordering contracts, and retention
       metadata rather than timestamp proximity alone.


===============================================================================
13. DEBUGGER INTEGRATION
===============================================================================

13.01. Debugger tooling may consume live and retained trace evidence and use
       the same graph and interpretation model as other trace tooling.

13.02. Debugger-specific annotations are derived tooling state unless an owning
       runtime domain emits them as TraceRecords.

13.03. Consuming trace evidence does not grant runtime mutation authority.

13.04. Breakpoints, pausing, safe points, inspection authority, mutation,
       disconnect behavior, and debugger-originated runtime control belong to
       the debugger design area.

13.05. Debugger control actions required to be traced are emitted through the
       ordinary tracing substrate.


===============================================================================
14. DERIVED ANNOTATIONS
===============================================================================

14.01. Tooling may calculate and persist derived annotations.

14.02. Derived annotations may include resolved graph edges, inferred causal
       edges, known-removal markers, unresolved-reference markers, continuity
       findings, search matches, graph validation findings, comparison results,
       and debugger navigation state.

14.03. Derived annotations remain distinguishable from producer-emitted
       TraceRecords even when stored beside them.

14.04. A derived annotation does not become producer evidence merely because
       later analysis considers it highly reliable.

14.05. A tooling annotation derived from authoritative retention metadata does
       not turn that metadata into producer-emitted evidence; provenance remains
       visible.


===============================================================================
15. DEVELOPMENT ENRICHMENT
===============================================================================

15.01. Development tooling may request or display richer trace evidence when
       owning domains support it.

15.02. Enrichment may include verbose domain evidence, why-not-chosen
       explanations, speculative-path evidence, resolution breakdowns, or
       debugger-oriented evidence.

15.03. Runtime-emitted enrichment remains producer evidence; tooling-created
       enrichment remains derived interpretation.

15.04. Development enrichment does not silently change runtime semantics or
       create committed-state authority.


===============================================================================
16. EXPORT AND SNAPSHOT BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

16.01. Trace exports and snapshots are evidence artifacts selected from source
       evidence and, when requested, derived annotations.

16.02. An export identifies its evidence selection boundary and known
       incompleteness.

16.03. An artifact claiming structurally complete span evidence includes the
       structural dependencies required by that completeness claim when they
       are available to the exporter.

16.04. An artifact that intentionally represents a partial evidence set may
       contain absent span dependencies when their known removal,
       unavailability, or unresolved state is explicit.

16.05. Export does not fabricate missing evidence to satisfy a completeness
       claim.

16.06. An export does not erase retention metadata needed to distinguish an
       intentionally collected target from an unexplained dangling reference
       when the exported relationship depends on that distinction.


===============================================================================
17. OPEN TOOLING DESIGN
===============================================================================

17.01. OPEN:
       - graph query API
       - live subscription protocol
       - visualization schemas
       - causal inference rule catalog and confidence representation
       - synchronized-producer ordering consumption
       - designated trace-root and bounded-region tooling semantics
       - cross-run stitching UX
       - diff-oriented trace views
       - concrete debugger trace bridge
       - export codecs
       - presentation and navigation of compact retention-removal metadata


===============================================================================
18. LOCKED MIGRATION DECISIONS
===============================================================================

18.01. LOCKED:
       - full graph materialization belongs to tooling
       - Full Trace Graph is not necessarily a DAG
       - Trace Causal DAG is a causal projection, not the full graph
       - relationship categories preserve their semantic differences
       - producer-local sequence orders only one producer's records
       - independent producers do not acquire fabricated total order
       - same-span same-producer evidence can be ordered by producer sequence
       - explicit causality and tooling-derived causality remain distinguishable
       - local structure and order may support inferred explanation
       - later pattern knowledge may derive explanation without rewriting what
         the original emitter knew
       - timestamp proximity alone does not create causal edges
       - graph-root status is relative to the available evidence set
       - structurally parentless span and designated trace root are not assumed
         to be the same concept
       - known removed/unavailable targets and unexplained unresolved targets
         remain distinguishable
       - a knowingly collected target remains navigable as known removed rather
         than becoming an unexplained empty link
       - retention semantics are consumed by tooling but owned separately
       - structured trace files may be valid tooling evidence surfaces
       - debugger runtime-control authority is outside trace tooling
       - derived annotations remain distinct from producer-emitted evidence
       - trace tooling does not create committed-state authority
