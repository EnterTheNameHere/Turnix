===============================================================================
DESIGN AREA DA-03 - TRACE RECORDS
===============================================================================

Status:
-------
EVOLVING

Description:
------------
Defines the implementation-independent portable evidence model emitted by the
Actant tracing substrate.

Purpose:
--------
This document defines trace-record structure and invariants independently of
implementation language, storage codec, transport, viewer, or debugger.

It defines:
-----------
- portable TraceRecord concepts and structural record kinds
- record identity and trace-producer-local ordering evidence
- timestamp, span, origin, outcome, correlation, and exception evidence
- portable TraceTypeDefinition semantics
- explicit causal-reference semantics
- record immutability and portability invariants
- the requirement that emitted evidence identity can be retained when needed

It does NOT define:
-------------------
- runtime emission, producer lifecycle, or destination publication mechanics
- persistence, retention, pruning, durability, or storage representation
- graph materialization, causal analysis, presentation, or debugger behavior
- concrete serialization codecs, field bindings, or language-specific APIs
- designated trace-root or bounded trace-region semantics that remain OPEN


===============================================================================
01. RECORD MODEL
===============================================================================

01.01. A TraceRecord is one immutable unit of trace evidence after creation.

01.02. TraceRecord is the fundamental evidence unit of the tracing substrate.

01.03. A TraceRecord does not contain or require a materialized trace graph.

01.04. TraceRecords carry explicit identity, structure, ordering evidence,
       correlations, outcomes, domain evidence, and optional explicit causal
       references from which consumers can reconstruct supported relationships.

01.05. TraceRecord semantics are independent of concrete in-memory object
       representation and serialization format.


===============================================================================
02. COMMON RECORD CONCEPTS
===============================================================================

02.01. Every TraceRecord contains concepts representing:
       - record identity
       - trace producer identity
       - producer-local sequence
       - wall-clock timestamp
       - monotonic timestamp
       - structural record kind
       - semantic domain and type
       - trace type definition identity
       - presentation level
       - human-readable message
       - structured attributes
       - explicit causal references, when present

02.02. According to record kind and available context, a TraceRecord may also
       contain:
       - captured exception evidence
       - span identity
       - span-start record identity
       - parent span identity
       - span family
       - structural origin
       - terminal outcome
       - duration
       - Actant runtime correlation identifiers

02.03. Concrete field names and surface syntax belong to interoperability or
       implementation specifications. The concepts and invariants defined here
       are platform design.


===============================================================================
03. RECORD IDENTITY AND PRODUCER ORDER
===============================================================================

03.01. Each TraceRecord has a stable identity unique within the trace evidence
       identity domain.

03.02. Each TraceRecord identifies the trace producer that created it.

03.03. Each TraceRecord has a producer-local sequence value.

03.04. For one trace producer, successfully created records have unique
       sequence values in record creation order.

03.05. Producer-local sequence is not publication order, destination arrival
       order, persistence order, or wall-clock order.

03.06. Sequence values from different trace producers are independent and are
       not directly comparable as one global sequence.

03.07. Consumers do not manufacture cross-producer ordering by comparing
       independent producer sequence values.

03.08. Producer identity plus sequence supports auditing producer-local record
       continuity when the relevant evidence is available.

03.09. Missing sequence values may indicate missing evidence, filtering,
       partial selection, retention boundaries, or another cause. The gap alone
       does not identify its cause.

03.10. When records share one structural span and one trace producer, tooling
       may select those span records and use producer-local sequence to recover
       their producer-relative creation order even when unrelated producer
       records occur between them.


===============================================================================
04. TIMESTAMPS
===============================================================================

04.01. A TraceRecord carries observed wall-clock timing evidence.

04.02. A TraceRecord carries monotonic timing evidence from its applicable
       monotonic clock domain.

04.03. Timestamps are observational evidence and do not themselves define
       causality.

04.04. Monotonic timestamps from different clock domains are not assumed to be
       directly comparable.

04.05. Cross-producer timestamp comparison establishes ordering only when a
       stronger contract explicitly establishes that the timestamps belong to a
       shared comparable ordering domain.

04.06. Span duration may be represented when it can be measured within a
       compatible monotonic clock domain.


===============================================================================
05. RECORD KINDS
===============================================================================

05.01. The tracing substrate defines three structural record kinds:
       - event
       - spanStart
       - spanEnd

05.02. An event represents an occurrence that does not itself establish or
       terminate a span lifecycle.

05.03. A spanStart establishes a span.

05.04. A spanEnd terminates a normally terminally-recorded span.

05.05. Domain semantics are represented by trace types rather than by
       proliferating structural record kinds.


===============================================================================
06. EVENT RECORDS
===============================================================================

06.01. An event may be structurally contained by a span or may exist without
       structural span containment.

06.02. A span-contained event identifies the span and its span-start record.

06.03. A span-contained event does not claim an independent structural origin.

06.04. An event without structural span containment records sufficient origin
       evidence to identify why it begins outside a parent span according to
       the applicable origin design.

06.05. Structural containment does not assert that the containing span caused
       the event.

06.06. An event may carry explicit causal references when the emitter knows
       causal information worth preserving explicitly.


===============================================================================
07. SPAN-START RECORDS
===============================================================================

07.01. A spanStart identifies its span and span family.

07.02. A spanStart identifies itself as the start record for that span.

07.03. A structurally nested spanStart identifies its parent span.

07.04. Parent span identity expresses structural nesting, not causality.

07.05. A structurally parentless spanStart carries structural-origin evidence
       according to the applicable origin design.

07.06. Structurally parentless span and designated trace root are not declared
       synonymous by this document. Trace-root semantics remain OPEN pending
       dedicated root-span design.


===============================================================================
08. SPAN-END RECORDS
===============================================================================

08.01. A spanEnd identifies its span, corresponding span-start record, span
       family, and terminal outcome.

08.02. A spanEnd may record duration.

08.03. A spanEnd does not establish a new structural parent or independent
       origin.

08.04. One span has at most one valid terminal record.

08.05. A spanEnd does not exist as valid complete span evidence without a
       corresponding span start in the evidence universe from which it
       originated.

08.06. A partial retained or exported evidence set may contain a spanEnd whose
       start is unavailable only when that incompleteness is represented
       honestly.

08.07. Tooling distinguishes malformed evidence from valid evidence whose
       dependencies are absent from the currently available evidence set.

08.08. When a missing structural dependency was knowingly removed by retention,
       the available evidence distinguishes that known removal from an
       unexplained unresolved dependency according to tracing-retention.txt.


===============================================================================
09. TRACE TYPE DEFINITIONS
===============================================================================

09.01. Each ordinary TraceRecord identifies the TraceTypeDefinition required
       to interpret its semantic type.

09.02. A TraceTypeDefinition is immutable semantic metadata.

09.03. A TraceTypeDefinition is either an event definition or a span-family
       definition.

09.04. An event definition identifies the domain and event semantic name.

09.05. A span-family definition identifies the domain, span-family semantic
       name, generated start type, and generated terminal outcome types.

09.06. TraceTypeDefinition identity is content-addressed from canonical
       semantic definition content.

09.07. Concrete canonical encoding and digest algorithm belong to an
       interoperability or implementation specification unless standardized
       across all Actant implementations.

09.08. A consumer interprets a record using its exact identified definition
       rather than guessing semantics from display text.


===============================================================================
10. DOMAIN EVIDENCE
===============================================================================

10.01. domain identifies the semantic subsystem or subject area of the trace
       type.

10.02. type identifies the semantic event or generated span type represented
       by the record.

10.03. level classifies presentation severity and does not by itself define
       runtime correctness or authority.

10.04. message provides human-readable evidence presentation.

10.05. Machine interpretation does not rely on message text when structured
       semantic fields provide the meaning.

10.06. attributes carry structured event-specific evidence.

10.07. The owning domain defines the meaning and required presence of its
       domain-specific attributes.

10.08. Domain evidence may include stable identifiers known by the emitter.

10.09. Attribute representation remains portable and immutable after record
       creation.


===============================================================================
11. EXPLICIT CAUSAL REFERENCES
===============================================================================

11.01. A TraceRecord may contain zero or more explicit causal references.

11.02. An explicit causal reference is a producer-emitted assertion that the
       referenced evidence or identified fact contributed causally to why the
       current record represents the event, decision, or outcome that it does.

11.03. Explicit causal references are not a generic relationship container.

11.04. Logical association, structural containment, correlation, and causality
       remain distinct concepts.

11.05. A causal reference identifies the semantic identity category and stable
       exact identity of its target.

11.06. Reference identifiers are not restricted to one universal identifier
       encoding.

11.07. Duplicate normalized causal references are not retained as separate
       assertions merely because they were supplied more than once.

11.08. Reference order is preserved when available but does not imply causal
       precedence unless an owning semantic definition explicitly gives that
       order meaning.

11.09. A causal reference may target evidence or a stable identity not present
       in the consumer's currently available evidence set.

11.10. Absence of a referenced target does not authorize a consumer to discard
       the reference or fabricate replacement evidence.

11.11. When the target was knowingly removed or became unavailable through a
       known retention/storage condition, that condition remains
       distinguishable from an unexplained unresolved reference according to
       tracing-retention.txt.

11.12. An unexplained unresolved reference means the available evidence cannot
       account for the referenced target. It is not silently interpreted as
       ordinary retention.

11.13. Ordinary local explanation need not be duplicated with an explicit
       causal reference when span structure, same-producer ordering, outcome,
       and domain evidence already express the relevant history adequately.

11.14. Explicit causal references are appropriate when the emitter knows a
       causal relationship that ordinary local structure and ordering do not
       adequately preserve, including causal links across independently ordered
       evidence.

11.15. The tracing record model does not currently define generic relatedTo
       references.


===============================================================================
12. STRUCTURE, ORDER, AND CAUSALITY
===============================================================================

12.01. Structural relationships answer where evidence belongs.

12.02. Producer sequence answers creation order within one trace producer.

12.03. Explicit causal references answer causal knowledge deliberately asserted
       by the emitter.

12.04. These relationship forms are not interchangeable.

12.05. A child span is not asserted to have been caused by its parent merely
       because it is structurally nested beneath that parent.

12.06. An error immediately preceding a failed span termination in the same
       span and same producer may provide strong local explanatory evidence,
       but the TraceRecord does not retroactively gain an explicit causal
       reference unless one was emitted.

12.07. Tooling may distinguish:
       - producer-emitted explicit causality
       - causality inferred from local structure and order
       - causality derived from later analysis or pattern knowledge

12.08. Derived causal interpretation does not mutate the original TraceRecord.


===============================================================================
13. ORIGIN
===============================================================================

13.01. origin is producer-emitted structural evidence explaining why a record
       or structurally parentless span begins without parent-span containment
       in its applicable tracing context.

13.02. origin does not prove that no external, domain, or causal predecessor
       exists.

13.03. Origin is not a substitute for a domain trace type or explicit causal
       evidence.

13.04. A record structurally contained by a span does not simultaneously claim
       an independent structural origin.

13.05. Concrete origin vocabulary and its extensibility remain subject to
       further design.


===============================================================================
14. OUTCOMES
===============================================================================

14.01. outcome identifies the terminal semantic category of a spanEnd.

14.02. Standard categories include completed, failed, errored, and cancelled.

14.03. completed means the operation reached its intended successful terminal
       state.

14.04. failed means the operation reached a recognized unsuccessful terminal
       state within its defined failure semantics.

14.05. errored means an exceptional or otherwise unhandled condition prevented
       the operation from resolving into an ordinary domain success or failure.

14.06. cancelled means the operation was intentionally prevented from reaching
       its ordinary terminal result by cancellation.

14.07. Intermediate error records do not force an errored or failed terminal
       outcome. A span may record handled errors and later complete.

14.08. A span family may define additional generated outcomes.

14.09. Outcome identity is machine-readable and does not depend on message
       wording.

14.10. Absence of a spanEnd from an available evidence set does not by itself
       prove which terminal outcome occurred.


===============================================================================
15. RUNTIME CORRELATION
===============================================================================

15.01. TraceRecords may carry identifiers correlating evidence with Actant
       runtime identity domains.

15.02. Correlation fields are optional when the corresponding identity is not
       known or not applicable at emission time.

15.03. Correlation is evidence linkage, not structural containment, causality,
       or authority.

15.04. Additional correlation identities may be added through explicit design
       evolution.


===============================================================================
16. EXCEPTION EVIDENCE
===============================================================================

16.01. A TraceRecord may carry immutable captured exception evidence when an
       exception or equivalent failure object contributes diagnostic evidence
       to the record.

16.02. Captured exception evidence is not a live implementation exception
       object and does not require that object to remain alive.

16.03. Language-specific exception capture mechanics belong to implementation
       specifications.

16.04. Cross-language representation preserves semantic diagnostic content
       without requiring identical host-language exception models.


===============================================================================
17. EMITTED EVIDENCE IDENTITY
===============================================================================

17.01. A tracing emission surface provides a means for the emitter to retain
       the stable identity of successfully created evidence when the emitter
       needs to reference that evidence later.

17.02. The design does not require every emitter to retain every emitted record
       or identity.

17.03. Access to emitted evidence identity enables explicit relationships
       without requiring the emitter to predict identities, search trace
       storage, or infer relationships from timestamps.

17.04. The concrete API representation returned to an emitter is an
       implementation concern.


===============================================================================
18. PORTABILITY AND IMMUTABILITY
===============================================================================

18.01. TraceRecords and TraceTypeDefinitions are designed to cross process,
       language, storage, and tooling boundaries without changing semantic
       meaning.

18.02. Once a TraceRecord is created, its evidence fields do not change.

18.03. Once a TraceTypeDefinition identity is established, semantic content
       associated with that identity does not change.

18.04. Derived annotations belong to tooling state or derived artifacts and
       remain distinguishable from producer-emitted evidence.


===============================================================================
19. OPEN DESIGN
===============================================================================

19.01. OPEN:
       - cross-producer structural participation in one span
       - synchronized-producer ordering representation
       - exact portable field surface and interoperability encoding
       - complete origin vocabulary
       - designated trace-root representation


===============================================================================
20. LOCKED MIGRATION DECISIONS
===============================================================================

20.01. LOCKED:
       - TraceRecord is the fundamental emitted evidence unit
       - records are immutable after creation
       - producer identity and producer-local creation order are explicit
       - independent producer sequences are not a global order
       - timestamps are observational and do not themselves define causality
       - event, spanStart, and spanEnd are structural record kinds
       - span structure and causality are semantically distinct
       - explicit causal references contain causal assertions, not generic
         logical relationships
       - generic relatedTo is not part of the current record model
       - ordinary local explanation need not redundantly assert causedBy
       - emitted evidence identity can be retained for later explicit reference
       - known removal of referenced evidence remains distinguishable from an
         unexplained unresolved reference
       - tooling-derived causality remains distinct from emitted causality
       - handled errors do not determine terminal span outcome
       - graph materialization is a tooling concern

20.02. The legacy generic parentEventIds and generic reason representations are
       not part of this record model.
